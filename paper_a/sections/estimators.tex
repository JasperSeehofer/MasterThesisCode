The failure modes catalogued in Section~\ref{sec:pitfall} share one structural defect: the density assigned to each candidate host's true redshift enters the numerator of the in-catalogue likelihood and its selection denominator \emph{differently}, so a factor that should cancel instead acquires a coherent, per-event tilt in $h$. This section presents the two estimator-level cures precisely. Both act on the in-catalogue ($G$) term alone, both preserve the selection rule that the detection probability $p_{\rm det}$ appears only in denominators \citep{Mandel:2018mve}, and both are evaluated downstream of the isotropic $1/(4\pi)$ completion sky-marginal of Section~\ref{sec:pitfall} (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:sky}). We refer to them by their library names, \texttt{local\_ratio} and \texttt{volume\_deconv}.

\subsection{A self-normalized local ratio of sums: \texttt{local\_ratio}}
\label{sec:est:localratio}

The in-catalogue likelihood of \citet{Gray:2019ksv} (their eqs~A9--A10) is a ratio of catalogue sums: per-galaxy gravitational-wave (GW) likelihood integrals over the host-redshift density in the numerator, the matching per-galaxy selection integrals in the denominator. The first estimator evaluates this ratio self-consistently over the event's localization region,
\begin{equation}
  L_{\rm cat}(h) \;=\;
  \frac{\sum_{g \in \mathcal{B}} w_g\, N_g(h)}
       {\sum_{g \in \mathcal{B}} w_g\, D_g(h)},
  \label{eq:est:lcat}
\end{equation}
with per-galaxy numerator and denominator
\begin{align}
  N_g(h) &= \int \mathrm{d}z\;
    p\big(x_{\rm GW} \mid d_L(z,h),\, \Omega_g\big)\; p_g(z),
  \label{eq:est:Ng}\\
  D_g(h) &= \int \mathrm{d}z\;
    p_{\rm det}\big(d_L(z,h),\, \Omega_g\big)\; p_g(z),
  \label{eq:est:Dg}
\end{align}
where $\mathcal{B}$ is the event's three-dimensional localization ball (sky \emph{and} distance), $x_{\rm GW}$ the event's GW data (Section~\ref{sec:framework}), $\Omega_g$ the galaxy's sky position, $p_g(z)$ the per-galaxy host-redshift density (Section~\ref{sec:est:voldeconv}), and $w_g = R_{\rm eff}(M_g)/(1+z_g)$ the per-galaxy rate weight: the per-black-hole EMRI rate $R_{\rm eff}$ of the population model of \citet{Babak:2017tow}, evaluated at the catalogue mass $M_g$ and divided by $(1+z_g)$, with $z_g$ the catalogue redshift, for source-to-detector time dilation --- instantiating the host-probability factors $p(s\mid z)\,p(s\mid M)$ of \citet{Gray:2019ksv}. The ratio $L_{\rm cat}(h)$ enters the per-event likelihood multiplied by the catalogue selection normalization $\beta_G(h)$ of Section~\ref{sec:framework}.

The structural point is that numerator and denominator of equation~\eqref{eq:est:lcat} run over the \emph{same} galaxy set with the \emph{same} weights and the \emph{same} kernel $p_g(z)$: every per-galaxy constant --- number-density calibration, rate normalization, completeness weighting --- cancels row by row, and no bridge between the discrete catalogue and a continuous density model is required. The global variant, by contrast, is the literal transcription of Gray et~al.'s eq.~A10 (denominator summed over the full catalogue) and implicitly requires the discrete catalogue sum to track the continuous $\beta_G(h)$ up to an $h$-independent constant; on GLADE+ \citep{Dalya:2021ewn} that assumption fails at the $-17$ per cent level end-to-end across the posterior grid (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:betag}), % src: .planning/gate/G1_beta_g_check.md (end_to_end_tilt_h3_corrected = -0.172)
a tilt that multiplies the in-catalogue term once per event and accumulates coherently over the sample. Restricting both sums to the localization region is a deliberate deviation from eq.~A10, whose nearest published analogue is the catalogue-patch case of \citet{Gray:2019ksv} (their eq.~A20); the exact mapping, and what the deviation does and does not change, is documented in Appendix~\ref{sec:app:gray}.

\subsection{Comoving-volume deconvolution of the host-redshift kernel: \texttt{volume\_deconv}}
\label{sec:est:voldeconv}

The second cure addresses the kernel $p_g(z)$ itself. A catalogue entry $z_g \pm \sigma_z$ is a measurement \emph{likelihood} for the true redshift, $p(z_g \mid z) = \mathcal{N}(z_g;\, z, \sigma_z^2)$, where $\mathcal{N}(x; \mu, \sigma^2)$ denotes a normal density; treating it directly as a density for $z$ --- the bare-Gaussian kernel --- silently assumes a flat prior on the true redshift. Hosts, however, are drawn from the event population: uniform in comoving volume and source-frame time, so that the number of events per unit detector time and redshift carries the weight
\begin{equation}
  w_{\rm pop}(z) \;\equiv\; \frac{1}{1+z}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}V_c}{\mathrm{d}z},
  \label{eq:est:wpop}
\end{equation}
the comoving volume element \citep{Hogg:1999ad} times cosmological time dilation \citep{Babak:2017tow,Mandel:2018mve}. The Bayes-correct per-galaxy posterior is therefore the deconvolved kernel
\begin{equation}
  p_g(z) \;=\; \frac{\mathcal{N}(z_g;\, z, \sigma_z^2)\; w_{\rm pop}(z)}{Z_g},
  \qquad
  Z_g = \int \mathrm{d}z'\; \mathcal{N}(z_g;\, z', \sigma_z^2)\; w_{\rm pop}(z'),
  \label{eq:est:pgz}
\end{equation}
used identically in the numerator~\eqref{eq:est:Ng} and denominator~\eqref{eq:est:Dg}. Equation~\eqref{eq:est:wpop} is the same population measure that the completion term and every selection integral of Section~\ref{sec:framework} already carry: with equation~\eqref{eq:est:pgz}, every redshift integral in the estimator carries $w_{\rm pop}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z$ exactly once --- never zero times (the bare kernel) and never twice. The per-galaxy normalization $Z_g$ makes each galaxy contribute one unit of prior mass, so galaxies are weighted by $w_g$ and by fit to the data, not by how much comoving volume happens to lie under their photometric error bar. This likelihood-times-prior construction has no counterpart in \citet{Gray:2019ksv}, whose mock catalogues had zero redshift error; it parallels the line-of-sight redshift priors of later \textsc{gwcosmo} releases \citep{MISSING:Gray2023-los-zprior}. % MISSING CITATION: Gray et al. 2023, "Joint cosmological and gravitational-wave population inference using dark sirens and galaxy catalogues", arXiv:2308.02281
Two properties matter in practice. First, writing $w_{\rm pop}(z; h) = h^{-3} g(z)$ shows the $h^{-3}$ prefactor cancels exactly against $Z_g$: the deconvolution injects \emph{no} $h$-dependence into $L_{\rm cat}$, only its redshift shape acts. Secondly, as $\sigma_z \to 0$ equation~\eqref{eq:est:pgz} reduces to $\delta(z - z_g)$, recovering the bare kernel continuously in the spectroscopic limit. % src: docs/derivations/G2b_host_z_volume_prior.md (Eq. 1.5 and limiting case)

\subsubsection{The Eddington-in-$z$ law: what the bare kernel costs}
\label{sec:est:eddz}

Omitting $w_{\rm pop}$ is not a neutral convention. Expanding $\ln w_{\rm pop}$ to first order about $z_g$ inside equation~\eqref{eq:est:pgz} and completing the square shows that the deconvolved posterior is, to leading order, the bare Gaussian recentred at
\begin{equation}
  \langle z \rangle \;=\; z_g + \sigma_z^2\, s(z_g) + \mathcal{O}(\sigma_z^4),
  \qquad
  s(z) \equiv \frac{\mathrm{d} \ln w_{\rm pop}}{\mathrm{d}z}.
  \label{eq:est:eddz}
\end{equation}
The shift arises because $w_{\rm pop}$ rises steeply with $z$: a galaxy observed at $z_g$ is more probably a higher-redshift galaxy scattered down than the reverse. This is the classical correction of \citet{MISSING:Eddington1913}, transcribed to redshift; % MISSING CITATION: Eddington, A. S. 1913, MNRAS 73, 359, "On a formula for correcting statistics for the effects of a known error of observation"
the bare kernel omits the shift and under-states every host redshift. Since the GW data fix $d_L$, the posterior peaks where the model distance at the assumed host redshift matches the measured one, and the omission propagates to a Hubble bias
\begin{equation}
  \Delta h \;\simeq\; -\,h\,
  \left.\frac{\mathrm{d}\ln \mathcal{D}}{\mathrm{d}z}\right|_{\bar z}
  \sigma_z^2\, s(\bar z)
  \;\equiv\; -\,C(\bar z)\,\sigma_z^2,
  \label{eq:est:eddh}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{D}(z) \equiv (1+z)\int_0^z \mathrm{d}z'/E(z')$ is the dimensionless distance function, $d_L = (c/100~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}})\,\mathcal{D}(z)/h$, $E(z) = H(z)/H_0$, and $\bar z$ is a representative detected-host redshift: $H_0$ is biased \emph{low}, quadratically in $\sigma_z$. The law is verified quantitatively on synthetic single-host tests: measured biases $\Delta h = -0.0016$, $-0.0064$, $-0.023$, $-0.046$ at $\sigma_z = 0.005$, $0.015$, $0.035$, $0.050$ follow the $\sigma_z^2$ scaling to ${\sim}10$ per cent after subtracting a $\sigma_z$-independent estimator floor of $\approx -0.002$, with an implied coefficient $C_{\rm meas} \approx 17$--$20$, constant to $\pm 8$ per cent across a factor 11 in $\sigma_z^2$ and matching the prediction of equation~\eqref{eq:est:eddh} at an effective $\bar z \approx 0.25$--$0.27$, inside the detected population (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:voldeconv}). % src: docs/derivations/G2b_host_z_volume_prior.md sec. 2.3

The implementation deconvolves exactly --- quadrature of the full product in equation~\eqref{eq:est:pgz} --- rather than applying the leading-order recentring of equation~\eqref{eq:est:eddz}, because for GLADE+-like hosts the shift is non-perturbative: at $z_g = 0.05$ the expansion parameter $\sigma_z\, s(z_g)$ exceeds unity for $\sigma_z \gtrsim 0.035$, and at $\sigma_z = 0.05$ the exact posterior-mean shift is $+0.054$ against the quadratic law's $+0.095$ --- saturated below the leading order, yet still ${\gtrsim}60$ per cent of $z_g$ itself. % src: docs/derivations/G2b_host_z_volume_prior.md sec. 2.3 (z_g = 0.05 table)

\subsection{The rate-weighted host-mass prior: Eddington-in-$M$}
\label{sec:est:eddm}

The two-dimensional channel of Section~\ref{sec:framework}, which adds the redshifted host black-hole mass $M(1+z)$ to the event observables, admits the same inconsistency in $M$. The bare mass kernel $\mathcal{N}(M;\, M_g, \sigma_M^2)$ treats all true masses within the measurement scatter $\sigma_M$ as equally likely to host an EMRI, whereas the per-black-hole rate falls with mass, $\mathrm{d}\log_{10} R_{\rm eff}/\mathrm{d}\log_{10} M \approx -0.43$ per dex over the catalogue range. % src: docs/derivations/G2d_host_mass_rate_prior.md sec. 1
The prior-consistent kernel is the mass analogue of equation~\eqref{eq:est:pgz},
\begin{equation}
  p_g(M) \;=\; \frac{\mathcal{N}(M;\, M_g, \sigma_M^2)\; R_{\rm eff}(M)}{Z_M},
  \qquad
  Z_M = \int \mathrm{d}M\; \mathcal{N}(M;\, M_g, \sigma_M^2)\; R_{\rm eff}(M),
  \label{eq:est:pgM}
\end{equation}
applied to the numerator and, as the importance-sampling proposal, to the Monte Carlo selection denominator, so the mass prior too is counted exactly once. A first-order expansion of $\ln R_{\rm eff}$ makes equation~\eqref{eq:est:pgM} an exactly shifted Gaussian, $M_g^{\rm eff} = M_g(1 + \alpha_g \sigma_{\rm rel}^2)$ with $\alpha_g = \mathrm{d}\ln R_{\rm eff}/\mathrm{d}\ln M|_{M_g}$ and $\sigma_{\rm rel} = \sigma_M/M_g$ --- again Eddington's correction. But GLADE+ mass estimates sit at $\sigma_{\rm rel} \approx 1$ (median $0.98$ over the analysed events), % src: .planning/gate/G7row9_eddington_m_impact.json (median_sigma_rel = 0.9847)
and near the low-mass roll-off of $R_{\rm eff}$, where the rate \emph{rises} with $M$, the local slope predicts the wrong sign of the shift: at $M_g = 10^5\,\mathrm{M}_\odot$ and $\sigma_{\rm rel} = 0.55$ the slope points down while the exact posterior mean moves up by ${\sim}3.7$ per cent. % src: docs/derivations/G2d_host_mass_rate_prior.md sec. 4
The implementation therefore uses the exact first moment of equation~\eqref{eq:est:pgM}, computed by per-galaxy quadrature, in a moment-matched Gaussian that retains the catalogue width $\sigma_M$ (conservative; the residual is the neglected skew, second order). Verification against independent quadrature and both slope-sign regimes is given in Appendix~\ref{sec:app:eddm}. As $\sigma_M \to 0$ the bare kernel is recovered, so forecast regimes with spectroscopic-quality masses are automatically unaffected. On the real-data configuration of Section~\ref{sec:realdata} the fix leaves the one-dimensional posterior essentially unchanged (mean shift $-5 \times 10^{-5}$) while moving the two-dimensional posterior mean from $0.790$ to $0.770$ --- toward the injected truth $h = 0.73$ --- and suppressing its grid-edge mass from $0.216$ to $0.023$. % src: .planning/gate/G7row9_eddington_m_impact.json

\subsection{The adopted default}
\label{sec:est:default}

The library default, used for every headline result in this paper, is \texttt{volume\_deconv}: the deconvolved kernels of equations~\eqref{eq:est:pgz} and \eqref{eq:est:pgM} inside the local ratio of sums of equation~\eqref{eq:est:lcat}. Three reasons. First, it is the only configuration in which the same population prior multiplies the same measurement likelihood on both sides of every ratio --- prior consistency by construction, not by empirical repair --- and the unique choice consistent with the event-rate model that generates the simulated hosts. Secondly, \texttt{local\_ratio} alone cures the normalization artifact but retains the bare redshift kernel and with it the $-C\sigma_z^2$ law of equation~\eqref{eq:est:eddh}, which at $\sigma_z = 0.035$ already costs $-0.023$ in $h$ % src: docs/derivations/G2b_host_z_volume_prior.md sec. 2.3
and drives the empirical probability--probability coverage of the bare kernel to approximately zero (Section~\ref{sec:coverage}); % src: .planning/gate/G7_systematics_budget.md row 2
because the bias is quadratic in $\sigma_z$, a calibration demonstrated at one photometric quality would not transfer across the $(\sigma_z, \sigma_M)$ regimes a forecast must span --- the same reason the mass channel was fixed rather than caveated. Thirdly, the deconvolution is exactly $h$-neutral (the $h^{-3}$ cancellation above), so the de-railing it produces on GLADE+ data (Section~\ref{sec:realdata}) cannot be manufactured by the fix itself. \texttt{local\_ratio} is retained as a cross-check closer to the published form: the agreement of the two estimators' interior posterior peaks on the same data (Section~\ref{sec:realdata}) shows the recovery is a property of prior consistency, not of one kernel.
