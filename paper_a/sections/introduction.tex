The Hubble constant is at present the most contested number in
cosmology: early-Universe inference from the cosmic microwave
background \citep{Planck:2018vyg} and late-Universe distance-ladder
measurements \citep{Riess:2021jrx} disagree at the level of several
standard deviations, and the discrepancy has resisted a decade of
systematic scrutiny \citep{MISSING:DiValentino2021-hubble-tension-review}.
% MISSING CITATION: Di Valentino et al. 2021, "In the realm of the Hubble tension — a review of solutions", arXiv:2103.01183
Gravitational-wave standard sirens offer an arbitration channel with
independent systematics: the waveform of a compact-binary inspiral
encodes the source's luminosity distance directly, with no distance
ladder \citep{Schutz:1986gp}.  When an electromagnetic counterpart
identifies the host galaxy, a single event already yields an
informative measurement \citep{Abbott:2017xzu}, and samples of a few
tens of counterpart events have been forecast to arbitrate the
tension \citep{Chen:2017rfc}.

Most gravitational-wave detections, however, have no counterpart.
The \emph{dark-siren} programme replaces the missing host
identification by a marginalization over a galaxy catalogue: every
galaxy consistent with the gravitational-wave localization volume
proposes a redshift, the correct proposals accumulate coherently
across events, and the incoherent ones average away
\citep{Schutz:1986gp,MISSING:DelPozzo2012-statistical-dark-siren}.
% MISSING CITATION: Del Pozzo 2012, "Inference of the cosmological parameters from gravitational waves: application to second generation interferometers", arXiv:1108.1317
The modern formulation of the method --- explicit in-catalogue and
out-of-catalogue hypotheses, a completion term for the galaxies the
survey misses, and selection normalizations for both channels --- is
due to \citet{Gray:2019ksv}, was developed through the
\textsc{gwcosmo} pipeline
\citep{MISSING:Gray2022-pixelated,MISSING:Gray2023-los-zprior},
% MISSING CITATION: Gray et al. 2022, "Pixelated approach to galaxy catalogue incompleteness: improving the dark siren measurement of the Hubble constant", arXiv:2111.04629
% MISSING CITATION: Gray et al. 2023, "Joint cosmological and gravitational-wave population inference using dark sirens and galaxy catalogues", arXiv:2308.02281
and underpins the flagship application to the GWTC-3 event catalogue
\citep{MISSING:LVK2021-gwtc3-cosmology};
% MISSING CITATION: Abbott et al. (LVK) 2021, "Constraints on the cosmic expansion history from GWTC-3", arXiv:2111.03604
\citet{Gair:2022zsa} give a pedagogical synthesis.  Independent
implementations of the same physics now exist in \textsc{chimera}
\citep{MISSING:Borghi2024-chimera}, \textsc{icarogw}
\citep{MISSING:Mastrogiovanni2023-icarogw}, and
\textsc{DarkSirensStat} \citep{MISSING:Finke2021-darksirensstat}.
% MISSING CITATION: Borghi et al. 2024, "Cosmology and astrophysics with standard sirens and galaxy catalogs in view of future gravitational wave observations" (CHIMERA), arXiv:2312.05302
% MISSING CITATION: Mastrogiovanni et al. 2023, "ICAROGW: a python package for inference of astrophysical population properties of noisy, heterogeneous and incomplete observations", arXiv:2305.17973
% MISSING CITATION: Finke et al. 2021, "Cosmology with LIGO/Virgo dark sirens: Hubble parameter and modified gravitational wave propagation", arXiv:2101.12660
The same machinery is expected to carry the space-borne programme:
LISA extreme-mass-ratio inspirals (EMRIs) will supply precisely
measured luminosity distances at cosmological depth
\citep{Babak:2017tow,LISA:2024hlh}, and dark-siren Hubble-constant
forecasts built on them already exist \citep{Laghi:2021pqk}.

The galaxy catalogues these analyses actually have at their disposal
are a different matter from the ones the method was validated on.
GLADE+ \citep{Dalya:2021ewn}, the workhorse catalogue of the
LIGO--Virgo--KAGRA dark-siren analyses, draws a substantial fraction
of its redshifts from photometric surveys, and at the depths where
gravitational-wave hosts live the relative redshift uncertainty of
those entries approaches order unity --- $\sigma_z/z \sim 0.7$ in the
regime probed by the analysis of this paper.
% src: .planning/gate/G5a_gwcosmo_inspection.md (sigma_z/z ~ 0.7, EMRI/GLADE+ regime)
The known-truth validations of the galaxy-catalogue method, by
contrast, live almost entirely in the spectroscopic limit: the mock
data challenge that established the \citet{Gray:2019ksv} framework
used galaxies with no redshift uncertainty at all, and subsequent
photometric-redshift studies on real data
\citep{MISSING:Turski2023-photoz} could quantify
information loss but not bias, because real data come without a
truth.
% MISSING CITATION: Turski et al. 2023, "Impact of modelling galaxy redshift uncertainties on the gravitational-wave dark standard siren measurement of the Hubble constant", arXiv:2302.12037
Section~\ref{sec:codes} documents this validation gap in detail.  It
is exactly the gap that the coming era of Rubin--LSST and
Euclid-scale photometric catalogues, and of LISA-era dark sirens,
will occupy.

This paper reports what happened when a complete, independently
implemented estimator of the \citet{Gray:2019ksv} family met that
regime.  In a simulated EMRI campaign evaluated against the real
GLADE+ galaxy field, the Hubble-constant posterior \emph{railed}:
essentially all of its mass piled onto the boundary of the prior
support --- a posterior that looks maximally decisive while being
maximally wrong.  Two natural readings suggest themselves.  The
first is information starvation: photometric redshift errors of
order unity have simply erased the catalogue's redshift information,
and no estimator could do better.  The second is a parochial
implementation bug, of interest only to the authors.  This paper
shows that both readings fail.  The rail is a \emph{prior-consistency
artifact} of the completion-term normalization --- a superposition of
normalization defects, each an individually plausible reading of the
published equations, whose effects are second order in the redshift
kernel width and therefore invisible in the spectroscopic regime
where the method's validations live, but leading order at photometric
widths.  On identical data, identical events, and an identical
catalogue, the pre-fix estimator rails at the upper edge of the
$h \in [0.60, 0.86]$ grid, at $h = 0.86$;
% src: results/commission_20260701/redteam/crux_results.json (prod); derail_matrix_results.json (h_values)
restoring only the isotropic $1/(4\pi)$ completion sky marginal
flips the rail to the opposite edge, $h = 0.60$ --- the fingerprint
of two opposing mis-normalizations, one masking the other;
% src: results/commission_20260701/redteam/derail_matrix_results.json (prod_global)
and restoring prior consistency between numerator and selection
normalization, by either of two principled routes, de-rails the
posterior onto an interior peak at the injected simulation truth,
$h = 0.73$.
% src: results/commission_20260701/redteam/derail_matrix_results.json (local_ratio, volume_deconv); master_thesis_code/constants.py (H = 0.73)
An estimator that rails silently at the edge of its prior under
realistic photometric errors --- while calibrating perfectly on the
spectroscopic-quality inputs used to validate it --- is a field-level
hazard for every independently implemented dark-siren pipeline, not a
code anecdote.

The contributions of the paper are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Diagnosis} (Section~\ref{sec:pitfall}).  We decompose
    the railed posterior into three distinct normalization defects:
    an Eddington--Malmquist-type prior inconsistency in the
    photometric host-redshift kernel, which biases $h$ low
    quadratically in $\sigma_z$; a completion-term sky factor
    evaluated at the peak density of the localization Gaussian
    instead of its isotropic $1/(4\pi)$ marginal, which over-weights
    the out-of-catalogue channel by orders of magnitude
    (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:sky}); and an $h$-dependent tilt in the
    global selection-sum normalization, which multiplies every event
    coherently (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:betag}).  All three violate a
    single requirement: every population measure must be counted
    exactly once, identically in the numerator and in the selection
    normalization.
  \item \textbf{Two principled estimators}
    (Section~\ref{sec:estimators}).  We construct two prior-consistent
    repairs: a local ratio-of-sums normalization adjacent to the
    published form of \citet{Gray:2019ksv}
    (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:gray}), and a comoving-volume
    deconvolution of the photometric host-redshift kernel
    (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:voldeconv}), extended to the host-mass
    channel of our two-parameter analysis
    (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:eddm}).
  \item \textbf{Synthetic calibration} (Section~\ref{sec:coverage}).
    An independent probability--probability and coverage harness,
    which re-derives the estimator from the written formulas without
    importing the production code, shows that the bare-Gaussian
    kernel collapses to near-zero frequentist coverage on synthetic
    universes while the volume-deconvolved kernel is calibrated close
    to nominal.
  \item \textbf{Real-data de-rail} (Section~\ref{sec:realdata}).  On
    identical real GLADE+ data, swapping only the estimator carries
    the posterior through the arc above --- rail, opposite-edge rail,
    interior peak at the injected truth --- and a two-factor ablation
    separates the roles of the kernel and the denominator.
    Section~\ref{sec:postmortem} explains why the earlier
    ``information starvation'' reading of the same data was a
    misdiagnosis.
  \item \textbf{External-code relevance} (Section~\ref{sec:codes}).
    We audit the published implementations at pinned versions.  The
    specific normalization inconsistency behind the rail is
    structurally excluded from \textsc{gwcosmo} by construction, and
    the volume-weighted redshift kernel we arrive at independently
    coincides with the published practice of \textsc{chimera},
    \textsc{icarogw}, and \textsc{DarkSirensStat}.  The bare-kernel
    assumption, however, is present by declaration in
    \textsc{gwcosmo}, and no known-truth validation of any published
    pipeline exists at $\sigma_z/z \sim 0.7$.
    % src: .planning/gate/G5a_gwcosmo_inspection.md; .planning/gate/G5b_chimera_icarogw_inspection.md
\end{enumerate}

Two scope statements apply throughout.  First, the events are
simulated: the gravitational-wave campaign is a mock EMRI population
injected at $h = 0.73$
% src: master_thesis_code/constants.py (H = 0.73, fiducial simulation value)
and analysed against the real GLADE+ galaxy field, so every claim in
this paper concerns estimator calibration against a known truth,
never a measurement of the actual expansion rate.  Second, the event
sample carries a known selection-scale caveat, stated where the data
are introduced (Section~\ref{sec:realdata}): it affects the depth and
loudness of the mock population but not the estimator-comparison
logic, and forecast-grade EMRI constraints are deferred to a
companion paper.

The paper is organized as follows.  Section~\ref{sec:framework}
assembles the complete single-event likelihood, fixes notation, and
defines prior consistency.  Section~\ref{sec:pitfall} dissects the
three normalization defects and derives their observable signatures.
Section~\ref{sec:estimators} constructs the two prior-consistent
estimators and motivates the adopted default.
Section~\ref{sec:coverage} presents the synthetic calibration
evidence, and Section~\ref{sec:realdata} the de-rail demonstration
and ablation on GLADE+.  Section~\ref{sec:postmortem} revisits the
information-starvation misdiagnosis, Section~\ref{sec:codes} relates
our findings to the published pipelines, and Section~\ref{sec:budget}
quantifies the remaining systematic-error budget.
Section~\ref{sec:conclusions} concludes.  The appendices contain the
exact completion-term sky marginal (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:sky}), the
volume deconvolution and the Eddington-in-$z$ law
(Appendix~\ref{sec:app:voldeconv}), the term-by-term mapping onto the
equations of \citet{Gray:2019ksv} (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:gray}), the
rate-weighted host-mass prior (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:eddm}), and the
discrete-sum check of the catalogue selection normalization
(Appendix~\ref{sec:app:betag}).
