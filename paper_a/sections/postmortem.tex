The de-railing of Section~\ref{sec:realdata} refutes more than the pre-fix estimator: it refutes an earlier diagnosis of the failure --- reached at an intermediate stage of this programme, before the normalization audit of Section~\ref{sec:pitfall} --- that was plausible, supported by every test then available, and wrong. That diagnosis held that catalogue dark-siren inference in the GLADE+ photometric regime is \emph{information-starved}: that redshift uncertainties comparable to the redshifts themselves erase the in-catalogue $H_0$ information, so that no normalization repair can recover a peaked posterior. Since photometric scatter is widely treated as a leading systematic of the catalogue method \citep{Gray:2019ksv,Turski:2023lxq}, the hypothesis deserves a post-mortem: why it looked right, why it is wrong, and which observables discriminate the two failure classes.

Three observations supported it. First, the bare-Gaussian estimator railed: on the full 3355-event sample, on a $0.01$-spaced grid spanning $[0.60, 0.87]$, the posterior maximum sat on the upper grid edge at $h = 0.87$. % src: scripts/bridge_closure/outputs/rungG2_results.json ("bare photo-z x1.0": h_refined 0.87, railed, n_events 3355)
Second, the posterior ignored the sample size: its 68~per~cent width stayed pinned at a single grid step ($0.010$) as the event count doubled from 838 to 1677 to 3355 --- a posterior indifferent to a fourfold change in $N$ looks, by the usual statistical reflex, like one with no information to accumulate. % src: scripts/bridge_closure/outputs/rungG2_results.json (n_scaling: width68 = 0.010 at N = 838/1677/3355)
Third, every repair then attempted failed. Weighting the numerator host-$z$ kernel by the population measure $w_{\mathrm{pop}}$ \emph{alone}, with the selection normalization left as the point-evaluated global sum of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pitfall:sigmaglobal}, still railed --- at $h = 0.87$ for photometric scatter at $\times 0.5$ and $\times 1.0$ of its catalogue value and, tellingly, at the \emph{opposite} edge $h = 0.60$ at $\times 0.25$; % src: scripts/bridge_closure/outputs/rungG2_results.json (volume-prior x0.25/x0.5/x1.0)
the complementary denominator-only repair, smearing the global selection sum over the same bare kernel used in the numerator, gave flat or multimodal posteriors whose width did not shrink with $N$. % src: .planning/gate/G6_starvation_postmortem.md (evidence item 3, smeared-denominator candidate falsified)
Conditional on this family exhausting the candidates, ``the photometric data cannot constrain $h$'' was the correct inference. The flaw was the conditioning: every member of the family shares the defect of Section~\ref{sec:pitfall} --- a host-redshift prior entering the numerator and the selection normalization differently.

Table~\ref{tab:partialfix} assembles the record: the two-factor ablation of Section~\ref{sec:realdata} (Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation}) extended by the two partial-repair negative controls. The pattern is exact. All four prior-\emph{inconsistent} configurations fail, in four different ways (rail up; rail down; rail with $\sigma_z$-dependent sign; flat--multimodal); both prior-\emph{consistent} configurations, in which the same kernel multiplies measurement and selection factors on both sides of every ratio, recover an interior peak --- at $h = 0.76$ with the globally tilted denominator retained, at the injected $h = 0.73$ with the local ratio. % src: .planning/gate/G3_ablation_cube.json (volume_global MAP 0.76; volume_deconv MAP 0.73); .planning/gate/G6_starvation_postmortem.md (verdict)
The numerator-only control is thus a feature of the record, not an embarrassment: it demonstrates that no \emph{single} ingredient cures the rail --- consistency does; the same holds verbatim for the host-mass channel (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:eddm}). On the identical events the consistent estimator behaves like a healthy likelihood --- peaked at the injected truth on the real data, and statistically calibrated on synthetic universes at full catalogue photometric width (near-nominal coverage, residual bias $-0.002$ over $120 \times 250$ events; Section~\ref{sec:coverage}) --- % src: .planning/gate/G6_starvation_postmortem.md (evidence item 5, d2 synthetic coverage)
while the catalogue-only baseline, which never touched the completion machinery, was never railed at all (Table~\ref{tab:derail}). The in-catalogue photometric information was present throughout.

Three observables separate the two failure classes; all three were available before the post-mortem, but were not read as discriminants.
\textit{(i) Railing versus broadening.} Starvation flattens the likelihood toward the prior: it produces \emph{wide} posteriors and cannot concentrate essentially all mass on a grid endpoint. Edge mass $\approx 1$ instead signals a coherent per-event multiplicative tilt whose accumulated log-likelihood slope grows linearly with $N$ --- sharpening with sample size exactly as a genuine signal would, which is why the pinned width mimicked starvation: a starved width stalls at an $N$-independent information floor, a railed one is pinned at the grid resolution, as measured.
\textit{(ii) The $\sigma_z$ response.} Starvation predicts posterior \emph{width} growing with $\sigma_z$ at preserved calibration; prior inconsistency predicts \emph{bias} growing as $\sigma_z^{2}$ at essentially fixed width, Eq.~\eqref{eq:pitfall:hbias} --- the signature actually measured in Section~\ref{sec:coverage}. The non-monotonic rail flip of the numerator-only control under $\sigma_z$ rescaling is impossible for monotone information loss; it is the fingerprint of competing normalization tilts trading dominance.
\textit{(iii) Invariance under repair.} Information content is a property of the data, invariant under changes of estimator; a normalization artifact is not. The decisive experiment is the one of Sections~\ref{sec:coverage} and~\ref{sec:realdata}: validate a prior-consistent estimator on known-truth synthetics at matched $\sigma_z$, then swap it in with the data held fixed. Starvation predicts a broad but calibrated posterior; mis-normalization predicts recovery --- and the identical events, catalogue, and photometric uncertainties returned a posterior peaked at the injected truth.

The correct summary is therefore not that photometric redshifts starve galaxy-catalogue dark-siren inference, but that prior-inconsistent estimators convert photometric scatter into coherent, $N$-amplified bias. Starvation was a property of the estimators, never of the data.
