# H0 Railing — Bias-Resolution Attempts Report

**Scope:** a standalone record of the *changes we tried* to resolve the H0
posterior **railing** in the LISA EMRI dark-siren pipeline, in the order they were
attempted, each with its motivation, result, and supporting tables/figures. This
complements the cumulative bias catalog in
[`docs/H0_BIAS_RESOLUTION.md`](H0_BIAS_RESOLUTION.md) (phases 9–48); the present
document covers the June 2026 investigation that traced the *remaining* railing to
the GLADE host photometric redshift and exhausted the per-event normalisation
search space.

**Date:** 2026-06-30 · **Branch:** `physics/photoz-joint-normalisation`

---

## Executive summary

The seed-600 production run rails the H0 posterior to the upper grid edge
(cluster MAP = **0.86** vs injected truth **0.73**, +0.13 ≈ +18%). A
one-ingredient-at-a-time "bridge" ladder (rungs A–I), each swapping a synthetic
closure ingredient for its real-pipeline counterpart, isolates the **single
decisive cause**: the GLADE flag-1 host redshifts are **photometric**, with
σ_z ≈ 0.035 ≈ **17×** the GW redshift precision (σ_z^GW ≈ 0.002 at z ≈ 0.05).
The in-catalogue likelihood convolves each host's broad redshift PDF, washing out
the sharp GW distance so that the catalogue redshift-density gradient — not the GW
measurement — drives H0. We then reverse-engineered five published photometric
dark-siren methods, none of which validates our corner (z ≈ 0.05, σ_z/z ≈ 0.7,
flat p_det ≈ 1), and used them to derive a candidate ladder of normalisations.
**Every candidate that keeps the per-event global-scalar denominator fails:**
the standard (bare-Gaussian) numerator rails DOWN to 0.60, every numerator-only
"clean" rails UP to 0.87, the local consistent denominator fails the σ_z → 0 gate,
and the only survivor — the global photo-z-smeared selection `D_sm` — de-biases the
density gradient but **recovers no peak** (multi-seed posteriors are flat or
multimodal, std ≈ 0.10, never peaking at the truth). The conclusion is a
demonstrated methodological result: **in-catalogue photometric dark sirens at
GLADE's σ_z/z ≈ 0.7, z ≈ 0.05, p_det ≈ 1 are information-starved.** We recommend
pivoting the H0 headline to the spectroscopic-host forecast arm (which recovers
h ≈ 0.725) plus a rigorously characterised GLADE-photometric limitation. A
separate, orthogonal frame bug (heliocentric vs CMB-frame host redshift) was found
and fixed; it contributes only +0.15% net and cannot cause the railing.

## 1. Root-cause identification — the bridge rung ladder (A–I)

### Motivation
The H0 railing survived the entire phase-9–48 bias catalog. The closure test
(synthetic spectroscopic hosts) recovered 0.73, but the real pipeline railed to
0.86 — a "closure ↔ pipeline gap." To localise the cause without ambiguity, we
started from the closure and swapped in **one** real-pipeline ingredient at a time
(a "rung"), re-running the *same* production normalisation functions
(`precompute_completion_denominator`, `precompute_missing_completion_denominator`,
`precompute_global_catalog_selection`), so only the ingredient under test changed.

### Result
The railing is reproduced **only** by (i) actively-wrong host↔sky matching
(C-iso, a sanity control) and (ii) the **host-redshift photo-z convolution**
(rung G). Every other real ingredient — measurement scatter, the catalogue n(z)
shape, the sky dimension / 3-D MVN / Fisher correlations, the candidate-selection
radius, the completion term `B_num`, the real survival `p_det`, the pixelated
completeness `f_k` — recovers 0.73 when added alone. The catalogue is 99.2%
in-catalogue (median z ≈ 0.046), so the bias is in the in-catalogue term, not the
dark/completion channel.

The decisive rung G sweeps the host-redshift error σ_z (fully faithful pipeline):

| σ_z | MAP `H0/100` | bias | rails? |
|-----|-----|------|--------|
| 0 (delta-z) | 0.725 | −0.005 | no |
| ≈0.002 (×0.05, spec-z) | 0.600 | −0.130 | yes |
| ≈0.009 (×0.25) | 0.870 | +0.140 | yes |
| ≈0.018 (×0.5) | 0.870 | +0.140 | yes |

Splitting σ_z by GLADE measurement flag confirms the data-usage origin (verified
against Dálya+ 2022, arXiv:2110.06184): flag 1 = **photometric** (σ_z median

dominated by photometric redshifts; the parse keeps `flag ∈ {1,3}` so the
large-σ_z photometric hosts are included. The column indices are all correct —
this is a data-usage finding, not a parse bug.

## 2. Literature reverse-engineering — is there a known unbiased form?

### Motivation
Before changing any normalisation, we reverse-engineered how five published
dark-siren H0 pipelines handle the in-catalogue photo-z term, across five scope
dimensions (per-galaxy z term; same-kernel num=denom identity; per-event vs
hierarchical normalisation; photo-z injection self-consistency; validated regime).
The goal: find a *working* photometric form and check whether our corner is inside
its validated range. Every load-bearing claim was put through an adversarial
verification pass; corrected/downgraded claims are flagged in the source docs.

### Result
**No literature method validates our corner**, and the user's premise that "the
correct unbiased photometric form already exists in one of these papers" was
**refuted**:

- **Gray et al. 2020** (1908.06050, our partition-norm template): photo-z is an
  *unexercised* equation — validated at σ_z = 0 only ("ignore these crucial
  redshift uncertainties altogether"). Under flat p_det its same-kernel denominator
  degenerates to a constant N.
- **Hitchhiker / Gair et al. 2023** (2212.08694): the per-event same-kernel form is
  explicitly an **approximation** (its Eq. 3); the exact object is the hierarchical
  Eq. 31/33 with a *shared latent* host redshift marginalised once across all
  events. The per-event form biases H0 **HIGH** for imperfect redshifts — the same
  direction as our cleaned-numerator 0.87 rail. Photo-z tested only to σ_z/z = 3%
  (we are ~0.7).
- **GWcosmo / Gray et al. 2023** (2308.02281): the comoving-volume LOS-prior
  H0-dependence **cancels** between numerator and denominator (their Sec. 2.1.4),
  so the denominator carries no local gradient — de-railing protection cannot come
  from the prior normalisation at all. Varying p_det and incompleteness do the work.
- **Cross-Parkin et al. 2025** (2502.17747): the purest per-event same-kernel ratio
  with injected photo-z — but it de-rails *only* because p_det varies across
  [0.15, 0.7] (threshold at z ≈ 0.29 inside the range) AND because their σ_z(photo)
  ≲ σ_z^GW (GW still localises the host). Both conditions fail for us.
- **Echoes / CHIMERA, Borghi et al. 2025** (2509.18243): structurally Option-A
  (our structure); explicitly **defers** photometric redshifts to future work
  (validated spectroscopic only). Its large-σ limit reverts to the rising
  `p_bkg ∝ dV_c/dz` — making the obstruction *stronger*, not weaker.

**The p_det ≈ 1 degeneracy** (confirmed by all four working extractions): when
p_det is flat over the in-catalogue support, `∫ p_det p_cat → ∫ p_cat = const`, so
the per-event/global-scalar denominator is H0-blind. The working methods break this
with *selection variation* (which we lack) or *ensemble/hierarchical coherence*.
The empirical two-rail bracket (0.60 / 0.87, truth between) independently shows the
defect is **not** in the numerator kernel.

### Displaying the result
The full method-by-method comparison and the ranked gaps live in
`.planning/derivation-photoz-incatalog/COMPARISON.md` (five papers × five
dimensions, with verification flags) and `GAP-ANALYSIS.md` (ranked divergences →
solution hints, the degeneracy proof, and the regime verdict). The single most
likely missing piece identified there: the **per-event frozen global-scalar
normalisation itself** — the lever that survives at flat p_det is ensemble /
hierarchical coherence (Hint 2), realised via a photo-z-consistent shared-latent
likelihood (Hint 3) with dV_c counted once (Hint 4); the global same-kernel ratio
(Hint 1) is necessary but provably insufficient here.

## 3. Catalogue interpretation — likelihood vs posterior, dV_c-once

### Motivation
The form of any fix hinges on whether the per-host PDF `N(z; z_g, σ_z)` is a
measurement **likelihood** (multiply by `p_bg ∝ dV_c/dz` once to get a true-z
distribution) or already a **posterior** (no extra prior). This also settles
whether GLADE photo-z is cosmology-circular and whether the code counts the
comoving-volume factor correctly.

### Result
- **Not circular.** GLADE+ flag-1 photo-z is an empirical neural-network (ANNz)
  estimate from broadband colours (WISE W1/W2 + SuperCOSMOS B_J/R_F), direction
  `z → d_L`. The genuinely circular `d_L → z` class is **flag 2, which the code
  correctly excludes** (`handler.py:297-300`). The railing is the *magnitude* of
  σ_z, not circularity.
- **Treat as a LIKELIHOOD.** ANNz outputs a regression point estimate + scatter
  with no comoving-volume prior applied; at σ_z/z ≈ 0.7 the prior dominates the
  posterior, so GWcosmo's posterior shortcut (valid only as σ_z → 0) is
  inapplicable. The correct object is the regularised
  `p_red(z) = N(z; z_g, σ_z) · p_bg(z) / Z_g`, `p_bg ∝ (dV_c/dz)/(1+z)`
  (Hitchhiker Eq. 16) — **dV_c counted exactly once.**
- **Our code has an internal inconsistency.** In the in-cat branch the host term
  carries **net 0** dV_c (the bare Gaussian's `β_G` cancels `D(h)`), whereas the
  dark/out-of-cat branch `B_num` carries **net 1** dV_c. The two host branches use
  different redshift-prior conventions for the same physical quantity. The Hint-4
  fix is therefore to **ADD one dV_c** to the in-cat numerator (net 0 → 1), which
  directionally lifts H0 off the low rail — but, per §4, the numerator-only fix is
  not sufficient.

### Displaying the result
The net-dV_c accounting per term (verified at file:line) and the
likelihood-vs-posterior verdict with literature reconciliation are in
`.planning/derivation-photoz-incatalog/CATALOG-INTERPRETATION.md`. Key cross-check:
the project's own negative result (numerator-only fixes do not de-rail) is
consistent with the conclusion that the dV_c-once rule must be applied
*consistently across numerator + selection denominator + re-injection*, not just
the numerator.

## 4. The candidate ladder tested in rung_I

### Motivation
With the interpretation fixed, we built every normalisation candidate the
literature comparison suggested and tested them side-by-side in the clean rung_I
closure, against a hard two-part acceptance criterion: **(gate)** at σ_z = 0.002
recover a peak near 0.73; **(de-rail)** at σ_z = 0.035 still recover an interior
peak near 0.73, beating the standard's 0.600.

### Result
See the candidate-results table above and panel (a) of the summary figure.
- **STANDARD** (bare-Gaussian, doubly-smeared dV_c): gate 0.744 ✓ / de-rail
  **0.600** (rail DOWN). The effective dV_c double-count biases H0 low (Hitchhiker
  "Inconsistency 1" direction).
- **Angle A/C** (per-galaxy regularised posterior `N·p_bg/Z_g`) and **Angle B**
  (global volume de-count `g = p_bg/(S p_bg)`): both pass the gate (0.748 / 0.744)
  but **rail UP to 0.870** at de-rail. Cleaning the numerator from `n_smooth` to
  `p_bg` does not remove the gradient — `p_bg ∝ dV_c/(1+z)` is *itself rising* — it
  only flips which way the imbalance tips. In the f = 1 continuous limit all three
  drive the numerator to the same `∫ p_GW p_bg`, giving the identical upper rail
  (0.8700, bit-for-bit).
- **Local same-kernel** (consistent denominator `Σ w_g ∫ p_det N dz`): **fails the
  gate** — rails to 0.870 at *both* σ_z, because with p_det ≈ 1 it reduces to the
  local number density `Σ w_g = n_gal`, reintroducing exactly the gradient it was
  meant to cancel.
- **Global `D_sm`** (photo-z-smeared selection, the same kernel in numerator and
  denominator over the *full* catalogue): the only candidate that passes the gate
  AND de-biases — but recovers **no peak** (see §5). Its lever is real and
  deterministic (edge-galaxy-dominated `D_sm/D(h)` cancels the global
  density-gradient rail), but a single global per-h scalar cannot track the *local*
  numerator gradient at z*(h), and with σ_z ≫ σ_z^GW there is no per-event
  localisation to track.

### Displaying the result
Numerator-only negative result (Angle A/B/C all rail UP) and the gate proofs:
`.planning/derivation-photoz-incatalog/NORMALISATION-FIX.md`. The `D_sm`

## 5. The decisive diagnostics — why `D_sm` is de-bias-without-peak

### Motivation
`D_sm`'s single-seed de-rail run landed at 0.693 (interior) and the ensemble mean
came out E[h] ≈ 0.735 ≈ truth — which *looked* like a win. Before promoting it, we
stress-tested whether the apparent success was statistical (a favourable draw),
deterministic (a real lever), or a grid artefact, via three diagnostics:
multi-seed stability, lever convergence vs catalogue size, and posterior shape.

### Result
- **Multi-seed (statistical vs deterministic).** At σ_z = 0.035, n_ev = 250: 6
  interior / 4 rail-up (0.87) / 2 rail-down (0.60), std 0.11. The single 0.693 was
  a **favourable draw**, not a property of the estimator.
- **More events.** At n_ev = 2000 the std is **0.097 — it did NOT shrink**, so the
  scatter is *not* event noise.
- **Lever convergence vs n_gal.** `d/dh log(D_sm/D)@0.73` = **+0.19 ± 0.014 → ±0.007**
  as n_gal grows 12k → 400k (`D_sm/D ≈ 0.920`) — already deterministic at 12k, so
  the scatter is *not* catalogue/edge under-sampling either. The lever is real;
  what is missing is a peak.
- **Posterior shape.** Per-seed (n_ev = 2000), the posterior peaks at
  **0.64, 0.64, 0.69, 0.87 — never 0.73**, several multimodal/bumpy.
- **E[h] ≈ 0.735 is an artefact.** The grid [0.60, 0.87] has midpoint 0.735 ≈
  truth, so a flat posterior gives E[h] ≈ 0.73 trivially. The *shape* (flat /
  multimodal), not the mean, is the real readout.

**Mechanism (resolved):** `D_sm` cancels the global density-gradient rail (the
deterministic lever), but the residual is the bare discrete-catalogue + photo-z
noise structure with no per-event localisation — a flat/multimodal posterior. This
is the structural obstruction (global scalar vs local gradient) plus irreducible
information loss, made manifest.

### Displaying the result
Panel (b) of [`figures/bias_resolution_summary.png`](figures/bias_resolution_summary.png)
shows the per-seed peaks scattered across the full grid, the favourable single-seed
0.693, and the E[h] ≈ 0.735 artefact line. All diagnostic numbers:
`.planning/derivation-photoz-incatalog/INCREMENT3-DSM-VERDICT.md`. Adjacent
controls confirming the diagnosis: the photo-z-consistent re-injection
(`rungH_results.json` — both inconsistent and consistent injection rail to 0.87 at
fixed catalogue) and the prior-domination check (`rungI_prior_domination.pdf`).

## 6. Conclusion — information starvation, and the recommended pivot

### Motivation
After exhausting the per-event normalisation search space (standard, three
numerator cleans, local consistent denominator, and the global same-kernel `D_sm`)
and confirming the lever is real but produces no peak, the question is whether the
in-catalogue photometric channel can be de-railed *at all* in our corner, or
whether the honest scientific output is a characterised limitation.

### Result
**In-catalogue photometric dark sirens at GLADE σ_z/z ≈ 0.7, z ≈ 0.05, p_det ≈ 1
are information-starved.** This is a demonstrated methodological result, not an
assertion:

1. The numerator kernel is not the lever (two-rail bracket, §4; NORMALISATION-FIX).
2. No denominator change that keeps the per-event/global-scalar structure can cure
   it (the p_det ≈ 1 degeneracy proof, §2).
3. The one structurally-correct survivor (`D_sm`, global same-kernel) de-biases the
   density gradient but recovers no peak, and the residual scatter does not shrink
   with events or catalogue size (§5).
4. The photo-z error (~17× the GW precision) destroys per-event host localisation,
   so there is no information left for any normalisation to recover.

The remaining untested object — the full hierarchical cross-event Monte-Carlo — is
predicted **null** at p_det ≈ 1 via the 1/N_gal "space is big" suppression
(`DERIVATION-HIERARCHICAL.md` + verifier); optional to run for definitive closure,
low expected value.

### Recommended pivot
- **Headline result → spectroscopic-host forecast arm.** Self-consistent spec-z
  hosts recover h ≈ 0.725 (`BRIDGE-FINDINGS.md`); delta-z on the full catalogue
  recovers 0.725. This is the project's defensible H0 forecast.
- **Characterise the GLADE-photometric limitation** rigorously (this report + the
  derivation docs) rather than quoting a railed posterior.
- **Carry forward the validated corrections** that feed the forecast arm: the
  frame fix (§7), the likelihood-vs-posterior / dV_c-once interpretation (§3), and
  the bridge harness for future spec-z mocks.

## 7. Orthogonal change shipped — heliocentric → CMB-frame host redshift

### Motivation
During the catalogue audit we found the parser feeds the **heliocentric** host
redshift (0-based column 27) into `d_L(z; H0)` with no peculiar-velocity / frame
correction, leaving the solar-motion dipole (v_sun ≈ 369.8 km/s) uncorrected in
`cz = H0 d_L`. This is a coherent, direction-dependent systematic for an H0
measurement.

### Result
The fix reads the adjacent CMB-frame redshift (0-based column 28, `z_cmb`).
Quantified impact: **per-event envelope ±2.47%** at z = 0.05 (apex / anti-apex),
but the dipole largely sky-averages over the detected sample, giving a **net
ensemble bias of only +0.15%**. This is **~120× smaller than the +18% railing and
orthogonal to it** — the railing is reproduced in a no-sky closure (sky-position
independent), whereas the frame error is intrinsically direction-dependent and
vanishes without sky directions. The fix is a correctness improvement, not a
railing cure.

### Displaying the result
| Property | Frame systematic | H0 railing |
|----------|------------------|------------|
| Magnitude | net +0.15% (per-event envelope 2.47%) | ~+18% |
| Sky dependence | direction-dependent (∝ cos θ_apex) | sky-independent |
| Reproduces in no-sky closure? | no | **yes** |
| Mechanism | uncorrected solar dipole in cz | normalisation / prior domination |

Full quantification, column map, and PV recommendation:
`.planning/derivation-photoz-incatalog/FRAME-SYSTEMATIC.md`. Shipped as the
`[PHYSICS]` frame fix (commit `7021f6f`, GitHub issue #15, PR #17); the host
peculiar-velocity value-correction is filed as issue #16.
