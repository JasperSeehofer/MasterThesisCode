Chapter 15 — The Slot Gets Filled

Chapter 14 ended with a characterised structure and an empty new-formula slot: “there is nothing to derive to, yet.” Two sessions later there was. This chapter follows the derivation that filled the slot, the second defect it uncovered on the way, the fix for that one too, the production change built on both, and the pre-registered measurement that checked what the change actually did to the campaign of record — not what it did in the venue, which A3 forbids assuming transfers.

1. Where Chapter 14 left off

Chapter 13 closed the unowned-residual thread as a ratified dissolution; Chapter 14 told the mechanism-isolation study that followed the venue-transfer campaign’s TRANSFER-CONFIRMED ruling — six candidate mechanisms for the σz-dosed coverage collapse, narrowed to a characterised but unowned structure: a host gate multiplying a graded impostor-sea amplifier. Both registered functional forms for that structure were refuted by the scan’s own statistics. Chapter 14 ended, deliberately, without a repair:

Stated plainly: the new-formula slot is empty. Filling it is a derivation — an author-gated step this readout does not take. PHYSICS_CHANGE_INTAKE_DOSSIER.md §2/§3, quoted in Chapter 14 §9

This chapter is that derivation, and everything that followed once it landed. The story runs through five ratified stages: a correct-form estimator that closes the 1D venue thread; a second, independent defect on the 2D mass channel that survived the first repair intact; a mechanism (“channel B”) that owns most of that second defect; a correct-form fix for it that closes the 2D thread too; and a production landing followed by a measurement of what the landing actually did to the campaign — which turned out to be less dramatic than the venue work might have suggested, and the record says so without softening it.

2. L4: the correct-form derivation, and why it explains an old result too

L4-DER Part 2 (ledger row #109) decomposed the coded venue tilt into named terms by exact single-switch A/B tests on a bit-validated mirror. The standout term was not new bookkeeping — it was a piece of physics the estimator had been silently doing wrong: the GW z-mass growth term, +1059.5 nats/h, derived from the identity ΣG = N/h − Σx/h with Ge = (1/h)(1 − D·D″/D′²). This single term retro-explains an earlier finding at 98.7% mass-kill (the A-M2′ candidate from the mechanism study) — a coincidence striking enough that the author’s ruling on the whole Part 2 account was simply “all approved.”

4. L6-DER2: the correct-form 2D fix, and the second thread closes

L6-DER2 diagnosed channel B’s own root cause: a S̄φ×g factorisation error — the coded form integrates two separate ∫dM factors where the selected joint prior demands one ∫dM φ·pdet·N. The fix is a fused mass kernel, g_sel, that folds the survival factor directly inside the mass quadrature rather than applying it as a separate multiplier. A bit-exact mirror pre-measurement (15 seeds, all four validation gates bit-exact, including the S≡1 unit-recovery check) predicted the fused form would collapse the excess to −11.74 ± 1.04 nats/h — already 91.4% of channel B’s own magnitude.
