Two things have to happen in this chapter. The likelihood needs a branch for a host it cannot point at, and that branch has to be weighted — because “is the host in the catalogue?” is not a fact you observe, it is a quantity you must put a probability on.

1. How incomplete, exactly?

“The catalogue is incomplete” is not yet a number. Make it one. For a patch of sky $\Omega$ and a redshift $z$, define the completeness $f(z,\Omega)$: the fraction of the galaxies actually out there at that redshift, in that direction, that made it into the catalogue. It runs from 1 (you have them all) to 0 (you have none), it is not a single number for a survey, and it is the only honest way to describe a flux-limited catalogue: nearby galaxies are bright enough to be listed, distant ones of the same intrinsic luminosity are not.

Completeness as implemented RATIFIED G2c D2 pixel_completeness.py Gray, Messenger & Veitch 2022, arXiv:2111.04629, Eqs. (2)(3)(5) The sky is tiled into 12,288 equal-area pixels. Each pixel $k$ carries one data-estimated number — the median apparent $B$ magnitude of its catalogued galaxies, $m_{{\rm th},k}$ — and the completeness is the closed-form Schechter luminosity ratio above that flux threshold, $$ f_k(z,h) \;=\; \frac{\Gamma\bigl(\alpha+2,\;x_{{\rm th},k}(z,h)\bigr)} {\Gamma\bigl(\alpha+2,\;x_{\rm dim}\bigr)}, \qquad x_{{\rm th},k} = 10^{\,0.4\,(M_\ast - M_{{\rm th},k}(z,h))} , $$ with $M_{{\rm th},k}(z,h) = m_{{\rm th},k} - 25 - 5\log_{10}\bigl(d_L(z,h)/\mathrm{Mpc}\bigr)$. This replaces Gray et al.’s hard magnitude cut $\Theta[m_{\rm th}-m]$ plus Schechter integral with a single smooth pre-integrated fraction (deviation D2); the per-pixel resolution follows Gray, Messenger & Veitch (2022), arXiv:2111.04629, Eq. (5) and Gray et al. (2023), arXiv:2308.02281, Eq. (2.3). The sky average $\bar f(z) = N_{\rm pix}^{-1}\sum_k f_k(z)$ is exact because the pixels are equal-area. Empty and zone-of-avoidance pixels self-set $m_{{\rm th},k}\to-\infty$, so $f_k \equiv 0$ there — pure darkness, with no separate mask.

Smoothness is not cosmetic here. It is what makes the missing-galaxy branch differentiable in $h$, which is the only reason the whole construction below can be swept across a grid at all.

2. A second branch: the host you cannot point at

Chapter 3 marginalized over a discrete unknown — which of these galaxies was it. The repair here is the same move one level up, over a binary unknown: was the host in the catalogue at all? Call that event $G$, and its complement $\bar G$. The law of total probability does the rest: $$ p(x_i \mid h) \;=\; p(x_i \mid G, h)\,\Pr(G\mid h) \;+\; p(x_i \mid \bar G, h)\,\Pr(\bar G \mid h) . $$ Two branches, two weights that sum to one. Nothing clever has happened yet — but notice what the second branch has to be. It cannot be “the catalogue sum with the missing galaxies filled in”, because you do not know where the missing galaxies are. That is what missing means. It has to be a smooth integral over where the population model says the uncatalogued galaxies would be, weighted at every redshift by the fraction the catalogue is missing, $1 - f(z,\Omega)$.

$$ p_i(h) \;=\; \underbrace{w_G(h)}_{\textbf{Ch 5}}\; \underbrace{\mathcal L^{\rm cat}_i(h)}_{\text{Ch 3, 4}} \;+\; \underbrace{\bigl(1-w_G(h)\bigr)}_{\textbf{Ch 5}}\; \underbrace{\mathcal L^{\rm comp}_i(h)}_{\textbf{Ch 5}} $$
$w_G$ — the probability this detection’s host is catalogued $\mathcal L^{\rm cat}$ — the catalogue branch (Ch 3, 4) $\mathcal L^{\rm comp}$ — the completion branch the host-redshift kernel inside $\mathcal L^{\rm cat}$ — Ch 7 the second observable — Ch 8

That is the master equation of this book, and from here to the last chapter nothing is added to it — only sharpened, attacked, and in three places found wanting. Read it once more in words: every event contributes through both branches, always. There is no per-event decision about which case applies, because you never learn which case applies. The weight $w_G$ is the probability that this detection’s host is in the catalogue, and it is a probability, not a flag.

The code assembles it as a single ratio rather than the two-term convex sum (deviation D4): algebraically identical, and numerically safer in the limit where the catalogue becomes complete and the dark branch’s normalization goes to zero. This chapter therefore treats the two forms as one object.

Everything about $w_G$ that matters at this rung is measurable, and it is measured. On this run it falls, monotonically, from 0.1625 at $h = 0.60$ through 0.1215 at $h = 0.73$ to 0.0947 at $h = 0.86$ real: seed61000/real_r1. Two facts about that sentence deserve to be uncomfortable.

Before either of them, be clear about what kind of object those three numbers are, because the type decides what the numbers are allowed to mean. Read the sentence above carefully: $w_G$ is not a per-event flag and not a per-event count. It is one number per $h$ — a selection- and volume-weighted average of the completeness $f$ along the whole line of sight, not $f$ at any one redshift and not a tally of this run’s events. So there is no arithmetic reason it must equal the fraction of events that happened to have a catalogued host. Whether it nevertheless should is a live claim, and it is not this chapter’s to settle ⏭ Ch 9.

First: 12%. Not 5%, which is the fraction of events whose host really is catalogued in this run — 76/1588 = 4.8% on seed 61000’s single realization. (The campaign figure the project’s own claim file quotes for the same quantity is 164/3135 = 5.2%, two seeds pooled; same quantity, larger sample, and the book keeps the two labelled because they are not interchangeable.) Those are different quantities from $w_G$, and the gap between them is a live, adjudicated problem — Chapter 9 measures it and Chapter 11 puts it on the board ⏭ Ch 9.

Trap — “w_G is roughly the catalogue’s completeness fraction.” It is a detection-weighted, rate-weighted, $h$-dependent posterior probability, and the paragraph you just read is why. Two measurements kill the identification. Locally: at EMRI-889’s redshift the sky-averaged completeness is 0.920, while the weight its likelihood is actually multiplied by is 0.1215 — a factor 7.6, in the direction that matters. Globally: $w_G = 0.1215$ against a realized in-catalogue rate of 76/1588 = 4.8% in this run. Whether that gap is a defect, and how large it is once it is measured properly, is claim C9 — Chapter 9’s subject, not a number to carry away from here ⏭ Ch 9. Treating $w_G$ as “completeness” is exactly the confusion that hid C9 for months.

Second: it has a slope. And the slope has nothing to do with the completeness curve you just plotted, which does not depend on $h$ at all. It comes from the only place it can: $h$ is what converts a redshift into a distance, so raising $h$ moves the detectable population out to higher redshift — the run’s own selection domain runs $z \le 1.133$ at $h=0.60$ and $z \le 1.500$ at $h = 0.86$ — and at higher redshift the catalogue holds a smaller share of the galaxies. A larger $h$ means a darker universe, in the literal sense of “fewer of the possible hosts are ones I have a name for”.

A slope in a weight is a tilt on the posterior, and it is present whether or not either branch prefers any particular $h$. That claim is easy to make and easy to check, so check it.

▸ For the GW reader — what $w_G$ is made of

$w_G = \beta_G/D$, with $\beta_G = D - \beta_{\bar G}$ (bayesian_statistics.py:3309-3311; G2c §2 rows beta_G, beta_Gbar, w_G), where $D(h)$$ = \int \langle$$p_{\rm det}$$\rangle_\Omega\,(dV_c/dz\,d\Omega)\,dz/(1+z)$ is Chapter 4’s full-volume selection integral and $\beta_{\bar G}(h) = \int \langle (1-f_k(z))\,p_{\rm det}\rangle_\Omega\,(dV_c/dz\,d\Omega)\,dz/(1+z)$ is its dark-side restriction — Gray (A18) with the hard magnitude threshold replaced by the smooth $(1-f)$. That ratio of integrals is why the narrator above could call $w_G$ a selection- and volume-weighted line-of-sight average: the weighting is $p_{\rm det}$ times comoving volume, and $f$ enters only inside it. What that normalizes to, and why the choice is not unique, is Chapter 9 ⏭ Ch 9.

3. The completion branch, and the step where you must integrate rather than evaluate

Write down the dark branch. The source is somewhere in a shell of luminosity distance $d_L$ that the gravitational wave has measured; it is in some direction, and the catalogue has no entry for it. So: integrate over redshift $z$, weight each shell by how much of the galaxy population the catalogue is missing there, $1-f(z,\Omega)$, times how much comoving volume that shell contains per unit redshift, times the gravitational wave’s own probability density for the source sitting at that distance. And — the step that costs orders of magnitude if you get it wrong — average over direction against the only honest prior for a source you cannot localize to a galaxy: an isotropic one, $1/4\pi$ per steradian.

The completion numerator RATIFIED G2a §3, §5, §7 bayesian_statistics.py:3210-3238 Gray et al. 2020, arXiv:1908.06050, Eq. (A17) $$ B^{\rm num}_i(h) \;=\; \int_{z_-}^{z_+} \bigl(1 - f_{k(\Omega_i)}(z)\bigr)\; \frac{\sin\hat\theta_i}{4\pi}\; \mathcal N\!\left(\frac{d_L(z,h)}{\hat d_{L,i}};\,1,\,\sigma_{{\rm marg},i}\right)\; \frac{dV_c}{dz\,d\Omega}\;\frac{dz}{1+z} $$ The sky average is not approximated: the gravitational wave’s three-dimensional measurement density factorizes exactly into a marginal in the distance direction times a conditional on the sky [Bishop 2006, PRML Eqs. (2.81)–(2.82)], and the sky factor integrates to one — leaving $1/4\pi$ times a one-dimensional Gaussian in the distance fraction $d_L(z,h)/\hat d_L$ with the marginal width the detector achieved (G2a Eq. 10; the pull-out is good to a relative error $\lesssim 10^{-5}$ at the median EMRI sky error). The $\sin\hat\theta$ is the solid-angle Jacobian: the measurement density lives in the bare sky coordinates, so an integral against $d\Omega = \sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi$ picks it up. What that width is, and why sky and distance are correlated in the first place, is Chapter 6 ⏭ Ch 6. Then $\mathcal L^{\rm comp}_i = $$B^{\rm num}_i$$/$$\beta_{\bar G}$ — the branch, normalized by the dark side of the selection integral, exactly as the catalogue branch is normalized by its own.
Trap — “The completion term is a fudge factor for incompleteness.” You have just watched it be built, so notice what it is not. It is a full likelihood branch derived from the same population model and the same selection function as the catalogue branch — Gray et al. (2020) Eqs. (A17)/(A18)/(A19), implemented as $B^{\rm num}/\beta_{\bar G}$ — and it is the side of the estimator that the 2D channel’s difference lives on. The replicated finding is qualitative and blunt: the dark class dominates that difference, and always with the same sign; the r1 partition puts its share at 84.2%, and off-r1 replication spans 84.2%–112.5%, mean 91.6%, so the share is a run-specific number and the dominance is the result CLAIM_2D_BIAS_20260730.md C3. Calling the branch a fudge is how you avoid noticing that its prefactor is the largest tilt in the inference C10. ⏭ Ch 8

Now the part worth stopping for. Before commit cb16142 that sky step was not an integral. It evaluated the measurement density at its own peak — at the best-fit sky position — and used that number as though it were the sky average. That is the difference between asking “how well does a source at exactly this spot fit?” and “how likely is it that the dark host is anywhere in the beam?”, and for a well-localized event those two questions differ by the ratio of a peak height to a flat prior.

4. The event with no candidates at all

Return to the 493 events that went silent in the cold open. In the mixture they are not a special case: if the localization volume contains no catalogue galaxy, the catalogue branch is an empty sum, $\mathcal L^{\rm cat}_i = 0$, and the master equation degrades — smoothly, correctly — to $p_i = (1-w_G)\,\mathcal L^{\rm comp}_i$. The event still speaks. It just says only what the population model lets it say.

That is not what the code did. For six years — the line dates to 2024 — an event whose catalogue lookup came back empty hit a continue and contributed no factor at all to the joint likelihood, logged at debug level.

A real bug, fixed — and the symptom did not move FINDING ledger #54 ledger #55 H0R:1368-1420 bayesian_statistics.py:2832-2844 Part one. Dropping zero-host events conditions the event sample on catalogue support, which is exactly the thing selection corrections exist to prevent. On the deep campaign venue it discarded 58% of the events, leaving a shallow, high-latitude subsample whose likelihoods tilt low, and the combined posterior railed at $h = 0.6000$ in both channels. Real bug, correctly diagnosed, fixed under the physics-change protocol (8db6c6e): a zero-host event now contributes the pure-completion likelihood $p_i = B^{\rm num}/D$ — the exact $\mathcal L^{\rm cat}\to 0$ limit of the mixture — and the skip was promoted to a warning with a per-$h$ lookup-yield metric. Part two. The re-evaluation with the fix in place still railed at 0.6000, in both channels. The restored events turned out to be $h$-inert: −59 nats across the whole grid, against −4265 nats from the events that had hosts all along. Fixing a real bug is not the same as explaining the symptom.

And the estimator the fix installs is itself under suspicion. Read the empty-catalogue limit of the master equation as a sanity check — $w_G \to 0$, $p_i \to \mathcal L^{\rm comp}$, a pure population-model inference with no catalogue information, which ought to be wide but centred. The project measured it against known truth with its calibration harness and found it biased high by +0.7…+5.4% in $h$ at deep incompleteness, with 68% coverage collapsing to $\le 0.27$ over completeness fractions 0.22–0.85, while the control configurations stayed calibrated ledger #57. A limit that is supposed to be trivially safe was not.

In this run that limit is the $\kappa = 0$ end of I5.1’s dial: MAP 0.755, posterior mean 0.7461 — high, by 0.025 in $h$, on one realization. Consistent in sign and order with the harness result; not a substitute for it.

▸ For the GW reader — what “493” is, precisely

The measurable statement is: 493 of the 1588 events have $\mathcal L^{\rm cat}_i = 0$ at every one of the 41 grid points, so the completion branch is their entire likelihood; the remaining 1095 have a non-zero catalogue leg at every grid point, and no event is zero at only some $h$ (0 of 1588). The delivered diagnostics do not separate “the ball-tree returned no candidate at all” from “candidates were returned but none contributed”, so 493 is the number this chapter quotes rather than a zero-host count. Two of the 493 carry in_catalog = True — the generator did put their host in the catalogue, and the candidate search still came back with nothing usable.

The nearest thing to a true empty-ball count is Chapter 3’s, and it is a different object measured a different way: reconstructing the production search ($n_\sigma = 1.5$, bayesian_statistics.py:2838) against the truth catalogue, 607 of 1590 events have no catalogue candidate at all, and 983 have at least one Ch 3 census. That is a reconstruction count on a different catalogue and a different event list — never a drop count, and never this run’s 493. Nothing in this run is dropped: the completion branch carries every event, which is the whole point of the section you are reading.

Note also that in_catalog (76 events) is the generator’s truth flag from prepared_cramer_rao_bounds.csv — whether the injected host was drawn from the catalogue — and is a different question from whether the estimator found a candidate (1095 events). The chapter uses the first for the class split of I5.2 and the second for the branch-alone curves of I5.1, and never mixes them.

The 58% figure is the deep Phase-2 venue (depth-1.5 pool, seed 1000), not this campaign; it is quoted as the historical measurement it is ledger #54. Its companion mechanism — the mass-pruned host catalogue being 99.98% below $z = 0.3$ — belongs to Chapter 8 ⏭ Ch 8.

The mixture is built. It has a catalogue branch, a completion branch, a weight with a measured slope, and a graceful limit for events with nothing to point at. Reading A: both branches are sound, the two classes genuinely carry different statistical power, and their crossing is a legitimate combination that happens to land near truth. Reading B: at least one branch is tilted, and the crossing is a coincidence of two opposing errors — in which case the measurement is not centred on truth, it is balanced there, and it will move as soon as anything disturbs the balance.

Distinguishing them needs a test, not an opinion, and the test is leverage: under Reading A a small perturbation of the class composition should barely move the answer. The project ran it. What it found is the subject of Chapter 11, and this chapter’s job is to make sure you arrive there having seen the crossing rather than been told about it.

Hold that thought for six chapters. In the meantime the bias ledger in the margin gains something it has not had before: an entry with no number — two branches that disagree, and no verdict on which one is wrong.

Self-check

Q5.1   $w_G$ is “the probability that this detection’s host is in the catalogue”. Why is it a function of h, and which way does it go as $h$ increases?

Show answer
Because $h$ sets the distance–redshift mapping, and therefore how far out in redshift a source at a given measured $d_L$ must be. Larger $h$ pushes sources to higher $z$, where the catalogue is more incomplete, so $w_G$ falls with $h$. Note what does not cause it: the completeness curve $\bar f(z)$ is exactly $h$-independent (measured deviation $\sim 10^{-15}$ across this grid). This is measured directly in the campaign diagnostics: $w_G$ runs 0.1625 → 0.0947 across $h = 0.60 \to 0.86$, monotonically real: seed61000/real_r1. That slope is not a detail — Chapter 8 shows the $(1-w_G)$ prefactor’s tilt is worth +31.55 nats across a 0.08-wide $h$ window, larger than any other single term CLAIM_2D_BIAS_20260730.md C10 ⏭ Ch 8.

Q5.2   The completion term is built from the population model in the region where the catalogue is blind. Why can it not simply be “the catalogue term with the missing galaxies filled in”?

Show answer
Because you do not know where the missing galaxies are — that is what “missing” means. The completion branch replaces the discrete sum over known positions with a smooth integral over the population’s expected distribution, marginalized over the sky against an isotropic prior. It is a genuinely different mathematical object, and the switch from discrete to smooth is where a factor of 5000 hid: evaluating the sky marginal at the peak of the density instead of integrating $1/4\pi$ inflated $B^{\rm num}$ ~5000× and railed the posterior at the grid edge G2a ledger #46.

Q5.3 (transfer)   Both sanity limits are reachable on I5.1’s dial, and one of them is discontinuous: the MAP reads 0.755 at exactly $\kappa = 0$ but 0.61 at $\kappa = 0.05$, a weight of well under one percent. Which limit is the discontinuous one, and what property of this run’s two branches — a property you can read off the event dossier — makes a vanishing weight fail to vanish?

Show answer
The empty-catalogue limit, $\kappa \to 0$. The reason is that the two branches are not the same size. The dossier gives it for EMRI-889: its catalogue leg is $\approx 5\times10^{7}$ times its completion leg at $h = 0.73$. A mixture $w\mathcal L^{\rm cat} + (1-w)\mathcal L^{\rm comp}$ is still catalogue-dominated for any $w \gg 10^{-8}$, so shrinking the weight to 0.7% does not fade the catalogue branch out — it leaves it in charge, complete with its rail at 0.600. Only at exactly $w = 0$ does the term disappear, and the answer jumps to 0.755. The complete-catalogue limit has no such jump; it approaches $p_i \to \mathcal L^{\rm cat}$ smoothly — and applied to a catalogue that is not complete it rails at 0.600 while silencing 493 events, which is exactly the bug of §4. Two lessons: a limit is not a small perturbation, and “the empty-catalogue estimator” is a genuinely different machine, one the project’s harness measured as biased HIGH by +0.7…+5.4% in $h$ at deep incompleteness with 68% coverage collapsing ledger #57.

Q5.4 (transfer)   §5 gives you two incompatible readings of the crossing — A, two honest classes combining legitimately; B, two opposing errors balancing. Do not restate them: design the measurement that tells them apart. Say what you would perturb, what you must hold fixed while you perturb it, and what size of response would falsify A. Then check your design against what the project actually ran.

Show answer
Perturb the class composition — the relative number of in-catalogue and dark events entering the sum — and nothing else. Hold fixed every per-event likelihood, both branch normalizations, the $h$-grid and $w_G(h)$; if you let the estimator re-fit anything you have changed two things at once and the response is uninterpretable. The falsifying scale is set by what the composition is already uncertain by: a sample of $N$ events knows its own class split only to $\pm1/\sqrt N$, so a perturbation of that size is one the data cannot tell you did not happen. Under A the MAP should move by less than the posterior’s own width; if it moves by an appreciable fraction of the answer, A is dead.

That is exactly the test the project ran, and the result is claim C5: a $\pm1/\sqrt N$ Poisson reweight moves the MAP by up to 0.025 in $h$ in the realistic venue against 0.0000 idealized — a response bigger than the distance between the answer and the truth, from a perturbation the data cannot exclude. That figure reproduces here to $10^{-6}$. The ratio form of the same statistic is disputed and the book carries both halves: the adjudicated $dh^\ast/d\varepsilon$ leverage is 1500–2400×, while recomputing it from the adjudicator’s own output gives 142–2458×, median 197× ch11_FLAGS.md F-ch11-1. Nothing in the argument depends on which is right — either way the response is enormous, and reading B survives. Chapter 11 is where C5 lands CLAIM_2D_BIAS_20260730.md C5 ⏭ Ch 11.

Q5.5 (transfer)   Both branches depend on how precisely the gravitational wave pins the source. Up to now the localization has been “an ellipsoid, somehow”. Name two properties of that ellipsoid you would need to know before you could trust either branch.

Show answer
Its size (how many galaxies are in it, hence whether the catalogue sum is dominated by one host or ten thousand impostors) and its orientation/correlation (whether the sky and distance errors are independent — they are not, because the LISA antenna pattern couples them, and treating them as independent mis-weights every candidate). You have already met the size question twice in this chapter without being able to answer it: it sets the width of the distance Gaussian inside $B^{\rm num}$, and it is the $\sigma_\phi\sigma_\theta$ in the factor of 5000. Defect Museum · interlude

Information starvation REFUTED

The hypothesis
By the end of June the evidence looked conclusive. Every attempt to get a peaked $H_0$ out of in-catalogue photometric hosts had failed. A five-paper, five-dimension reverse engineering of the literature found no published method validated in this regime ledger #40. So the verdict was written down formally: in-catalogue photo-z dark sirens are information-starved — the information is not in the data, and no normalization can conjure it ledger #41. Notice how comfortable that conclusion is. It is not a mistake anyone made; it is a limit of nature.
The decisive test
Two days later, an unrelated line of work produced a peaked, calibrated posterior at 0.73 from the same real data. The reconciliation gate (G6) then assembled every configuration that had ever “starved” or railed and asked what they had in common: bare numerator with a bare global denominator; a $4\pi$-fixed numerator with a mismatched denominator; a numerator-only volume tilt; a smeared denominator against an unsmeared numerator. Every single one puts the population prior into the numerator and the selection denominator differently.
The measured outcome
Overturned. “Information starvation” was “a property of prior-INCONSISTENT estimators, not of the data” ledger #41 → #52 G6_starvation_postmortem.md:24-33. The one configuration family that recovers and calibrates is the one where the same population prior multiplies the same measurement likelihood on both sides — Chapter 4’s counted exactly once. The failed partial fix was retained deliberately, as the negative control that shows a numerator-only repair does not cure the rail.
What it cost
A formally recorded verdict that had to be superseded in place, and a supersession note now carried at the top of the report that stated it. It also very nearly cost the project the whole approach: a conclusion of “the data cannot do this” is a stopping condition.
The transferable lesson
A negative result about your estimator is not a negative result about nature. The most seductive failure is the one that flatters you by being fundamental — it ends the investigation, it is not anyone’s fault, and it sounds like physics. Before accepting one, enumerate the estimators you actually tested and ask what they share. And note that this exhibit is now itself on the do-not-retry list: starvation may not be resurrected as an explanation ledger §2 item 13.
