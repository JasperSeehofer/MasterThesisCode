1. How to read an exhibit

A defect museum is only useful if it is not a morality play. Each exhibit below carries the same six fields, in the same order, because those six are what turn an anecdote into a transferable lesson:

  1. The hypothesis — stated as it was believed at the time, in its strongest form. If it sounds stupid, the exhibit is written badly.
  2. The pre-registered prediction — what the investigator said would happen before the measurement. Several exhibits exist only because this field was filled in first.
  3. The decisive test — the measurement that could have gone either way, and its venue.
  4. The measured outcome — the number, with its artifact.
  5. What it cost — cycles, sessions, or a shipped default.
  6. The transferable lesson — the thing you would tell someone starting the same investigation tomorrow.
The standing scoping rule RATIFIED BIAS_HISTORY_LEDGER.md §2 Negative conclusions are venue-scoped. “X was ruled out” is always “X was ruled out on venue V”. Two of this museum’s exhibits — volume_trunc and mass_trunc — were both falsified on the same seed600 494-event shallow subsample, so a shared venue idiosyncrasy would have fooled both. Every exhibit here names its venue, and none of them claims to be universal.
The museum meta-rule — binding on the rest of the book The ledger’s §2 “DO NOT RE-TRY” list plus the claim file’s Exonerated list form a binding union. Any interactive anywhere in this book that lets you try one of those configurations must volunteer the measured verdict — never leave a dead hypothesis open as if it were a live option. That is what the ledger browser at the bottom of this page is for CLAIM_2D_BIAS_20260730.md §Exonerated ledger §2 items 1–17.

2. Fixes that made it worse

The four exhibits in this room were all corrections: each one moved the code closer to something the investigator could defend on paper, and each one moved the answer further from the truth. They are first because they are the least intuitive failure mode in the whole collection.

Exhibit 1 · ledger #70 · 2026-07-12

volume_trunc — unify the numerator window REFUTED

The hypothesis
In the in-catalogue leg, the numerator $N_g$ and the per-galaxy selection integral $D_g$ were integrated over different supports: $N_g$ over the narrow event-level GW window, $D_g$ (and the kernel normalization $Z_g$) over the wide per-host galaxy window $[z_g - 4\sigma_z,\ z_g + 4\sigma_z]$. The book’s own “counted exactly once” principle says a prior should enter both sides the same way ⏭ Ch 4 §4. Unify the supports and the shallow-venue $+0.013$ residual should go away.
The pre-registered prediction
Registered in PRODUCTION-KERNEL-FIX-SCOPING §7b before the run: the bias moves toward truth. The A/B gate, the venue and the 7-point grid were all fixed in advance.
The decisive test
seed600, 494-event shallow subsample, volume_trunc vs the production volume_deconv, identical inputs, same 7-point $h$-grid. The baseline arm was required to reproduce the established reference (1D mean 0.745, 2D 0.768) — it did, to the digit, so the divergence is the kernel and not the driver.
The measured outcome
1D mean 0.7450 → 0.8000; 2D mean 0.7681 → 0.8000. Wrong direction, and about 4× the residual it was built to remove. Both channels collapse onto a single grid point: 99.9% of the 1D posterior mass sits at $h = 0.80$.
results/volume_trunc_ab_20260712/gate_result.json FINDING.md:1-58 ledger #70
What it cost
One implemented normalization mode (retained behind a flag, marked FALSIFIED, never wired into the CLI), one pre-registered campaign gate, and the working hypothesis that the shallow residual had a numerator-window cure. It did not: the ledger’s §2 carries volume_trunc as do-not-re-try, and the open attribution of that +0.013 has been unowned since 2026-07-13 ledger #69.
The transferable lesson: quadrature is physics. An integral you cannot resolve is not a weaker version of the integral you meant — it is a different number, and it will happily be a confident one.

Venue scope. Falsified on the seed600 494-event shallow subsample — the same subsample as Exhibit 5. Not cross-venue confirmed ledger §2, standing scoping rule.

M1 — the Falsified Fix Gallery: a quadrature dial

The FINDING names two compounding causes. The second is easy to state and is reproduced below without controversy: even evaluated exactly, the host-window numerator rises monotonically with $h$ — 0.2417 at $h = 0.60$, 0.4314 at 0.73, 0.6537 at 0.86 — so unifying the support rewards a higher Hubble constant in this regime, all by itself. The first cause is about the machinery, and it is the one worth putting a dial on.

The object being integrated is the in-catalogue numerator for one representative shallow photometric host — $z_g = 0.05$, $\sigma_z = 0.033$, so $\sigma_z/z = 0.66$: $$ N_g(h) \;=\; \int_{z_{\rm lo}}^{z_{\rm hi}} \underbrace{p\!\left(x_{\rm GW} \,\middle|\, d_L(z;h)\right)}_{\text{width } \approx 0.0025 \text{ in } z} \; \underbrace{\frac{\mathcal N(z; z_g,\sigma_z)\, w_{\rm pop}(z,h)}{Z_g(h)}}_{\text{width } \approx 0.033} \; dz ,$$ with $w_{\rm pop} = (dV_c/dz)/(1+z)$ G2b §1. Over the host window the integration range is $[0,\ 0.182]$ — about 73 times the width of the GW peak inside it. Production evaluates this with scipy.integrate.fixed_quad at a fixed Gauss–Legendre order $n = 50$ bayesian_statistics.py:3867. Fifty nodes. Across a window that holds one needle.

Predict, then turn the dial

You are integrating a needle in a haystack with 50 quadrature nodes. Now turn the node count down to 10. What happens to the value of the integral?

At $h = 0.73$ the exact value is 0.4314. The measured ladder reads 1.504 at $n = 10$ (3.5× high), 0.068 at $n = 20$ (6.3× low), 0.604 at $n = 30$, and does not settle until $n \approx 75$. It is not biased low and it is not stable: it is erratic in both directions, and which direction depends on $h$ — which is precisely how a quadrature artifact turns into an $h$-dependent tilt in a posterior. recomputed: gen_museum.py

The FINDING’s first mechanism is stated as “quadrature aliasing (dominant)”, and its evidence is a column of 0.0000 at $n = 50$ against an exact 0.24–0.65. This page reproduces that diagnostic script unchanged and gets its published table exactly. But re-computing the same integrand with the GW leg written the way production writes it — dist_vectorized, bayesian_statistics.py:3806 — gives 0.2376 / 0.4412 / 0.6524 at $h = 0.60/0.73/0.86$: $n = 50$ is accurate to 1.7–2.3%, not to 100%.

The reason is measurable and it reproduces the diagnostic’s entire table: physical_relations.py:132 dist() is scalar-only, and given an array it returns the value at that array’s first element. fixed_quad passes all nodes in one call, so inside that script the GW factor is a constant — which is why the scalar mode of the dial above is flat in $n$. Set the host window’s lower limit $z = 0$ and that constant is $e^{-200}$; set the GW window’s lower limit and it is $e^{-8}$, predicting 0.000265 / 0.000468 / 0.000701 — the published GW-window column 0.0003 / 0.0005 / 0.0007, digit for digit.

What this does and does not touch. It does not touch the verdict: volume_trunc is falsified on a production-code A/B gate, and the second mechanism (the exact numerator tilts high, 0.2417 → 0.6537) is reproduced here and is on its own sufficient. It does not touch production, which uses the vectorized call. It touches the attribution of mechanism (1) and the “two independent causes” framing. Aliasing is still real and still visible — at $n \le 30$, where the dial swings by factors of 3.5× and 6.3×. The book records this and does not resolve it: book/design/flags/museum_FLAGS.md F-museum-1.

▸ For the GW reader

fixed_quad(f, a, b, n) is Gauss–Legendre on the affine map of $[-1,1]$ to $[a,b]$: it is exact for polynomials of degree $\le 2n-1$ and says nothing at all about functions with structure below the node spacing. For $n = 50$ on $[0, 0.182]$ the mean node spacing is $3.6\times10^{-3}$ (Legendre nodes cluster at the endpoints, so the interior spacing is larger still) against a GW peak whose $\sigma$ in $z$ is $\approx 2.5\times10^{-3}$ — the marginal regime, which is exactly why the answer comes out accurate-ish and not reliable-ish.

Production hard-codes FIXED_QUAD_N = 50 and later re-implements the same node map by hand for a batched per-row reduction that is required to be bit-identical to fixed_quad (bayesian_statistics.py:450-500). That is the right engineering call — but it also means the quadrature order is a physics parameter of this pipeline that no gate currently sweeps. The narrow event-level GW window, which is what production actually integrates over in every shipped mode, is peak-centred and about $8\sigma$ wide, so $n = 50$ is comfortable there; the exposure is specific to wide-window variants like volume_trunc.

Exhibit 2 · ledger #77 · 2026-07-25

absolute_marginal (V1) — cure the deep rail REFUTED as a cure

The hypothesis
On the deep venue the posterior railed low, at $h = 0.60$. The diagnosis was that the mixture’s catalogue leg was normalized against a quantity that does not describe the absolute number of hosts, so V1 replaced it with an absolute-mass host marginal, calibrated through $\bar n_w = \Sigma_{\rm glob}/\beta_G$.
The pre-registered prediction
The deep rail clears; the MAP moves into the interior.
The decisive test
seed1000 probe plus the seed600 gate, commit 49b9ade.
The measured outcome
The rail relocated: LOW → HIGH, 0.86 in both channels; seed600 1D 0.775 (+0.030) and 2D railed at 0.86. And the identity it rests on was then measured on the real catalogue and found violated by 33% in value and 0.39/h in log-slope ledger #77, #78 H0R:1475-1482, :1548-1552.
What it cost
It became the shipped normalization of campaign #53 anyway — the realistic campaign this whole book’s final chapters read — and the successor generator_marginal exists specifically because $\bar n_w = \Sigma_{\rm glob}/\beta_G$ had to be replaced by $W_{\rm cat}/V_f$ ledger §3 Gate C item 1 ledger #81.
The transferable lesson: a fix can be correct in mechanism and still be a different bug. “It no longer rails low” is not the same sentence as “it is calibrated”.

Still live downstream. The failure of that calibration identity is not retired: it is the measured half of claim C9, which the adjudication leaves open pending the cell-B control ⏭ Ch 9, Ch 11.

Exhibit 3 · ledger #37 / #38 / #39 · 2026-06-30

Numerator-only normalization cleans REFUTED CONTESTED PROVENANCE

The hypothesis
Three sibling proposals, all of the same shape: clean up the way the host-redshift kernel is normalized in the numerator (Angles A/B/C), or build a locally consistent same-kernel denominator, or smear the global selection integral with the photometric width ($D_{\rm sm}$). Any of them should de-rail the estimator.
The pre-registered prediction
De-rail to an interior peak at truth, with the $\sigma_z \to 0$ gate passing.
The decisive test
The rung_I harness, a $\sigma_z \to 0$ gate, and a multi-seed $n_{\rm gal}$-convergence run.
The measured outcome
Angles A/C and B rail UP to 0.870 at $\sigma_z = 0.035$; the local same-kernel denominator fails the $\sigma_z \to 0$ gate (0.870 at both widths); $D_{\rm sm}$ de-biases without producing a peak — the width does not shrink with $N$ (std 0.11 → 0.097 at 2000 events) and the peaks land at 0.64 / 0.64 / 0.69 / 0.87, never 0.73 BRA:76-82, :81, :309-322.
What it cost
An era of the investigation, and — read forward — the “information starvation” verdict of Exhibit 10, which was assembled largely out of these three failures.
The transferable lesson: symmetry between numerator and denominator is not optional. Changing a prior on one side of a ratio is not a refinement; it is a different estimator.

Exhibit 4 · ledger #60 · 2026-07-11

The full Gray mixture as the cure REFUTED

The hypothesis
The pipeline’s deep-venue coverage failure comes from running a simplified two-branch mixture instead of the published form. Replace it with the full $(\beta_G \mathcal L^{\rm cat} + B^{\rm num})/D$ mixture — literally Gray et al. (2020), arXiv:1908.06050 — and calibration should be restored.
The pre-registered prediction
Bias shrinks toward the two-branch estimator’s +0.032 or below.
The decisive test
The EXP-41 coverage harness, 12 deep cells with known truth.
The measured outcome
It amplifies: worst-cell bias +0.123 against the two-branch +0.032, 12/12 cells fail, and the host branch flips from counterweight (−26…−182 nats) to co-tilt (+47…+166) pp_coverage_graymix_20260711/SUMMARY.md:12-42.
What it cost
A cycle, and the last comfortable belief available to a pipeline that had been drifting from its own reference paper.

Room 2

3. Right about the physics, wrong about the number

Every exhibit in this room identified something genuinely defective. Every one of them fixed it. None of them moved the number they were built to move. This is the most expensive category in the collection, because there is no moment at which the investigator is wrong — only a moment at which they are irrelevant.

Exhibit 5 · ledger #71 → #72 · 2026-07-13

mass_trunc — the truncated-lognormal mass kernel as the 2D driver EXONERATED

The hypothesis
The 2D channel’s residual is owned by the host-mass prior. Production used an untruncated linear Gaussian in $M$ — which assigns real probability to negative masses — where the physically correct object is a lognormal, truncated and renormalized on $[10^4, 10^7]\,M_\odot$ and multiplied by the EMRI rate $R_{\rm eff}(M)$ Reines & Volonteri 2015, arXiv:1508.06274.
The pre-registered prediction
Uniquely in this museum, the prediction came with a measured effect size first: an isolated single-host toy gave +0.016…+0.02 in $h$, with the right sign, at $\sigma_z/z \approx 0.5$–0.65. Confirmed in isolation ledger #71.
The decisive test
The same kernel, in the full pipeline: seed600 494-event A/B, commit 70bee1f.
The measured outcome
Δ2D mean +0.0029 — the wrong sign. Δ1D 0.0000, exact. Re-measured later against the ratified kernel in a 4-cell A/B: it moves the 2D leg down by only −1.8 / −2.3 nats of a +25.6 / +29.1 excess, and the 2D MAP does not move from 0.80 ledger #72, #89.
Why the toy was wrong
The toy isolated the numerator. The pipeline applies the same prior to the per-galaxy selection integral, and the likelihood is the ratio $\mathcal L^{\rm cat} = \sum_g w_g N_g / \sum_g w_g D_g$. A prior-shape change moves $N_g$ and $D_g$ in the same direction, so most of it cancels; what survives is the small piece where $p_{\rm det}$ weights the denominator differently from the way the GW likelihood weights the numerator.
What it cost
Two measurement cycles and, more expensively, a plausible story about the 2D residual that had to be dismantled twice — once as mass_trunc, once as “mass-kernel family” on the claim file’s own list.
The transferable lesson: the selection denominator is not a spectator. An isolated toy can be right about the physics and irrelevant to the answer.

Venue scope. Same seed600 494-event shallow subsample as Exhibit 1. DO NOT RE-TRY — ledger §2 item 1, which explicitly says: do not re-derive this a third time.

Exhibit 6 · claim file HA / C8 · 2026-07-30

HA — “$D(h)$ is not mass-marginalised” EXONERATED as the owner the defect it found is LIVE

The hypothesis
The 2D likelihood adds a term carrying a mass density to a term carrying none, so the mixture is dimensionally inconsistent — and the culprit is the selection normalization $D(h)$, which is not marginalised over mass.
The pre-registered prediction
Correcting the mismatch moves the 2D MAP down, toward truth.
The decisive test
Apply the correction to the delivered realistic runs and read the MAP.
The measured outcome
It moves r1 from 0.8133 to 0.8492 and r2 from 0.7820 to 0.8527 — a real effect, and the wrong sign. And the attribution is wrong too: a code trace shows $D$, $\beta_G$ and $\beta_{\bar G}$ are correctly mass-dimensionless (they are Mandel–Farr–Gair selection probabilities). The mismatch is numerator-internal — a 4D catalogue leg added to a 3D completion leg README_C8.md CLAIM_2D_BIAS_20260730.md §Exonerated.
What it cost
Nothing wasted — this is the exhibit that paid. HA-as-owner is dead, but the dimensional analysis that killed it is what produced C8: the 2D MAP walks with an arbitrary choice of mass unit (0.81329 / 0.78107 / 0.74440, then a rail at 0.600) while the 1D channel is bitwise invariant.
The transferable lesson: a confirmed dimensional mismatch can still be the wrong explanation of the number you care about. Separate “is this defective?” from “does this own the residual?” — they need different tests.

C8 is live. This museum retires HA-as-owner, not the well-posedness problem it found. The claim file’s adjudication keeps C8 open, with its cause attributed to the numerator legs’ measure mismatch — not to $D(h)$ ⏭ Ch 8 §6, Ch 11.

Exhibit 7 · ledger #54 → #55 · 2026-07-10 → 2026-07-25

The zero-host fallback (issue #29) REAL BUG, FIXED NOT THE SYMPTOM

The hypothesis
Events whose candidate ball contained no catalogued galaxy at all were being silently dropped from the combination — not down-weighted, not flagged, dropped. On the deep venue that was 58% of the events. Restore them via the pure-completion branch and the deep rail at $h = 0.600$ should clear.
The pre-registered prediction
The rail clears once the discarded 58% are back in the likelihood.
The decisive test
EXP-40: a full re-evaluation with the code fix as the only variable.
The measured outcome
The bug was real and was fixed (8db6c6e). The rail persisted at 0.6000 in both channels. The split explains why: the host-bearing events carry −4265 nats across the grid, and the restored fallback events are $h$-inert — a total of −59 nats ledger #54, #55 H0R:1368-1420.
What it cost
A campaign hold, and a follow-up measurement that made things worse before better: the restored pure-completion estimator is itself biased high by +0.7…+5.4% in $h$, with 68% coverage collapsing to ≤0.27 at completeness fractions 0.22–0.85 ledger #57.
The transferable lesson: fixing a real bug is not the same as explaining the symptom. The size of a bug is not evidence about its effect; only a measurement is.

Do not confuse two 58%s. The number above is the drop rate of ledger #54 on a deep venue. It is a different quantity, in a different venue, from claim C5’s statistic about in-catalogue events in campaign #53 — which must always be quoted with its fair-framing amendment (per-event cosmetic and class-summed +3.4–6.1σ) ⏭ Ch 11 §2.

Exhibit 8 · ledger #61 · 2026-07-11

The $w_G = \beta_G/D$ bookkeeping fix REFUTED as the fix C9 is LIVE

The hypothesis
The mixture weight $w_G$ — the probability that this event’s host is in the catalogue — is computed as $\beta_G/D$, and that is the wrong bookkeeping. Re-derive it as a membership-conditioned inverse and the deep tilt should go.
The pre-registered prediction
Bias shrinks; the failing coverage cells recover.
The decisive test
N-2b on the coverage harness, 12 deep cells.
The measured outcome
Bias +0.005…+0.044, 12/12 fail — and the diagnosis of why is the lesson: the tilt did not shrink, it relocated to the host branch (+94…+455 nats) pp_coverage_graymix_20260711/SUMMARY.md:64-75.
What it cost
A cycle — and it bought a permanent guard: the ledger now records that this particular fix form is dead, so nobody spends the cycle again.
The transferable lesson: relocating is not removing. If your correction moves a discrepancy from one leg to another, your model of the discrepancy is wrong, not your arithmetic.

This exonerates the fix, not the defect. Claim C9 — that $w_G$ is mis-calibrated by 2.3–2.5× against the realized catalogued-host rate, at $z = -11.86$ — is live and gated on the cell-B control. The re-litigation guard is narrow and specific: do not re-try the membership-conditioned inverse ledger §2 item 3 ⏭ Ch 9 §6.

4. The wrong layer — and one that simply wasn’t there

A museum of only near-misses would be a morality play. This room has two hypotheses that were investigated at the wrong altitude, and one that was a perfectly reasonable suspicion about a bug that did not exist.

Exhibit 9 · ledger #17 · 2026-05-02/03

The $p_{\rm det}$ anchor escalation REFUTED

The hypothesis
The detection probability is built on a finite grid in $d_L$, and its very first bin — the $d_L \to 0$ asymptote, where detection should be certain — read 0.7931. That underestimate sits in the denominator of every event’s likelihood. A real defect, and an obvious suspect.
The pre-registered prediction
Raise the anchor, and the MAP moves toward truth. Plan 45-06 raised it to 0.8873 — a +12% lift applied to every event at once.
The decisive test
Production re-evaluation, anchor as the only variable.
The measured outcome
1D 0.7550, 2D 0.7450 — unchanged. Zero grid steps. ledger #17 H0R:184-208, :1989-1990
What it cost
Three plan cycles (45-02/03, 45-04/05, 45-06). The two shipped anchor fixes moved the production MAP by at most one grid step; the decisive +12% escalation moved it by none.
The transferable lesson: the layer you can see is not the layer that matters. Before spending a cycle on something because it is visibly defective, ask what the answer is actually a function of.
Exhibit 10 · claim file HB · 2026-07-30

HB — the hard mass window as support truncation REFUTED — by its own investigator

The hypothesis
The 2D candidate search applies a hard mass cut where the mass information should enter as a soft kernel weight. Because the window’s bounds depend on $z_{\min}$/$z_{\max}$, which depend on $h$, the truncation is $h$-dependent by construction — a natural candidate for the −504.8-nat dark-class tilt. The catalogue mass error is huge ($\sigma_{\ln M} \approx 1.28$) against a near-exact GW mass, so only the lower edge bites: 193 low-side rejections against 1 high-side.
The pre-registered prediction
The window’s $h$-tilt accounts for a substantial share of the dark tilt.
The decisive test
Measure the truncation’s own $h$-tilt over 0.73 → 0.81 and compare it to the target.
The measured outcome
−0.317 nats = 0.063% of the 504.8-nat target, roughly 50× too small at its ceiling, and sign-inverted — removing the window moves the MAP up by ~+0.0015. The investigator killed their own candidate HANDOFF_20260730.md §4 (historical record only) CLAIM_2D_BIAS_20260730.md §Exonerated.
What it cost
The session that ended with HB written up as “the one unrefuted candidate”. Two of the framing assumptions used to motivate it were also false: the “why is 1D spared?” screen has no discriminating power for mass hypotheses (the 1D channel never sees the mass window at all — handler.py:592 vs :605), and campaign #51 is a stronger exposure, not a non-control (153,473 impostors pass the window in the unscattered case).
The transferable lesson: size your candidate before you believe it. A mechanism that is real, $h$-dependent, and 1000× too small is not a partial explanation — it is a different subject.

Citation hygiene. HANDOFF_20260730.md §4 still reads as though HB were live; it is retained only as historical record behind a superseding block, and this book never cites it as current sources map §7.2.

Exhibit 11 · ledger #4 · 2026-03-31

The Gaussian 3D/4D index bug NOT A FACTOR

The hypothesis
The likelihood evaluates a multivariate Gaussian in either three coordinates $(\phi,\theta,u)$ or four (adding $M_z$). Two code paths, two index conventions, one plausible off-by-one. Early in the investigation, with a large unexplained bias on the table, this is exactly the sort of thing that is usually the answer.
The pre-registered prediction
If the indexing is wrong, the $\delta$-approximation analysis will show a measurable shift.
The decisive test
A $\delta$-approximation analysis of the evaluation path.
The measured outcome
NOT A FACTOR H0R:1975. There was no bug. The indices were right.
What it cost
Very little — which is the point of including it.
The transferable lesson: sometimes the plausible bug simply is not there. A museum that only exhibits near-misses teaches you that every suspicion is nearly right. Most are not, and the cheap check that says so is a good check.
Room 4

5. Failures about your estimator, mistaken for facts about nature

The two exhibits in this room are the most dangerous in the collection, because both of them are flattering. One concluded that the data were fundamentally uninformative. The other produced a beautifully stable, precise, reproducible number that contained no information about $H_0$ whatsoever.

Exhibit 12 · ledger #41 → #52 · 2026-06-30 → 2026-07-02

Information starvation OVERTURNED

The hypothesis
Formally concluded, in writing, as a verdict: in-catalogue photometric dark sirens at $\sigma_z/z \approx 0.7$ are information-starved. Not a bug — a limit of the data. It was supported by a five-paper, five-dimension literature reverse-engineering that found no published method validated in this regime ledger #40, and by three independent failed cures (Exhibit 3).
The pre-registered prediction
The negative control: a numerator-only volume tilt (rung G2) should also rail, because if starvation is a property of the data, no estimator can escape it.
The decisive test
Run the negative control and the production ablation cube side by side, and see whether any configuration recovers.
The measured outcome
One family does recover — the one where the same population prior multiplies the same measurement likelihood on both sides of the ratio. The ablation cube reads bare+global 0.60 → volume kernel in $N_g$ and $D_g$ 0.76 → with the local denominator 0.73, and a synthetic coverage run of the consistent estimator is calibrated at bias −0.002. Verdict, in the postmortem’s own words: starvation was “a property of prior-INCONSISTENT estimators, not of the data” G6_starvation_postmortem.md:24-33 ledger #48, #52.
What it cost
A month of work aimed at the wrong target, a literature review commissioned to confirm a conclusion, and a written verdict that had to be publicly superseded. The “counted exactly once” rule that runs through this whole book is the artifact that survived it.

Self-check

M.1   mass_trunc was confirmed in an isolated single-host toy (+0.016…+0.02, right sign) and exonerated in the full pipeline (Δ2D +0.0029, wrong sign). Both measurements are correct. What is the toy missing, and what is the general rule?

Show answer
The toy computes only the numerator $N_g$. The pipeline computes the ratio $\sum_g w_g N_g / \sum_g w_g D_g$, and the same host-mass prior enters $D_g$ — so a change in prior shape moves numerator and denominator in the same direction and mostly cancels. What survives is only the part where $p_{\rm det}$ weights the denominator differently from the way the GW likelihood weights the numerator, and that residue happened to have the opposite sign ledger #72. The general rule is the one the finding itself names: the selection denominator is not a spectator. Any toy that omits the selection integral can predict the sign of a numerator effect and nothing about the H₀ shift.

M.2   The volume_trunc gate found two reasons the fix failed. Suppose you could only have measured one of them. Which one would still have been decisive for the campaign, and why does that matter for how you write up a falsification?

Show answer
The second — that even the exact host-window numerator rises monotonically with $h$ (0.2417 → 0.4314 → 0.6537, reproduced on this page). That is a statement about the estimator and it transfers to any implementation; the first is a statement about a particular quadrature call and would be repaired by a better integrator.
