From Bayes’ Rule to H₀ an interactive discovery book

From Bayes’ rule to the EMRI dark-siren H₀ pipeline

A step-by-step, interactive companion to the thesis. Every representation is introduced with its motivation — either “without this, the posterior is defective” (shown, not asserted) or “this addition measurably helps, here is how much.” The book ends where the research currently ends: with the estimator’s live, honestly inconsistent state.

The contract of this book We will build an estimator for the Hubble constant out of gravitational-wave events that carry no redshift — and then spend most of the book trying to break it. Every number on every page carries a provenance chip to the project artifact that measured it; toys are labelled toys; and the four live inconsistencies the project has not resolved are presented unresolved. The last chapter does not end with an answer, because the project has not ended with one.

The journey

Twelve chapters plus an annex, each opening on the failure the previous chapter could not answer. The map below names each chapter’s question; what each chapter discovers is folded underneath, because four of the discoveries are cold-open reveals the chapters ask you to predict first.

  1. Two Numbers That Should Be One (prologue) — two precise measurements of the same constant disagree; what would a third method have to be to matter?
  2. A Ruler That Needs No Ladder — general relativity hands you a distance with no calibration ladder; what it refuses to hand you is the problem. The dossier opens: EMRI-889.
  3. Bayes, Once and For All — one distance and no redshift is not yet an inference. What does stacking many events actually buy?
  4. Which Galaxy? — the sky box is exquisite, and the host is still not determined.
  5. The Universe Only Shows You Its Loud Half — you only ever fit the events you detected. What does leaving that fact out of the likelihood do? Museum interlude: the p_det anchor.
  6. The Galaxy You Cannot See — most hosts are not in the catalogue at all, and the estimator must answer anyway. Interlude: information starvation, overturned.
  7. Opening the Black Box — “an ellipsoid, somehow” is no longer good enough: how big, and correlated with what?
  8. A Redshift Is Not a Number — a catalogue redshift is a distribution, and this chapter measures what treating it as a number does. Interlude: volume_trunc.
  9. A Second Handle: the Mass Channel — the mass channel should sharpen everything. Watch what it does instead. Interlude: mass_trunc.
  10. Building a Universe to Break Your Estimator — every claim so far compared to “truth”; where did truth come from, and does it agree with the estimator?
  11. Is It Calibrated? — recovering the truth once is not the same as being right. Interlude: the H₀-independent estimator.
  12. The State of the Art, Honestly — the scoreboard, exactly as the project adjudicates it — and the control that landed the night the book was built. This chapter does not resolve.
  13. The Defect Museum (annex) — 98 hypotheses, and what each one cost.
Spoiler: what each chapter discovers (the four cold-open reveals included — open after your first read-through)
  1. every rung of the local ladder is calibrated against another rung; there is no independent ruler.
  2. GR gives dL absolutely, and says nothing at all about z.
  3. one distance and no redshift is not yet an inference; and N events do not sharpen like √N — a handful carry almost everything.
  4. not knowing the host is not an error bar: it is a sum, and the weights in that sum are astrophysics you have to commit to.
  5. without dividing by what you could have seen, the posterior does not get noisy; it runs to the edge of the prior and stops caring what the truth is — and the repair has to be derived, not patched.
  6. for 1512 of 1588 events the host is not in the catalogue at all.
  7. the error ellipsoid is real, measured, and correlated in exactly the ways that matter later.
  8. catalogue redshifts are photometric at σz/z ≈ 49%, and plugging in the central value moves the answer.
  9. the mass channel should sharpen everything — and the answer moves the wrong way.
  10. the mock universe is the estimator’s harshest critic: the generator and the inference disagree about a quantity both of them own.
  11. it recovers truth at −0.24σ; that is not the same as being right.
  12. three measured inconsistencies plus a fourth, and an attribution that stayed confounded until the pre-registered control ran — 2026-07-31, two hits and a one-grid-step miss.
  13. most of the 98 dead hypotheses were the most plausible ideas — which is exactly why they needed killing in writing.

How to read this book

  • Reading as… — the topbar switch sets how much depth is open by default: Curious (the main thread only — the default), Methodology (code sites and Gray et al. mappings open), and All details (methodology plus every numbers view, provenance emphasized). Stepping back down re-collapses what the switch opened — never a fold you opened yourself.
  • The bias rail — the panel on the right of every chapter is the estimator’s live scorecard: its measured bias in h as it is built, repaired, and broken again. Amber dots are measured-but-unresolved defects. The scorecard does not monotonically improve; that is the point.
  • Symbol passport — tap any dotted symbol for its definition, units, defining code site, and status; pin it to your personal glossary (★ in the topbar).
  • Provenance chips and badges — every measured number carries a chip naming its artifact; every claim carries the project’s own trust tier (RATIFIED CANDIDATE FINDING OPEN REFUTED). Toys are chipped toy: analytic, real data real: seed61000/real_r1.
  • Predict, then reveal — the interactives ask you to commit to a guess before showing the measurement. The reveal is locked, not suggested — the same instrument the project itself uses (pre-registration), turned on the reader.
  • “Has this been tried?” — every sandbox page carries a search box over the project’s 98-row defect ledger, seeded with the dead hypotheses that page’s own dials can reach; and a dial tagged with a dead hypothesis volunteers its verdict (⚖ #N) inside the widget the moment you turn it. If your clever fix has a name, a date, and an obituary, the search box will find it.
  • Self-checks — every chapter ends with questions whose answers stay hidden until you ask; the last one is always the transfer whose answer is the next chapter’s opening failure. Chapter 11’s last question has no answer key, because the project has none.

The honest state, up front

What this book does not resolve The pipeline’s current adjudicated state (2026-07-30, as amended 2026-07-31) carries three live, measured inconsistencies — C7 (the ratified host-z kernel and a measured inflation law collide; the 2×2 cell B control settled the magnitude and attribution on 2026-07-31, not the collision), C8 (the 2D headline moves under an arbitrary mass-coordinate rescale), and C9 (the delivered mixture weight disagrees with the generator’s realized rate at z = −11.86) — plus a fourth (C5: the two class-conditional posteriors rail at opposite edges, and the 1D headline is their crossing), and an attribution (C6) that was confounded until the control was run on 2026-07-31 — the pre-registered 2×2 cell B landed the night this book was built, and the estimator configuration owns the bulk of the 2D displacement. Four questions stay live; none is resolved by narrative. Chapter 11 is the scoreboard; the museum is the graveyard it stands on.

How this book is built

No build framework — plain HTML/CSS/JS, vendored Plotly and KaTeX, works over a trivial local server and on GitHub Pages alike. Every figure’s data is produced by a deterministic Python generator (book/generators/) reading the thesis pipeline’s own artifacts read-only; make_all.py rebuilds everything. See book/design/BOOK_TECH_DESIGN.md for the architecture, book/design/BOOK_DESIGN.md for the authoritative build spec, and book/BUILD_REPORT.md for the integration QA report.
