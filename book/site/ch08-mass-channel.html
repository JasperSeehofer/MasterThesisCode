Chapter 8 — A Second Handle: the Mass Channel

The mass channel should sharpen everything. Watch what it does instead.

The promise this chapter starts from Every chapter so far fought a deficiency: a missing denominator, a missing branch, a redshift that was not a number. This one starts from an abundance. The gravitational wave measures the binary’s redshifted mass $M_z = M(1+z)$ to a fractional precision of about $10^{-4}$ — the claim file’s figure; recomputed from this campaign’s own Cramér–Rao table the median is $8.8\times10^{-8}$. Both values are live and §1 carries both ch06_FLAGS.md F-ch06-5; nothing below depends on which is right. Every catalogue galaxy carries an estimate of its own black-hole mass $M_g$. Then $M_z/M_g - 1$ is a second, independent estimate of the redshift of that host — a second handle on the same event, free, already in the data. It should sharpen everything.

Predict, then reveal — what does a second observable do to the answer?

Same 1588 detections, same selection machinery, same host-redshift kernel. The only change is that the likelihood now also asks each candidate galaxy whether its black-hole mass is consistent with the measured $M_z$ at the hypothesised redshift. Commit before you scroll.

It moves the centre, and it does not move it a little. Across the ten realistic runs the 1D MAP averages 0.7205 and no run exceeds $2\sigma$; the 2D MAP averages 0.807 — a bias of +0.077 in $h$, with a mean pull of +4.04 and 10 runs out of 10 beyond $2\sigma$. 1. The second handle

Chapter 6 established what the waveform actually measures. Among the fourteen parameters, the two masses are measured through the phasing: an EMRI spends of order $10^5$ cycles in band, and the accumulated phase is exquisitely sensitive to the mass that sets the orbital frequency. But the detector never sees the source-frame mass. Cosmological expansion stretches every timescale by $(1+z)$, so a binary of source-frame mass $M$ at redshift $z$ produces exactly the waveform of a binary of mass $M(1+z)$ at rest. The detector measures the product, and only the product.

The redshifted mass, and why it is a redshift measurement RATIFIED dark_siren_likelihood.md §7–§11 bayesian_statistics.py:4014 The GW datum is the detector-frame mass $$ M_z \;=\; M\,(1+z), \qquad \frac{\sigma_{M_z}}{M_z} \;\approx\; 10^{-4} \quad\text{(claim file)} \quad\big|\quad 8.8\times10^{-8} \;\text{(measured median)} $$ for the events in this campaign CLAIM_2D_BIAS_20260730.md C4 ch06_FLAGS.md F-ch06-5.
Two values, both printed, neither preferred. CLAIM_2D_BIAS_20260730.md:172 states $\sigma_{M_z}/M_z \approx 10^{-4}$; Chapter 6 recomputed the same stored quantity — $\sqrt{\Sigma_{MM}}/M$ from prepared_cramer_rao_bounds.csv — and measured a median $8.8\times10^{-8}$ (p5–p95 $2.5\times10^{-8}$–$3.0\times10^{-7}$; EMRI-889 itself $1.36\times10^{-9}$, which is the number Chapter 6 §4.1 prints). Three orders of magnitude apart, and this book does not silently pick one: the dispute is open in ch06_FLAGS.md F-ch06-5, and amending the claim file is the author’s call. Nothing below depends on which is right — every argument in this chapter uses only that the GW side is negligible against the catalogue side’s $\sigma_{\ln M} \approx 1.28$, and $10^{-7}$ is negligible for the same reason $10^{-4}$ is.

It carries no cosmology: it is a measured number, like $\hat d_L$. Now suppose a candidate host galaxy $g$ supplies an estimate $M_g$ of its central black hole’s source-frame mass. Under the hypothesis that $g$ is the host, at the hypothesised redshift $z$, $$ M_z \;\overset{!}{=}\; M_g\,(1+z) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 1+z \;=\; M_z/M_g . $$ This is a redshift estimate built from masses alone — it never touches $d_L$, and so it is statistically independent of the distance channel given the host. The likelihood implements it as a marginal over the host’s true mass, in the dimensionless fraction coordinate $x = M_z/M_{z,\det,i}$ that Chapter 6 introduced for distance — the analytic Gaussian product mz_integral bayesian_statistics.py:4363-4370, with the matching mass marginalization inside the per-galaxy selection integral $D_g$ bayesian_statistics.py:3362 — counted once on each side, exactly as Chapter 4’s rule demands. There is no analogue of this term in Gray et al. (2020), arXiv:1908.06050: the mass axis is this project’s own extension G2c D6.
This is not the spectral-siren method — and the failure modes are different A GW reader arriving at “the redshifted mass is a redshift handle” will reasonably think of spectral sirens (Farr et al. 2019, arXiv:1908.09084; Ezquiaga & Holz 2022, arXiv:2202.08240; Mastrogiovanni et al. 2021, arXiv:2103.14663), which is the method LVK actually uses when no catalogue is available. They are not the same thing.

Spectral sirens read $z$ off a feature of the population’s source-frame mass function — the pair-instability gap, a break, a peak. The detector-frame spectrum is the source-frame one stretched by $(1+z)$, so the location of a known feature in $M_z$ dates the population statistically. No host, no catalogue: the redshift information is population-level, one number for many events, and the dominant systematic is that the assumed mass function may itself evolve with redshift (a feature that moves fakes an $H_0$).

What this chapter does is a per-host association: for each individual candidate galaxy $g$, compare the measured $M_z$ against that galaxy’s own catalogue BH-mass proxy $M_g$ and ask whether the pair is consistent at the hypothesised redshift. The redshift information is per-event and per-candidate; there is no population feature anywhere in it. Its failure modes are therefore an entirely different list: the proxy’s scatter (§2), the reference measure the proxy’s distribution is written in (§2), the fact that the proxy is derived at a fixed fiducial cosmology (§4’s [RATIFY-M7]) — none of which a spectral-siren analysis has, and none of which is fixed by anything the spectral-siren literature does. The one thing the two share is that both are sensitive to a mass distribution being wrong; the distributions in question are different objects.

Two of this term’s previous incarnations were wrong in ways worth a sentence each, because both are the kind of error a careful person makes. A spurious $1/(1+z)$ Jacobian sat in the with-mass numerator until Phase 15 ledger #5 — a double-counted time dilation. And until commit f01595c the numerator queried the observation $M_{z,\det}$ where it should have queried the hypothesis $M_g(1+z)$, while the grid’s mass axis mixed source and observer frames; the observer-frame fix moved the 2D bias from +0.0141 to +0.0007, a factor 20 ledger #22. Neither is the subject of this chapter. Both are the reason the subject of this chapter is not “a bug”: by the time campaign #53 ran, the obvious bugs had been found.

If measures and Jacobians are old friends, the GW stratum below is where the mass measure actually enters the code — or flip the topbar to Reading as: Methodology to keep these folds open.

▸ For the GW reader — where the mass axis lives, and where it does not

The 2D path is single_host_likelihood_batch (bayesian_statistics.py:4014); the mass factor is a conditional Gaussian marginalization in the Bishop (2.81–2.82) sense, taken against the same 4×4 Cramér–Rao block Chapter 6 built, with means_4d = [φ, θ, 1, 1] — both the distance and the mass enter as fractions with mean 1.

The candidate search is where the two channels first diverge, and it is load-bearing for everything below. The BallTree query returns a sky-and-redshift ball; a redshift filter alone produces the 1D candidate list (handler.py:592), and a second, mass filter is applied on top of it to produce the 2D list (handler.py:605). So the 2D candidate set is a strict subset of the 1D one, and the 1D channel never sees the mass window at all. That asymmetry is why every mass-side defect in this chapter is 2D-only — and it is also why “why is 1D spared?” turns out to be a screen with no discriminating power for mass hypotheses (§7).

One nuance, because “the 1D channel is mass-blind” is too strong: the rate weight $w_g = R_{\rm eff}(M_g)/(1+z_g)$ of Chapter 3 uses the host mass in both channels. It is a rate, not a density in the mass coordinate, which is exactly why the invariance argument of §6 still goes through untouched README_C8.md, correction 3.

2. The kernel, and the measure it is written in

A galaxy does not hand you $M_g$. It hands you a stellar mass, and the black-hole mass is inferred from it through an empirical scaling relation. The pipeline uses Reines & Volonteri (2015), arXiv:1508.06274: $\log_{10}(M_{\rm BH}/M_\odot) = 7.45 + 1.05\,\log_{10}(M_*/10^{11}M_\odot)$, whose intrinsic scatter is 0.24 dex — and which is fit in log space, on a sample of local broad-line AGN, and then applied to the mostly inactive galaxies of GLADE+.

So the catalogue’s mass is uncertain by a factor of a few. The GW’s mass is uncertain by one part in ten thousand — or one part in ten million, depending on which of the two live values you take. Hold those numbers next to each other, because their ratio is the mechanism of this entire chapter: $\sigma_{M_z}/M_z \approx 10^{-4}$ (claim file) or $8.8\times10^{-8}$ (measured median) against a catalogue $\sigma_{\ln M} \approx 1.28$, a factor $\approx 3.6$ CLAIM_2D_BIAS_20260730.md C4 ch06_FLAGS.md F-ch06-5. Either way, the comparison of a needle with a barn door — and every step below needs only that, which is exactly why the chapter can carry both values honestly instead of choosing.

Two numbers both called “$\sigma_{\ln M}$” — say which The ratified kernel derivation quotes a floor of $\sigma_{\ln M} \ge \sqrt{0.553^2 + 0.184^2} \approx 0.58$ — the irreducible part (intrinsic scatter plus the relation’s $\alpha$-uncertainty) mass_marginal_2d_kernel.md §1. The measured median over the catalogue’s actual host population is $\approx 1.28$, because it also carries the propagated stellar-mass error and the $\beta$-slope term CLAIM_2D_BIAS_20260730.md C4. These are different objects and this book names which one it means every time.
The ratified mass kernel — and the family the pipeline actually shipped RATIFIED CANDIDATE (RATIFY-M6) mass_marginal_2d_kernel.md, gates M1–M7 The derived per-galaxy mass kernel is a truncated lognormal times the EMRI rate, on a flat-in-$\ln M$ reference measure, $$ p_g(M) \;\propto\; \mathrm{LN}\bigl(M;\,M_g,\ \sigma_{\ln M}\bigr)\;R_{\rm eff}(M), \qquad M \in [10^4,\,10^7]\,M_\odot, $$ renormalized on that interval (gates M1–M2). Because the relation is fit in $\log M$, $M_g$ is a median, not a mean; the reference measure is not a technicality (flat-in-$M$ instead would shift the median up by $e^{\sigma_{\ln M}^2} \approx 1.40$ at $\sigma_{\ln M} = 0.58$).

The production pipeline does not use this. It marginalizes with an untruncated linear Gaussian $N(M; M_g,\,M_g\sigma_{\ln M})$. At $\sigma_M/M = 0.6$ that distribution puts 4.8% of every host’s kernel mass at $M < 0$, 29% below $M_{\min}$ for low-mass hosts and 24% above $M_{\max}$ for high-mass ones — and 65% of $R_{\rm eff}$-weighted EMRI hosts sit in the $M_{\max}$ boundary zone. It is the wrong distribution family, not a mis-set width mass_marginal_2d_kernel.md §1–§2.

Gate [RATIFY-M6] is the one that matters for how you read this chapter: the real-data 2D mode is designated CANDIDATE / OPEN — “necessary, not established sufficient” — pending discriminators that have not run.

Now the sieve. The 2D candidate search keeps a galaxy only if its mass interval $[M_g - \sigma_M,\ M_g + \sigma_M]$ overlaps the interval the GW allows, $[(M_z - 2\sigma_{M_z})/(1+z_{\max}),\ (M_z + 2\sigma_{M_z})/(1+z_{\min})]$ handler.py:605. Predict the shape of what that throws away before you look.

Predict, then reveal — the shape of the rejection

The GW side of the comparison is exact to $10^{-4}$ (claim file) or $8.8\times10^{-8}$ (measured median — both are live, see §1 and ch06_FLAGS.md F-ch06-5); the catalogue side is uncertain by a factor $\approx 3.6$. Is the resulting rejection symmetric about the allowed mass, one-sided against galaxies that are too light, or one-sided against galaxies that are too heavy?

Strongly one-sided low, and the reason is not statistical but structural. The window’s width is set almost entirely by the catalogue side, and once $\sigma_M \gtrsim M_g$ the lower edge $M_g - \sigma_M$ goes negative: the upper leg of the test becomes vacuous. A galaxy can always be “too light for this redshift”; it can essentially never be meaningfully too heavy. Measured on the production venue: 193 low-side rejections against 1 high-side CLAIM_2D_BIAS_20260730.md C4. Asymmetric rejection means asymmetric surviving support — which means a tilt, and a tilt in $h$ is a bias.

Panel A is a toy and proves nothing about the pipeline; what it does is make two simultaneous effects visible as one gesture. Drag $\sigma_{\ln M}$ up from 0.1 and watch both counters at once. The rejection stops being two-sided — at $\sigma_{\ln M} \approx 0.9$ the high-side leg starts to vanish, and by the catalogue’s own 1.28 every single rejection is on the low side, because $M_g - \sigma_M$ has gone negative and the upper test can no longer fail. On the real run the pipeline’s own numbers say the same thing twice over. The sieve works: the mass window and kernel reject roughly 97–99% of impostor hosts CLAIM_2D_BIAS_20260730.md. And the surviving dark catalogue legs are suppressed by a median factor $7.78\times10^{-3}$, dragging the dark class’s mean catalogue mixture weight from 0.0354 to 0.0061. Only the first of those was designed.

3. Eddington, in mass this time

Chapter 7 met the Eddington bias in redshift: a wide measurement error against a rising population prior does not blur the answer, it moves it. The mass axis has exactly the same disease, for exactly the same reason, and the project fixed it the same way.

The catalogue’s mass likelihood $N(M; M_g, \sigma_M)$ says how compatible each true mass is with the measurement. It does not say how likely each true mass is to host an EMRI — and that is not flat. The per-MBH EMRI rate $R_{\rm eff}(M)$ falls with mass, about $-0.43$ per dex across the catalogue’s range. Omitting it treats every mass within the scatter as an equally likely host, which they are not.

G2d — the host-mass rate prior RATIFIED G2d_host_mass_rate_prior.md bayesian_statistics.py:500 The correct per-galaxy mass prior carries the rate: $$ p_g(M) \;=\; \frac{N(M;\,M_g,\sigma_M^2)\;R_{\rm eff}(M)}{Z_M}, \qquad Z_M = \int N\,R_{\rm eff}\,dM . $$ Expanding $\ln R_{\rm eff}$ to first order makes this an exactly shifted Gaussian, $M_g^{\rm eff} = M_g\,(1 + \alpha_g\,\sigma_{\rm rel}^2)$ with $\alpha_g = d\ln R_{\rm eff}/d\ln M$ and $\sigma_{\rm rel} = \sigma_M/M_g$ — the same $\sigma^2$ law as the redshift channel, symmetric to volume_deconv.
…and the linearization lies at this width FINDING G2d_host_mass_rate_prior.md §3 That tidy closed form fails at GLADE’s $\sigma_{\rm rel} \approx 1$, near the rate model’s kappa_cap roll-off where $R_{\rm eff}$ stops falling and rises with $M$. There the local slope gets the shift’s sign wrong: at $M_g = 10^5\,M_\odot$, $\sigma_{\rm rel} = 0.55$, the tangent says the effective mass moves down while the exact posterior mean moves up by ~3.7%. The shipped implementation is therefore the exact moment-matched posterior mean by 401-point quadrature, pinned against an independent 20001-point quadrature to better than 0.5% of $M_g$ across both slope-sign regimes and the $\sigma\to0$ limit.

A first-order expansion is not a small error when the function you expanded turns around inside your error bar. This is the chapter’s quietest lesson and its most portable one.
Stale code comment — carried, not propagated FINDING ledger #50 bayesian_statistics.py:2400-2401 The code records this fix’s 2D effect as −0.020 in $h$. That number is from before commit 713fbd1; re-measured afterwards, the effect is −0.0022 — an order of magnitude smaller. The mathematics is as documented; the comment is a fossil. This book quotes the re-measurement, and notes that a stale magnitude in a comment is exactly how a fix’s importance gets mis-remembered.

4. Why only the 2D channel breaks

Here is the fact that has to be explained before anything else makes sense. Campaign #51 — the idealized run, where the host redshift is pinned at the catalogue value — recovered truth in both channels. The mass kernel was just as wrong there as it is here. Why did it not bite?

Eq. (M9) — the mass factor is a second redshift likelihood RATIFIED mass_marginal_2d_kernel.md §3.5 Under the point (δ) host-redshift kernel, $z$ is pinned at $z_g$. The mass factor $mz(z_g;h)$ then varies with $h$ only through the narrow Cramér–Rao cross-correlation $\mu_{\rm cond}(d_L(z_g;h))$, against a fixed $\mu_{\rm gal}(z_g)$ which equals truth by construction. The kernel’s shape is never swept. Family, tilt and truncation enter as a nearly $h$-inert per-host weight — the defect is invisible.

Under a broadened $p_g(z)$ the numerator convolves along the trajectory $M^*(z) = \mu_{\rm cond}(z)\,M_{\det}/(1+z)$: as $z$ sweeps the window, $M^*$ sweeps through the kernel’s shape. In the narrow-GW limit, $$ \ln mz(z) = \ln p_g\bigl(M^*(z)\bigr) - \ln(1+z) + \text{const}, $$ $$ \frac{d\ln mz}{dz} = \Bigl[\tfrac{d\ln p_g}{d\ln M}\Bigr]_{M^*} \Bigl(\tfrac{d\ln\mu_{\rm cond}}{dz} - \tfrac{1}{1+z}\Bigr) - \tfrac{1}{1+z}. $$ $mz(z)$ has become a second likelihood in $z$. A wrong kernel shape is then a multiplicative tilt $\Delta(z)$, which displaces the numerator’s effective redshift by $\delta z \approx \sigma_w^2\,\Delta'(z_g)$ — hence $d_L$, hence $h$. And because mass enters only the 2D channel, the resulting defect is 2D-only.

Read that backwards and it is a design lesson. The idealized campaign was not a weaker test of the mass kernel; it was a test of a different estimator, one in which the kernel’s shape is unreachable. Chapter 7’s realistic redshift kernel is what switched the mass kernel on. Two changes were made at once, and one of them turned the other into an observable — which is also, precisely, why the attribution was confounded, until the 2×2 cell B separated them on 2026-07-31 ⏭ Ch 11 §5. §5 below reports what that control did to this chapter’s own accounting.

Two open first-order caveats on the datum itself OPEN [RATIFY-M7] mass_marginal_2d_kernel.md §3.5 GLADE+ stellar masses are luminosity-derived at a fixed fiducial cosmology, so $M_* \propto d_L^2$ and $\delta \ln M_{\rm BH} \approx 2.1\,\delta\ln d_L$. Two consequences the clean statement above misses:
(a) for photometric hosts the catalogue-$z$ error enters $\ln M_g$ at first order, fully correlated with the redshift kernel — so the $p_g(z)\,p_g(M)$ factorization fails at first order for exactly the host class whose $z$-broadening gives the 2D channel its leverage;
(b) the datum $M_g$ carries a hidden $h$-dependence: across the 0.60–0.86 grid, $\delta \ln M_g$ reaches $\approx 0.4$ — comparable to $\sigma_{\ln M} = 0.58$ itself. The kernel contains no $h$; the number it is centred on does. Both are filed as gates on any real-data 2D promotion, and neither has been closed.

C4-mechanism: refuted as stated, and replaced REFUTED FINDING (amended mechanism) ADJUDICATION_20260730.md §6.3 CLAIM_2D_BIAS_20260730.md C4 Two exact grounds, verified to $6.2\times10^{-13}$ on all 65,108 diagnostic cells.

(1) The completion term contributes exactly zero to the channel difference. Write each event’s likelihood as $p = C\,(1+R)$ with $C = (1-w_G)\mathcal L^{\rm comp}$ and $R = w_G\mathcal L^{\rm cat}/C$. $C$ is channel-common — the completion leg knows nothing about mass — so $\ln C$ cancels identically between the channels. “Falls back on the completion term” cannot appear in the +15.83 as an accounting statement at all.

(2) Deletion is a minority carrier; the survivors carry the rest. On seed61000/real_r1: the 487 deleted events carry +0.24 nats = 1.5% of the +15.83, the 491 events dead in both channels carry exactly 0.00, and the remaining 98.5% (+15.60) is carried by the 534 survivors — the events the sieve kept. The zeroed events’ 1D legs were already negligible, so deleting them cost almost nothing.

That split is configuration-scoped, and this book will not print it as the mechanism’s signature. Run the same partition on the 2×2’s cell B — same estimator, unscattered parent catalogue — and it reads 219 survivors carrying 80.8% (+14.53) against 688 always-zeroed events carrying 19.2% (+3.46), with 605 both-dead at exactly 0.00. The sieve deletes more and de-weights harder without any realized scatter (dark mean catalogue mixture weight 0.0361 → 0.0043, a factor 8.39, against 0.0354 → 0.0061 and 5.79 on r1). 6. The well-posedness bomb

Everything so far has been an argument about bias — about a number being in the wrong place. This section is about something worse and simpler, and it is independent of any bias question: for a stretch, the 2D number is not a measurement of anything at all.

Recall the master equation. Each event’s likelihood is a two-term mixture, and the two terms are added: $$ p_i(h) \;=\; \underbrace{w_G\,\mathcal L^{\rm cat}_i(h)}_{\text{host in the catalogue}} \;+\; \underbrace{(1-w_G)\,\mathcal L^{\rm comp}_i(h)}_{\text{host not in the catalogue}} . $$ For that addition to mean anything, the two terms must be densities in the same variables. In the 1D channel they are. In the 2D channel they are not: the catalogue leg carries the analytic mass factor mz_integral — a density in the mass coordinate, exactly one factor of it — and the completion leg $B^{\rm num}$ carries none at all. Its integrand is $(1-f)\,N(d_{L,\rm frac})\,\sin\theta/4\pi\;dV_c/(1+z)$: sky, distance, volume. No mass.

C8 — the 2D posterior is reparametrization-dependent FINDING CLAIM_2D_BIAS_20260730.md C8 gate_b_20260730/README_C8.md Because exactly one of the two summed terms carries a mass density, rescaling the mass coordinate by a constant $C$ maps $\mathcal L^{\rm cat}_{\rm 2D} \to \mathcal L^{\rm cat}_{\rm 2D}/C$ while leaving $B^{\rm num}$, $\mathcal L^{\rm comp}$, $w_G$, $\beta_G$, $\beta_{\bar G}$ and $D(h)$ untouched — and $C$ does not cancel. Measured on seed61000/real_r1, the 2D MAP walks 0.81329 → 0.78107 → 0.74440 → rails at 0.600 for $C = 1 / 0.3 / 0.1 / \le 0.01$, at a rate $d(\mathrm{MAP})/d\ln C = \mathbf{+0.031}$ per e-fold — 3.1 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹ per e-fold of an arbitrary constant. The 1D channel is bitwise invariant across the whole sweep.

The cause is not what the claim originally said. It is not “a 4D numerator against a 3D selection denominator”: $D(h)$, $\beta_G$, $\beta_{\bar G}$ and $\Sigma_{\rm glob}$ are all mass-dimensionless, and correctly so — a Mandel–Farr–Gair selection probability should be. The mismatch is between the two numerator legs. Mass-marginalizing $D(h)$ could not restore invariance; it has no mass dimension to begin with.

And there is a subtlety that makes this sharper rather than softer. A consistent physical change of unit — rescale $M_g$, $\sigma_{M,g}$, $M_z$, $\sigma_{M_z}$, the $p_{\rm det}$ mass grid and the mass argument of the rate model, all together — is exactly invariant, because mass enters the 2D numerator only through the ratios $M_g(1+z)/M_{z,\det}$ and $\sigma_{M,g}(1+z)/M_{z,\det}$. So “units matter” is the wrong diagnosis. What is broken is the measure the mixture is taken in — and the code has already silently chosen one: $dM_z / M_{z,\det,i}$, the event’s own measured detector-frame mass, which spans $1.33\times10^5$ to $1.63\times10^6\,M_\odot$ across this catalogue, a factor 12. Every event is measured in its own private unit.

Writing $\mathcal L^{\rm cat}_{\rm 2D} \to C^{s}\,\mathcal L^{\rm cat}_{\rm 2D}$ under $M \to CM$, the exponent is $s = -1$ in every normalization mode and both mass-kernel families. The 2D catalogue numerator carries mz_integral, a Gaussian density in the mass-fraction coordinate (the mass_trunc family carries the same object through an explicit push-forward $p_a = p_M\,\det_M/(1+z)$). The measure question has an answer, and it is not “pick a convention”. The invariance-restoring object is the completion leg’s missing mass-data likelihood for a dark host at redshift $z$, $g_i(z) = \int \phi(M)\,N\bigl(M_{z,{\rm obs},i};\,M(1+z),\sigma_{M_z,i}\bigr)\,dM \approx \phi\bigl(M_{z,{\rm obs},i}/(1+z)\bigr)/(1+z)$, with $\phi$ the population’s own source-frame mass prior. With both legs carrying the same mass density, any measure change multiplies $p_i$ by the same event-wise, $h$-independent constant and the MAP is exactly invariant. The pricing of that fix is in the widget’s “has anyone tried to fix this?” box, and it is indicative, not ratified.

7. HB, killed by its own author

One more hypothesis belongs here, because of how it ended. Given everything above, the obvious suspect for the dark class’s $-504.8$-nat tilt is the hard mass window itself — not the smooth kernel but the binary cut in the candidate search. Its bounds depend on $z_{\min}$ and $z_{\max}$, which depend on $h$, so the truncation is $h$-dependent by construction. That is a real mechanism, correctly identified, and it was written up as the one unrefuted candidate.

HB — refuted by the investigator who proposed it REFUTED HANDOFF_20260730.md §4 (historical record only) CLAIM_2D_BIAS_20260730.md, Exonerated Measured, the window’s $h$-tilt is −0.317 nats over $0.73 \to 0.81$ — 0.063% of the 504.8-nat target, and ~50× too small even at its ceiling — and it is sign-inverted: removing the window moves the MAP up by about +0.0015.

Two framing assumptions that had been used to motivate it died with it, and both are more instructive than the hypothesis. (a) “Why is the 1D channel spared?” has no discriminating power for any mass hypothesis — the 1D candidate list never sees the mass window, so every mass mechanism passes that screen for free. (b) “Campaign #51 is a non-control, because at zero scatter no impostor passes the window” is simply false: 153,473 impostors pass in the unscattered case, which makes #51 a stronger exposure, not a weaker one.

The section of the handoff that argued for HB is retained as historical record and is cited here as such. It must never be quoted as the current state.

A hypothesis is not refuted by being implausible. HB was plausible, it was mechanistic, it named a real $h$-dependence in real code — and it was 40× too small and pointed the wrong way. The only thing that settled it was a measurement of the size and sign of the thing it claimed.

8. What this chapter cannot tell you

Assemble the state. The mass channel adds real information, and its sieve works as designed. The kernel it uses is the wrong distribution family, which is CANDIDATE ground by the project’s own designation. The de-weighting mechanism is measured and its budget closes exactly. The 2D posterior is reparametrization-dependent, which is a well-posedness defect independent of any bias question. And the bias is +0.077 with 10/10 runs beyond $2\sigma$.

Every one of those statements is a comparison against “truth” = 0.73. So here is the question this chapter cannot answer.

The failure the next chapter answers Where did 0.73 come from? It came from a simulator: a population drawn from a rate model, waveforms generated, a detection threshold applied, hosts drawn with a rate weight. And a simulator can make a correct estimator look biased, whenever the simulator and the estimator disagree about the same universe. That is not hypothetical: the generator draws hosts mass-aware while the estimator computes its mixture weight $w_G$ mass-blind, and they do not agree. How badly, and what it does to the answer, is Chapter 9’s measurement — note it and move on CLAIM_2D_BIAS_20260730.md C9 ⏭ Ch 9.

Self-check

Q8.1 (transfer)   §2’s mass window rejects one-sided low because once $\sigma_M \gtrsim M_g$ the window’s lower edge $M_g - \sigma_M$ goes negative and the upper leg of the overlap test can no longer fail. Name a source population for which the upper leg would not be vacuous, and say whether the chapter’s tilt survives there.

Show answer
The mechanism first, because it is easy to get wrong. The test is an interval overlap: $[M_g - \sigma_M,\ M_g + \sigma_M]$ against $[(M_z - 2\sigma_{M_z})/(1+z_{\max}),\ (M_z + 2\sigma_{M_z})/(1+z_{\min})]$. It fails high only if $M_g - \sigma_M$ exceeds the GW-allowed upper bound — and with a catalogue proxy whose $\sigma_M$ is of order $M_g$ itself, $M_g - \sigma_M \le 0$, so that condition is unreachable no matter how heavy the galaxy is. (It is not the kinematic inequality $M_z = M(1+z) \ge M$: that bounds the GW-allowed source-frame mass from above, and says nothing about a catalogue galaxy’s own upper test. Measured: 193 low-side rejections against 1 high-side CLAIM_2D_BIAS_20260730.md C4.)

A population that restores the upper leg is any one whose BH masses are measured rather than proxied — hosts with resolved dynamical $M_{\rm BH}$ (the $M$–$\sigma$ calibrators), or reverberation-mapped AGN — where $\sigma_M/M_g$ drops from $\approx 1$ to a few tenths. Then $M_g - \sigma_M > 0$, the test can fail on both sides, and the rejection becomes two-sided. (The mirror-image answer also scores: keep the catalogue and instead degrade the GW side to $\sigma_{M_z}/M_z \sim 1$; the window is then GW-set and again symmetric — for a different reason, and with the sieve doing nothing useful.)

Does the tilt survive? Mostly not — but not because the window became symmetric. §7 measures the hard window’s own $h$-tilt at −0.317 nats, 0.063% of the target and sign-inverted: fixing only the window would move nothing. What carries the effect is the smooth kernel’s de-weighting of the survivors, and that is driven by the same $\sigma_M \gtrsim M_g$ fact. A population with a sharp mass proxy kills both at once — two-sided rejection and survivors that keep their weight. Which is the real lesson: the one-sidedness and the de-weighting are not two problems, they are one number ($\sigma_M/M_g$) seen twice. What would not be fixed is §6’s well-posedness defect: the measure mismatch between the two numerator legs is independent of how good the proxy is.

Defect Museum · interlude

mass_trunc — right in isolation, irrelevant in the pipeline EXONERATED

The hypothesis
§2’s defect is real: the production mass kernel is an untruncated linear Gaussian where the derivation says truncated lognormal × $R_{\rm eff}$. Replace it, and the 2D residual should close. This was not a guess — it was the ratified kernel, implemented properly, with Gauss–Hermite on the narrow GW mass peak in the numerator and Gauss–Legendre-in-$\ln M$ over a peak-aware window in the selection integral.
The pre-registered prediction, and the isolation test
A single-host toy — numerator only, no selection term — confirmed it: production minus correct came out at +0.016…+0.02 at $\sigma_z/z = 0.5$–0.65, sign HIGH, ~3σ, growing monotonically with photo-z leverage across all five tested leverages ledger #71. By any normal standard that is a confirmed driver.
The measured outcome, in the full pipeline
A pre-registered A/B on the seed600 494-event shallow venue: Δ2D = +0.0029 — the wrong sign, an order of magnitude smaller than the toy, and Δ1D = 0.0000 exactly ledger #72 mass_trunc_ab_20260713/FINDING.md. The kernel change was numerically clean — deterministic, no rail, MAP unmoved at 0.76 — and simply did not do what the toy said it would.
Why
The in-catalogue likelihood is a ratio, $\mathcal L^{\rm cat} = \sum_g w_g N_g / \sum_g w_g D_g$, and the very same mass prior enters $D_g$ that enters $N_g$. A shape change moves numerator and denominator in the same direction, so most of it cancels. What survives is only the part reweighted differently by $p_{\rm det}$ inside $D_g$ — and that residue points the other way. The toy had no denominator, so it measured a different estimator.
What it cost, and what it did not buy
One implementation cycle plus a cluster A/B. It did not buy an explanation of the 2D residual. It did buy the exactly-zero 1D delta, which is a plumbing gate worth keeping: a mass-only change that moved 1D would have been a bug report. Note the venue scope — this A/B and volume_trunc’s share the same 494-event subsample, so “exonerated” here means “exonerated on that venue”.
The transferable lesson
The selection denominator is not a spectator. An isolated toy can be right about the physics and irrelevant to the number you care about, because the estimator you ship is a ratio and the ratio is what cancels. Before believing a numerator-only experiment, ask what the denominator does with the same change.
