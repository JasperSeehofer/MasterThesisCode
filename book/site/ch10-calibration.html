Chapter 10 — Is It Calibrated?

It recovers truth at −0.24σ, twice. What does that establish?

The failure this chapter answers Chapter 9 built the universe the estimator is graded against, and then ran the estimator on it. On the idealized campaign-#51 mock the combined posterior over 1588 events peaks at h = 0.72990 with $\sigma_h$ = 0.00030 — the injected truth 0.73 sits −0.24σ from the peak. A second, independent seed lands at 0.72990 too, −0.36σ. IDEALIZED_BASELINE_READOUT.md:25-30 That is a recovery to four decimal places, twice. So: is the estimator calibrated?

Predict, then reveal — commit before you read on

Two independent synthetic universes. Two MAPs on truth to four decimals. Two sub-σ pulls, both small. Answer in one click, then keep reading; the chapter is an unpacking of your answer either way.

“No” and “I cannot tell” are the same answer, and both are right. Nothing in those two numbers is a statement about calibration. Calibration is a property of an estimator over many universes: it says that when the estimator reports a 68% interval, truth is inside it 68% of the time. A MAP is a single point from a single draw; two of them cannot resolve a bias smaller than the scatter — and note that both pulls are negative, which the readout itself flags as worth re-testing.

Worse: those two numbers cannot even tell you which parts of the estimator were tested. That turns out to be the more interesting failure, and it has a measurement attached. IDEALIZED_BASELINE_READOUT.md:31-36

It recovers truth. That is not the same as being right. This chapter is about instruments. Not about the estimator — the estimator is now built, and Chapters 3 through 9 each installed one piece of it. This chapter asks the question a referee asks: how do you know? Four answers exist in this project, they are of genuinely different strengths, and one of them is the methodological jewel of the whole repository.

1. Coverage is the currency

Chapter 2 gave you three independent ways for an estimator to be wrong: bias (the answer sits off truth on average), scatter (the answers spread), and coverage (the stated uncertainty is dishonest). Only the third is a statement about the error bar, and only the third is what a reader of your paper is implicitly trusting.

Coverage is a frequentist audit of a Bayesian object, and it is defined by a procedure, not by a formula. Build many synthetic universes, each with a known injected $h_{\rm true}$. Run the estimator on each. For each posterior, form the credible region of mass $q$ and ask whether truth is inside it. If the estimator is honest, truth falls inside the $q$ region a fraction $q$ of the time — for every $q$ at once. Plotting measured fraction against nominal $q$ gives a P–P plot, and calibration is the diagonal.

The instrument, and why it is trustworthy validation/pp_coverage.py commission 2026-07-01, investigator d2

The harness builds a synthetic galaxy catalogue and EMRI detections in a flat ΛCDM universe, runs a from-scratch single-host dark-siren $H_0$ estimator with a switchable host-redshift kernel, and measures P–P coverage at the 50 / 68 / 90% highest-posterior-density levels together with MAP bias, across many realizations and several injected truths.

Its scientific value is in what it refuses to do. The module is pure numpy/scipy and deliberately does not import the production inference code: it re-derives the estimator from the written formulas, “so a calibration failure here cannot be explained away as a shared implementation bug.” It was written from scratch by the 2026-07-01 verification commission (investigator d2) — by someone whose job was to break the pipeline, not to ship it.

For a formal HPD region there is a compact way to say the same thing. For one realization, let $p(h)$ be the normalized posterior over $h$ and define

The credible level at which truth enters pp_coverage.py _hpd_contains $$ L \;=\; \int_{\{h\,:\;p(h)\,\ge\,p(h_{\rm true})\}} p(h)\,dh , $$ the posterior mass sitting above the density at truth. The HPD region of mass $q$ is exactly $\{p \ge t_q\}$ with $\int_{p\ge t_q} p = q$, so $$ h_{\rm true} \in \mathrm{HPD}_q \iff L \le q . $$ Coverage at level $q$ is therefore the empirical CDF of $L$ over realizations, and the P–P curve is that CDF. A calibrated estimator produces $L$ uniform on $[0,1]$.

The harness stores coverage at three levels; the widget below evaluates $L$ per realization and draws the whole curve. Every cell it shows is re-run from that cell’s own archived configuration — same seed, same random stream — and the recomputation is required to reproduce the archived coverage, bias, rail fraction and completion fraction exactly before anything is written. All 16 cells × 3 truths pass. That is a small point worth pausing on: the July archives still reproduce, bit for bit, from today’s code.

The signature that separates a bias from noise. Suppose your coverage is poor. Is that a real systematic offset, or is it finite-sample skew that will wash out with more events? There is a clean discriminator: run the same estimator at increasing event count. A finite-sample artifact shrinks like $1/\sqrt{N}$ and coverage returns to nominal. A real asymptotic bias does the opposite — the offset stays put while the posterior narrows around it, so coverage degrades.

Read from pp_coverage_noisemodel_20260711/SUMMARY.md:80-86 and the archives behind it. At $h_{\rm true} = 0.72$ the const-$\sigma$ estimator’s bias is flat in $N$ (+0.0023 → +0.0024 → +0.0022) while 68% coverage runs 0.63 → 0.38 → 0.12. That is a real asymptotic model bias, diagnosed without ever knowing its cause. (The cause, as it happens, was an inconsistent noise model in the likelihood; correcting it takes the bias to $\le$ +0.0008 and restores coverage at campaign scale, with a small residual that only surfaces at $N = 4000$. ledger #67)

2. The instruments, ranked by what they can refuse

Coverage is one instrument. This project has five more, and they are not interchangeable. Ranked by how hard they are to argue with:

2b. Closure at a different truth

A mock whose injected truth is 0.73 and an estimator that returns 0.73 is a suspicious pairing: 0.73 is also the value baked into constants.py, so a tuned estimator would pass. The counter-test is to inject a truth the estimator has never seen and demand that it follow. This project ran two.

  • h = 0.65, in the era of ledger #20. It failed usefully: the closure came back at 0.6708, a +5.62σ pull. The cause was the selection denominator being applied twice — inside the per-event likelihood and again as an outer $-N\log D$ term ⏮ Ch 4. Removing the duplicate moved the closure to 0.6555, a pull of +1.67σ, and eliminated a +0.020…+0.025 shift in $h$ ledger #20 6754ddb.
  • h = 0.67, on the current stack: MAP 0.670 on 1343 events, zero excluded; a parabola on the three central bins gives $\sigma_h$ = 4.42e−4 and a peak at 0.670053, i.e. +0.12σ from truth — against the two baselines’ −0.24σ and −0.36σ HANDOFF_20260730.md:15-23. The handoff carries its own caveats and so does this book: the fitted bins sit 11.3σ from the peak, so the $\sigma$ is indicative rather than a substitute for a zoom grid, and the GPU array ended in a 100/100 wall-clock timeout, yielding 1345 detections against the baselines’ 1590. The deficit cannot now be split between truncation and a genuine detection loss at $h = 0.67$.

The combination is $\sum_i \ln \mathcal{L}_i(h)$, so deleting event $i$ subtracts one smooth curve from a sum of 1588. Near the peak each in-catalogue golden event contributes an approximately quadratic $\ln \mathcal{L}_i$ centred close to truth — the idealization audit shows the per-event curvature matches the analytic $I_e = (h\,\sigma_{d_L}/d_L)^{-2}$ prediction for “$z$ known exactly, GW distance error only” to within ~5%, event by event. Subtracting a centred quadratic removes precision without moving the vertex. The vertex only moves once the surviving sum is no longer concave, which is what happens between $K = 30$ and $K = 40$: the dark class’s small negative curvature (−3.0 in total) stops being negligible against what is left, the sum turns convex at truth, and the MAP is driven to whichever edge the residual monotone tilt prefers.

3. What the mock cannot see

Return to the cold open. The idealized baseline quotes $\sigma_{H_0}$ = 0.032 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹. Put that beside the numbers Chapter 0 opened with: the two measurements whose disagreement started this book differ by about 5 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹, and each carries an uncertainty near 1. A 0.032 would not arbitrate the Hubble tension; it would end the subject.

It is also not a forecast, and the project says so in its own words rather than being caught at it. When the author asked directly whether the setup was realistic, the answer was an audit.

The idealization ledger — the honest sentence, verbatim FINDING idealization_audit/IDEALIZATION_LEDGER.md

“Verdict up front: NO — this is not the full realistic setup.” The catalogue’s bad mass and redshift measurements are stored honestly, but on the production path they are never realized as noise and (for redshift) not even used as a width. “The quoted precision is the precision of a universe in which every GLADE+ catalogue redshift is exact.”

And the pair that produces it is internally consistent, which is the subtle part: I1 injects the catalogue redshift verbatim as the event’s true redshift, and I2 has the inference point-evaluate its numerator at that same redshift. Generator and estimator agree because they were handed the same number. The audit’s own framing: this is “a valid consistency baseline, not a forecast” — quote it “only as generator↔evaluation consistency at the 0.0003-in-$h$ level, never as a LISA $H_0$ forecast.”

4. C11 — an exoneration done right

So far the harness has been used to find defects. Its most valuable use in this project was the opposite: ruling a suspect out, quantitatively.

Chapter 8 left the 2D channel biased by +0.077 in $h$, on ground the project itself designates a CANDIDATE pairing mass_marginal_2d_kernel.md:1-17, 690-706. Chapter 5’s completion branch is an obvious suspect: it is the leg that handles the 95% of events with no catalogued host, and in the coverage harness — where the completion branch is the bare $B^{\rm num}$/$D$ limit and nothing else — ensembles dominated by that branch were already measured to bias high, by +0.7% to +5.4% in $h$ at completion fractions 0.22–0.85 ledger #57 pp_coverage_deepvenue_20260710/SUMMARY.md. (That is a statement about the harness’s branch, not about the production mixture’s budget, where the up-pull is carried by the $(1-w_G)$ prefactor ⏮ Ch 8.) Does the completion leg own the +0.077?

The wrong way to answer is to argue. The right way is to put the harness at the campaign’s own operating point — completion fraction around the delivered $w_G \approx 0.12$ — and measure how much bias the completion leg can produce there. That is the ladder you dragged in I10.1, and its answer is an order-of-magnitude elimination you can do in your head.

Read that caveat twice, because it is the most useful sentence in the block. The harness has no host-mass dimension: it cannot form the 2D likelihood at all. So what has actually been established is a bound on the completion leg’s 1D calibration at the right completeness, extrapolated to a channel the instrument cannot see. The elimination is sound and the scope is narrow, and C11 says both. The most useful thing a harness can tell you is often the scope of its own answer.

What C11 is, as a piece of scientific work, is worth naming. Compare that with the museum downstairs, which is full of suspects eliminated by assertion and later found to be innocent for reasons nobody had checked.

Q10.2   What does a P–P plot show that a bias measurement does not, and construct an estimator that is unbiased and badly mis-calibrated.

Show answer
A P–P plot shows whether the quantiles of the posterior are honest: over many synthetic universes, the fraction of times truth falls below the posterior’s $q$-th quantile should be $q$. An estimator that returns the correct mean but a $\sigma$ half the true scatter is unbiased and catastrophically mis-calibrated — its 68% intervals contain truth ~38% of the time. The project measured exactly this shape in the pure-completion regime, with 68% coverage falling to 0.27, and in the noise-model study where coverage went 0.63 → 0.38 → 0.12 as $N$ grew — the signature of a real asymptotic bias, since a purely noisy estimator’s coverage does not degrade with $N$ pp_coverage_noisemodel_20260711/SUMMARY.md:80-86.

Q10.4   Why is “coverage measured on synthetic universes” not circular, given that the synthetic universes come from the same population model the estimator assumes?

Show answer
It is not circular for the failure modes it can see — kernel mis-specification, selection mis-scoping, quadrature, bookkeeping — because those break even when the population model is shared. It is blind to a mis-specified population model, since both sides would be wrong identically. That is why the project separately tests generator–estimator agreement ⏮ Ch 9 and why C9 could only be found by comparing the model’s $w_G$ to the realized rate rather than by any coverage test.

Defect Museum · interlude

The $H_0$-independent estimator REFUTED

The hypothesis
By July 2026 the production estimator railed at $h = 0.86$ on photometric-host data, and the standing explanation was information starvation: photo-$z$ hosts are simply too uninformative, the posterior is wide and prior-dominated, and the rail is where a weak likelihood ends up. Plausible, and flattering — it makes the failure a property of nature.
The decisive test
The 2026-07-01 verification commission ran a tournament injection scan: the same estimator, on synthetic data generated at ten different injected truths spanning $H_0$ from 0.63 to 0.77. An estimator that is merely imprecise still moves with the truth. An estimator that does not move is not measuring the parameter at all.
The measured outcome
The production MAP came back at 0.86 for every single injected truth, 0.63 through 0.77 — while the local catalog_only normalization on the same data tracked truth exactly, 0.63→0.63 … 0.77→0.77. ledger #49a synthesis/DRAFT_REPORT.md:24-27 synthesis/WF2_DIGEST.md:26-30 The rail was not weak information. It was a mis-normalized selection term reporting where it points, and it was curable — a correctly specified estimator recovers the injected $H_0$ to 1–2% on the same $\sigma_z \approx 0.035$ data.
What it cost, and what it bought
It retired a formally recorded conclusion, and it rewrote the direction of the whole investigation: from “the data cannot support this” to “find the inconsistency.” The nuance was preserved rather than discarded — photometric redshifts do genuinely degrade $H_0$ precision; wide is real. But wide is not railed.
The transferable lesson
An estimator can be precise, stable, perfectly reproducible — and carry zero information about the parameter. Every reproducibility check that estimator would have passed, it did pass. The one test it could not survive was the one that varied the thing it claimed to measure. If you have an inference and you have never injected a different truth into it, you do not yet know whether it is an instrument or a constant.
