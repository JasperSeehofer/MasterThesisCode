1. What a catalogue redshift actually is

A galaxy catalogue does not store redshifts. It stores measurements of redshifts, and it stores them in two very different qualities. A spectroscopic redshift comes from identified emission or absorption lines and is precise to a few parts in ten thousand. A photometric redshift is a regression: broadband fluxes in a handful of filters, fitted against galaxy templates, producing an estimate whose fractional error is measured in tens of percent. GLADE+ Dálya et al. 2022, arXiv:2110.06184 is overwhelmingly the second kind: spectroscopic hosts are 0.56% of the catalogue ledger #42F4_SPECZ_DECOMPOSITION.md:8-15.

So for galaxy $g$ the catalogue hands you a pair: a central value $z_g$ and a width $\sigma_z$. The object those two numbers define is

$$ p(z_g \mid z) \;=\; \mathcal N\!\left(z_g;\, z,\, \sigma_z\right), $$

read: “if the true redshift were $z$, this is how probable the catalogue’s reported value $z_g$ would have been.” That is a likelihood. It is a statement about the measurement, given the truth. What every candidate-host sum in Chapter 3 needs is the other direction — given the reported value, where is the galaxy really? — and those are not the same object.

Why the mistake is so easy to make Because a Gaussian is symmetric in $(z_g - z)$, the expression above, read as a function of $z$ at fixed $z_g$, is numerically $\mathcal N(z; z_g, \sigma_z)$ — the same curve. Nothing in the arithmetic warns you. That is exactly what scipy.stats.norm(loc=host_z, scale=host_z_error).pdf(z) evaluates in the code, and it is correct as a likelihood and wrong as a weight G2b §1.1.

2. The Eddington shift, and why it is a shift

To turn the likelihood into a weight you need a prior: where were the hosts, before anybody measured this one? EMRI hosts are not spread uniformly in $z$. They are drawn from an astrophysical event rate, and the number of events per unit detector time per unit true redshift is

From the ratified derivation RATIFIED G2b §1.1, Eqs. (1.1)–(1.3) $$\frac{dN}{dt_{\rm det}\,dz} = \mathcal R(z)\,\frac{dV_c}{dz}\,\frac{1}{1+z} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \underbrace{w_{\rm pop}(z)}_{\text{the population weight}} \;=\; \frac{1}{1+z}\frac{dV_c}{dz}.$$ Two factors, two physical facts. $dV_c/dz$ is comoving volume per unit redshift, in Mpc³ sr⁻¹ Hogg 1999, arXiv:astro-ph/9905116, Eq. (28): there is simply more universe at larger $z$. The $1/(1+z)$ is cosmological time dilation — a clock at redshift $z$ ticks $(1+z)$ times slower as seen by the detector, so a source-frame rate is suppressed by exactly one such factor when counted in detector time Babak et al. 2017, arXiv:1703.09722, Sec. III.4. Under the module’s $p_0 = 1$ surrogate the intrinsic rate is $z$-independent, so after marginalising mass this is the $z$-shape of the population prior emri_rate.py:296-331.

Now the consequence. $w_{\rm pop}$ rises steeply with $z$ — at low redshift roughly as $z^2$. A galaxy observed at $z_g$ is therefore more probably a higher-$z$ galaxy scattered down than a lower-$z$ galaxy scattered up, simply because there were more of the former to begin with. This is Eddington’s 1913 argument, transcribed from stellar magnitudes to redshift. Expanding $\ln w_{\rm pop}$ about $z_g$ and completing the square:

From the ratified derivation RATIFIED G2b §2.1, Eqs. (2.1)–(2.2) $$\langle z\rangle = z_g + \sigma_z^2\,s(z_g) + \mathcal O(\sigma_z^4), \qquad s(z) \equiv \frac{d\ln w_{\rm pop}}{dz} = \frac{2}{I(z)E(z)} - \frac{E'(z)}{E(z)} - \frac{1}{1+z},$$ with $I(z) = \int_0^z dz'/E(z')$ and $E(z) = \sqrt{\Omega_m(1+z)^3+\Omega_\Lambda}$. At low $z$, $I \approx z$ and $s \approx 2/z$ — the shift is largest exactly where the GLADE-like hosts sit. The bare Gaussian omits the $+\sigma_z^2 s$ term, so it systematically under-estimates every host’s redshift.

Under-estimating $z$ at a fixed measured distance under-estimates $h$. The mapping is exact and worth carrying, because the whole rest of the chapter uses it: write $d_L(z;h) = f(z)/h$ with $f(z) = (1+z)I(z)\,c/100$ independent of $h$. An estimator that matches the same measured $d_L$ with an assumed host redshift $\hat z$ when the truth is $z_t$ lands at

$$ \frac{h_{\rm eff}}{h_{\rm true}} \;=\; \frac{f(\hat z)}{f(z_t)} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \Delta h \;\simeq\; -\,h_{\rm true}\left.\frac{d\ln f}{dz}\right|_{\bar z}\sigma_z^2\,s(\bar z) \;\equiv\; -\,C(\bar z)\,\sigma_z^2 . $$

The exact ratio form is stated at C7_README §5; the linearised $-C\sigma_z^2$ form at G2b §2.2, Eq. (2.3), with $d\ln f/dz = 1/(1+z) + 1/(IE)$. The widget evaluates the exact form and plots the linearised one beside it, because at real GLADE redshifts they visibly part company — see the last row of the table below.

3. The volume kernel: volume_deconv

The repair is the thing you already know how to write down. Bayes, applied to the pair the catalogue gave you:

From the ratified derivation RATIFIED G2b §1.2, Eq. (1.4) bayesian_statistics.py:3712 galaxy_redshift_prior_pdf $$p_g(z) \;=\; \frac{\mathcal N(z_g;\,z,\,\sigma_z)\;w_{\rm pop}(z)}{Z_g}, \qquad Z_g = \int dz'\;\mathcal N(z_g;\,z',\,\sigma_z)\;w_{\rm pop}(z').$$ $Z_g$ is the per-galaxy normalisation, in Mpc³ sr⁻¹, computed by 50-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature over $[z_g - 4\sigma_z,\,z_g + 4\sigma_z]$ — the same interval, and therefore the same nodes, that the per-galaxy selection integral $D_g$ uses G2b §3.1. $[\mathcal N] = z^{-1}$ and $[w_{\rm pop}] = \mathrm{Mpc^3\,sr^{-1}}$, so $p_g$ has units $z^{-1}$ and unit integral: a proper probability density in $z$.

This is the mode the pipeline calls volume_deconv, and it is what the chapter’s stage-2 button switched on. Two of its properties are load-bearing and neither is obvious.

(i) It is exactly $h$-independent. Writing $dV_c/dz = (c/100h)^3 I^2(z)/E(z)$ gives $w_{\rm pop}(z;h) = h^{-3}g(z)$, and the $h^{-3}$ cancels identically between the numerator of $p_g$ and $Z_g$. Passing the trial $h$ into the volume element is therefore harmless — the kernel injects no spurious $h$-dependence into $\mathcal L^{\rm cat}$; only the shape of $w_{\rm pop}$ acts. Measured: $Z_g \propto h^{-3}$ to $10^{-15}$ EXONERATED ledger #75, re-measured at $9.1\times10^{-16}$ in Gate B C7_README §Correctness checks 2.

(ii) It reduces to the point kernel as $\sigma_z\to0$. $\mathcal N \to \delta(z-z_g)$, so $Z_g \to w_{\rm pop}(z_g)$ and $p_g(z) \to \delta(z-z_g)$: the prior becomes irrelevant exactly where the likelihood has no width. A prior can only matter where the measurement does not G2b §1.2, limiting case.

5. The point kernel and its licence

There is a third kernel in the code, and for one specific purpose it is not an approximation at all — it is exact. The point (δ) kernel simply evaluates the numerator at the catalogue redshift: $N_g = \lim_{\sigma\to0} N_g(\sigma)$. If the simulator draws each host at its catalogue redshift verbatim, then the catalogue value is the truth, the width is zero by construction, and the δ-kernel is the generator-exact answer. That was the arrangement of this project’s idealised baseline.

From the ratified derivation RATIFIED realistic_host_observation_model.md §3.1 (RATIFY-R3) Under a scattered catalogue that premise is false by design: given the observed row $(z_{{\rm obs},g}, \sigma_g)$, the truth is distributed as $p(z_{\rm true}\mid z_{{\rm obs},g}) \propto \mathcal N(z_{{\rm obs},g}; z_{\rm true},\sigma_g)\,p_{\rm pop}(z_{\rm true})$ — a width-$\sigma_g$ object. Point-evaluating at $z_{{\rm obs},g}$ is a model error of order $\sigma_g\,d\ln d_L/dz$ relative to the GW width — for the golden events a factor 25–110 — and, worse, it is inconsistent: the realised data generating process is exactly the marginal the width kernel computes. “The δ-kernel is not ‘optimistic’ under scatter, it is wrong.”

This is the single most transferable epistemic point in the book, so it is worth saying slowly. The δ-kernel is correct for the mock and wrong for real data, and the thing that decides which is not a property of the kernel — it is a property of how the universe you are testing against was built. A kernel does not have a validity; a kernel-plus-generator pair does. And the leverage is enormous: in the three-way per-leg A/B the δ-kernel carries 85.3% (1D) / 86.7% (2D) of the total ln movement ledger #88THREEWAY_AB_READOUT.md:19-56. Nearly the whole answer sits on this one choice.

Read that 86.7% next to the 2×2 cell B’s 72% (§6) with the scoping in hand, because they are not the same decomposition and they do not disagree. Ledger #88 isolates one leg — the host-z kernel alone — on the seed1000 deep venue. The 2×2 cell B swaps the whole estimator configuration (kernel and normalisation together) on this campaign’s venue, and finds it owns +0.060 of the +0.083 total 2D displacement. One number says how much of a per-leg A/B rides on the kernel; the other says how much of the campaign’s 2D displacement rides on the estimator. Note also that ledger #88 calls its own leg “Cell B” — a different object on a different venue, which BIAS_HISTORY_LEDGER.md §3 relabels A′ museum ledger #88.

Which is also why the campaign that this book’s numbers come from runs volume_deconv, and why the code will now refuse a point-resolving host-z kernel whenever the loaded catalogue is a scattered realisation — the guard is one-directional, since running the unscattered baseline with the width kernel is merely conservative realistic_host_observation_model.md §3.4.

Trap 7.B — “Deconvolution is the safe, principled choice, so more of it is better.” The same operation that removes a −0.023 bias at $\sigma_z = 0.035$ creates a +16–49% inflation at $\sigma_z/z \approx 0.25$–$0.49$ G2b:229-237CLAIM C7. Principled operations have domains of validity. The domain here is set by whether the weight you deconvolve against is the population prior (which G2b establishes it is) or the selected population prior (which C7 measures it is not) — and which of those the numerator should carry is precisely what the 2×2 cell B was pre-registered to decide, and did, on 2026-07-31.

6. The twist: the fix and its failure mode are the same knob

Stage 3 asks a question the reader of Q7.4 has already been set up for. You have a deconvolution that removes an over-correction low. Push it: what happens when you deconvolve a very wide photo-z against a monotonically rising volume prior with no selection turnover at all?

Nothing penalises pushing the host to higher $z$. Nothing has to. The prior rises without limit over the integration window, the likelihood is wide enough to reach the rising part, and the deconvolved estimate inflates. The project measured this on its own numerator, host by host.

Adjudicated finding FINDING CLAIM_2D_BIAS_20260730.md C7 (MEASURED, adjudicated 2026-07-30) C7_README.md §1

bayesian_statistics.py:4201-4207 weights the host-z numerator kernel by $w_{\rm pop} = (dV_c/dz)/(1+z)$ — the cosmic prior — with no $p_{\rm det}$ and no catalogue selection $\varphi_{\rm cat}$. The induced inflation, measured by rebuilding the code’s own numerator for the 76 in-catalogue hosts, obeys

$$\frac{h_{\rm eff}}{h_{\rm true}} \;=\; \frac{1 + \sqrt{1 + 12\,(\sigma_z/z)^2}}{2} \;\longrightarrow\; 1 + 3\,(\sigma_z/z)^2 .$$

Here $\varphi_{\rm cat}$ is the catalogue’s own selected number density — the prior over “a row in a flux-limited catalogue lies at redshift $z$”, as against $w_{\rm pop}$’s prior over “an EMRI host lies at redshift $z$”. With a selection function $f(z,\Omega)$ (the fraction of galaxies at $(z,\Omega)$ the survey actually lists) it is $\varphi_{\rm cat}(z) \propto f(z,\Omega)\,(dV_c/dz)/(1+z)$, and for the numerator of a detected event it should carry $p_{\rm det}$ on top. The bare $w_{\rm pop}$ rises without limit; $\varphi_{\rm cat}$ turns over, because surveys run out of galaxies before the universe runs out of volume.

Worth knowing where this sits relative to everyone else: the standard-practice dark-siren kernels do not deconvolve host redshifts against a cosmic volume prior at all — they take the catalogue’s redshift and its error at face value Laghi et al. 2021, arXiv:2102.01708Turski et al. 2023, arXiv:2302.12037gwcosmo, the reference implementation of Gray Eq. (A10). Deconvolving is this project’s own deviation — D8, “no Gray equation prescribes this” BOOK_SOURCES_MAP §K3 / G2c §4.3 — which is precisely why it is the thing under test rather than a settled background assumption. Naming the two priors names the axis a resolution will lie on; it does not resolve C7, and this chapter does not.

The extra 3 (rather than 2) comes from the numerator window’s width being proportional to $z$ — the GW kernel has a fixed fractional distance width, so $d\ln N_g/dz$ picks up $+1/z$ from the window on top of $+2/z$ from the volume weight. The point kernel has no window and therefore no $+1/z$ C7_README §1. The claim’s own earlier $[1+\sqrt{1+8\varepsilon^2}]/2$ form understates by 1.35–1.5× and is superseded. At $\sigma_z/z = 0.25$–$0.49$ the inflation is +16% to +49%, $h_{\rm eff} = 0.85$–$1.11$. Rail threshold: $\sigma_z/z > 0.256$.

Three things make this a measurement rather than a prediction that happens to match, and each is worth knowing how to demand:

  • The driver is the code. The numerator was rebuilt exactly as bayesian_statistics.py:4099-4245 computes it, validated against scipy.integrate.fixed_quad on the same integrand at relative difference 0.0e0, with kernel $h$-invariance $9.1\times10^{-16}$ and $n=50$ vs $n=400$ agreement below $5\times10^{-12}$ — no quadrature aliasing, unlike the exhibit at the end of this chapter C7_README §Correctness checks.
  • The $\sigma_z\to0$ gate passes. The shift vanishes as $(\sigma_z/z)^2$: log–log slope 1.99 over the last decade, with the coefficient converging to 3. Whatever a fix does, it cannot disturb the idealised baseline C7_README §2.
  • It was confronted with the delivered data. The observed in-catalogue ball-numerator tilt over $0.73\to0.86$ is a median +0.308 nats, 93.2% positive (IQR [+0.208, +0.413]) against a predicted +0.33…+0.39 at $\sigma_z/z = 0.35$–$0.65$ — and against −408 nats, 0% positive for the point kernel. The production data therefore independently implies $\sigma_z/z \approx 0.35$–$0.6$ for these hosts, so the verdict does not rest on the stale local column C7_README §3.

That last number was re-derived from scratch for this chapter: taking $\ln \mathcal L^{\rm cat}_i$ at $h = 0.86$ and $h = 0.73$ straight out of real_r1/diagnostics/event_likelihoods.csv and adding $\Delta\ln\Sigma_{\rm glob} = +0.027597$ reproduces the stored per-event tilts to $7.2\times10^{-16}$ (gate G4 in gen_ch07.py). $\Sigma_{\rm glob}$ is an event-independent normalisation identical for both kernels, which is why it cancels out of the kernel comparison; where it comes from is Chapter 9 ⏭ Ch 9.

Self-check

Q7.1 (transfer)   You measured $\Delta h = -C\sigma_z^2$ with $C_{\rm meas} \approx 17$–$20$. Without re-reading §2: what feature of the volume prior sets the sign, and what would have to be true of the prior for a symmetric widening to have been the right answer?

Show answer
The sign is set by the slope of the prior across the width of the likelihood — nothing else. $s(z) = d\ln w_{\rm pop}/dz$ is positive (at low $z$, $s \approx 2/z$), so more of the probability that scattered into $z_g$ came from above than from below; the posterior mean sits at $z_g + \sigma_z^2 s$, the bare Gaussian omits that term, and under-estimating $z$ at a fixed measured $d_L$ under-estimates $h$. Two consequences you can state without the chapter: the effect is second order in $\sigma_z$ (the first-order terms cancel by symmetry of the likelihood), and it does not depend on the likelihood being Gaussian — only on the prior having a non-zero log-slope across the smear.

A symmetric widening would be right if — and essentially only if — the prior were flat over the width of the kernel: $s = 0$ makes the shift vanish identically and leaves nothing but variance. That is the tacit assumption every “plug in $z_g$, inflate the error bar” estimator makes. It is false here by orders of magnitude, and the diagnostic that tells you so is coverage, not width ledger #47. A flat prior would also make the sign ambiguous rather than low — which is the sharper way to say it: this is not a fact about redshift errors, it is a fact about where the galaxies were.

Q7.2   Why is $p(z_g\mid z)$ “not a posterior”, and what exactly does the volume prior supply?

Show answer
$p(z_g\mid z)$ is a likelihood — a statement about the measurement given the truth. Read as a function of $z$ at fixed $z_g$ it is numerically a Gaussian in $z$, and that numerical coincidence is what makes the mistake so easy. To weight candidate redshifts you need $p(z \mid z_g) \propto p(z_g\mid z)\,p(z)$, and the prior $p(z)$ is the rate of EMRIs per unit true redshift, $\propto (dV_c/dz)/(1+z)$: comoving volume per unit redshift, with a time-dilation factor converting source-frame rate to detector frame. That is the volume_deconv kernel, and G2b proves it is the unique weight consistent with this project’s own rate model G2b §1.1–1.2.

Q7.3   Why must the same volume prior appear in the per-galaxy denominator as well as the numerator, and what is the name of the invariant that enforces it?

Show answer
Because the numerator and denominator are integrals over the same true-$z$ variable with the same measure; if the prior appeared in only one, the ratio would carry a spurious $dV_c$ and become $h$-dependent for a purely bookkeeping reason. G2b calls this “$dV_c$ counted once” and verifies it explicitly, including the exact $h$-invariance of the per-galaxy normalisation $Z_g \propto h^{-3}$ to 1e−15. The project protects it with a binding regression gate — which is why any future fix to this kernel must explicitly supersede G2b rather than quietly contradict it G2b §3.2, §3.1ledger #75.

Q7.4   The deconvolution fixes an over-correction low. Now push it: what happens if you deconvolve a very wide photo-z against a monotonically rising volume prior with no selection turnover?

Show answer
You over-correct the other way. With no $p_{\rm det}$ and no catalogue selection in the weight, nothing penalises pushing the host to higher $z$, so the deconvolved estimate inflates. The measured law on this code is $h_{\rm eff}/h_{\rm true} = \frac12[1+\sqrt{1+12(\sigma_z/z)^2}] \to 3(\sigma_z/z)^2$, with a rail threshold at $\sigma_z/z > 0.256$; the campaign’s in-catalogue hosts sit at 0.25–0.49, predicting +16% to +49% inflation — and 58% of them do in fact rail at the top of the prior. Read that last number with its binding amendment: per event the rail is cosmetic (median 0.072–0.134 nats = 0.30–0.47 $\sigma_{\rm event}$; only 0–1.3% of events exceed 1σ), but the class-summed displacement is +3.4 to +6.1 $\sigma_{\rm class}$ above truth in 8/10 runs, so “not a centred measurement” stands. This is claim C7, adjudicated as a MEASURED finding on the code’s own numerator, and it is the book’s cleanest example of a fix and its failure mode being the same object CLAIM C7 + C5 amendment.

Q7.5 (transfer)   The spec-z cut was tried and refuted (I7.2). Write down the two conditions any “keep only the clean data” rescue must satisfy to work, and say which of them this one fails — then name a change to the world (not to the estimator) that would repair the one it fails, and say whether that would be enough.

Show answer
(i) The survivors must still carry the constraint, and (ii) the discarded rows must be where the defect lives. The cut fails both, which is why it is a good exhibit. On (i) the retained hosts hold at most 8.7% — median ≈ 0% — of the rate-weighted in-catalogue likelihood, so the cut throws away roughly the whole measurement to keep half a percent of the catalogue. On (ii) the defect is not resident in the wide rows at all: it is in the estimator’s treatment of width, so deleting wide rows deletes data, not the bug — measured, the cut still railed at 0.870 ledger #42.

A change to the world — a targeted spectroscopic follow-up of the localisation volumes, moving the spec-z share from 0.56% toward unity — would repair (i), and only (i). It buys you a catalogue whose widths are small enough that the kernel question stops mattering in practice; it does not make a wrong width-treatment right, and at the widths GLADE+ actually has it is a promise about a future survey rather than a fix to this analysis. That asymmetry is the general lesson: data cuts can satisfy (i) or fail it, but only an estimator change can satisfy (ii).

Q7.6 (transfer)   The redshift is wide and its treatment is delicate. The GW measures the redshifted mass $M_z = M(1+z)$ far more precisely than anything else on this card — the claim file quotes $\sigma_{M_z}/M_z \approx 10^{-4}$ CLAIM C4, while the delivered covariance measures a median $8.8\times10^{-8}$ across this venue’s events (EMRI-889: $1.36\times10^{-9}$) ch06 flag F-ch06-5. Both values are carried here because the project has not reconciled them; the argument below needs only that the number is very small. Propose how that could be used, and guess whether it will help.

Show answer
If a catalogue galaxy reports a black-hole mass $M_g$, then $M_z/M_g - 1$ estimates $z$ independently of the distance — a second handle, and an extremely sharp one on the GW side. It should sharpen host identification enormously, and mechanically it does: 97–99% of impostors are rejected. Note that the sharpness on the GW side is not the binding quantity, which is why the $10^{-4}$-vs-$8.8\times10^{-8}$ disagreement does not change the answer: at either value the GW mass is orders of magnitude sharper than the catalogue’s mass, and it is the catalogue’s width that decides what the comparison can do. Whether the posterior improves is Chapter 8 ⏭ Ch 8, and the answer is the most instructive “no” in the book.

Interlude — volume_trunc, or: your quadrature can delete the signal you are integrating

A pre-registered fix, sitting exactly where a reader of this chapter would put one. The idea: unify the numerator’s integration support with the host window — integrate $N_g$ over $[z_g-4\sigma_z,\, z_g+4\sigma_z]$, the same interval that $Z_g$ and $D_g$ already share, instead of over the event-level GW window. Symmetric, tidy, and motivated by the very “counted once” instinct §3 taught you. It was implemented behind an isolated mode, tested against a decisive 494-event A/B, and REFUTED.

The measured outcome REFUTED volume_trunc_ab_20260712/FINDING.md:1-58 ledger #70 1D mean $0.745 \to \mathbf{0.800}$, 2D mean $0.768 \to \mathbf{0.800}$: away from truth, by roughly 4× the +0.013 residual it was built to remove. And it failed for two independent reasons at once. (1) The shared fixed_quad(n=50) — correct over the narrow, peak-centred GW window — is numerically invalid over the wide host window: the sparse Gauss–Legendre nodes straddle the narrow GW peak and miss it, returning 0.0000 where the exact integral is 0.24–0.65. Which nodes catch a peak depends on the host and on $h$, so the per-host numerators are erratic and $h$-dependent. (2) Even the exact host-window numerator increases monotonically in $h$ (0.24 → 0.65 over $h = 0.60 \to 0.86$), so the unified-support idea itself tilts high in this regime. The transferable lesson is not “use more nodes”. It is that quadrature is physics: an integration rule encodes an assumption about the shape of the integrand, and when a change of variables, a widened window, or a new kernel breaks that assumption, the rule fails silently and in a way that is correlated with the parameter you are inferring. The fix was rejected as designed, and the follow-up requirement written down: any wide-window numerator integral must be peak-aware or adaptive before its physics can even be evaluated FINDING.md §Conclusion. volume_trunc is retained as an experimental, FALSIFIED mode so the result stays reproducible; it is not wired into the CLI, and it must not be revived realistic_host_observation_model.md §3.2.

What this leaves on the table

You now hold a redshift kernel that is derived, ratified, measured to repair a quadratic bias and restore coverage — and separately measured to inflate the answer at the widths its own hosts actually have. The deciding experiment was pre-registered and landed on 2026-07-31: it sized the effect and told you who owns it (the estimator, not the realism layer), and it left the derivation-level collision exactly where it was, because that is not a thing a posterior can decide. So the chapter closes one question and hands you a sharper version of the other. That is an uncomfortable place to leave a chapter, and it is the honest one.

It also sets up the obvious escape. The redshift is the wide, badly-known quantity; the gravitational wave measures the redshifted mass $M_z = M(1+z)$ with a fractional precision the claim file puts at $\approx 10^{-4}$ CLAIM C4 and the delivered covariance measures at a median $8.8\times10^{-8}$ ch06 flag F-ch06-5 — both carried, both far sharper than any redshift here. It should help enormously. ⏭ Ch 8 — and the reader was promised an improvement.
