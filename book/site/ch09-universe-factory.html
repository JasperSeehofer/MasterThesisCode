Chapter 9 — Building a Universe to Break Your Estimator

Every claim so far was measured against “truth”. Where did truth come from — and would I notice if my simulator and my estimator disagreed about the same universe?

The failure this chapter answers Eight chapters of measured biases, every one of them a distance from a number called truth. That number came from a simulator: a population model, a detection cut, a host draw. So “biased” is not a property of the estimator — it is a relation between two pieces of code. And the two pieces of code in this pipeline, running on the same universe, disagree about how often the host galaxy is in the catalogue by a factor of 2.3.

1. The universe factory

Everything the book has measured runs on a mock universe built in four moves.

  1. A population. Massive black holes and their EMRI rates come from the Barausse (2012) M1 model (arXiv:1201.5888) — the same rate model that supplies the per-galaxy rate weight $w_g = R_{\rm eff}(M_g)/(1+z_g)$ from Chapter 3.
  2. A cosmology. $\Omega_m = 0.2726$, $h_{\rm true} = 0.73$ (constants.py). Not the Planck value, and not by accident — §1.2.
  3. A definition of “event”. An EMRI counts if it plunges inside the mission span. This is a convention, and it is load-bearing.
  4. An injection campaign. 200,100 simulated EMRIs pushed through the same waveform and response code as the analysis, which is where $p_{\rm det}$ came from in Chapter 4 ↩ Ch 4.

1.1 An event is a plunge inside the window

The plunge-window convention RATIFIED plunge_window_initial_conditions.md §1 Babak et al. 2017, arXiv:1703.09722 §III C/D Colpi et al. 2024, arXiv:2402.07571 The source is observed from $t = 0$ for the full mission span $T = 4.5\ \mathrm{yr}$; its plunge time is drawn $$ t_{\rm plunge} \sim U[0,\,T], $$ and the initial semi-latus rectum $p_0$ is fixed by the time-to-separatrix condition on the same PN5 trajectory that generates the waveform, $$ t_{\rm insp}\bigl(p_0;\ M_z,\mu,a,e_0,x_0\bigr) \;=\; t_{\rm plunge}, $$ solved by Brent root-finding. Events that would plunge after $T$ are excluded from the population — exactly Babak’s construction. The window is defined in the detector frame; no extra $(1+z)$ enters the draw.

The retired convention drew $p_0 \sim U[10,16]$ independently of everything else — a range lifted from a waveform-package docstring. The difference is not cosmetic. A rate model that counts plunges was being fed initial conditions that, above $M_z \approx 10^{6.2}\,M_\odot$, essentially forbid a plunge inside the window: the packet names this “audit item 1”, an input-validity domain used as an astrophysical prior. Repairing it recovered one to one-and-a-half decades of horizon at the top of the mass band — $d_{\rm hor}$ at $M_z = 10^7\,M_\odot$ went from 0.014 Gpc under the snapshot draw to 0.35–0.48 Gpc plunge_window_initial_conditions.md §11.

Trap — “The simulator is the easy part.” The simulator contains the population model, the plunge-window convention, the injection sampling measure and the host-draw weight — four places where it can disagree with the estimator. Three of this book’s live claims are exactly such disagreements (C6, C9, and the C8 measure mismatch). The simulator is not the part you check last.

2. Making the mock honest

Until campaign #51 the mock had a comfortable habit: it handed the inference each host’s redshift and black-hole mass exactly. Chapter 7 showed what a real catalogue redshift is — photometric, $\sigma_z/z \approx 49\%$ — so a mock that injects catalogue redshifts as truth and point-evaluates them is not testing the redshift kernel at all. Campaign #53 realizes the noise. Doing that correctly takes four decisions, and the first one is the one everybody gets wrong.

(A) Catalogue is TRUTH — scatter forward RATIFIED realistic_host_observation_model.md §1.2 (RATIFY-R1) Davis et al. 2011, arXiv:1012.2912 Eqs. (1)/(A1)

The stored $z_g$ is the host’s cosmological redshift. The event is generated at $d_L(z_g, h_{\rm inj})$ exactly as before; the inference is handed a realized observed catalogue, $z_{{\rm obs},g} = z_g + (1+z_g)v_{\rm pec}/c + \mathcal N(0,\sigma_{\rm meas}) \equiv z_g + \mathcal N(0, \sigma_{z,g}^{\rm tot})$. A forward model: no inverse problem, no prior ambiguity.

(B) Catalogue is the OBSERVATION — scatter truth REJECTED ibid., the analysis that rejects it

Keep $z_g$ as what the inference reads and draw a latent truth. This matches the real-data reading — and it requires the posterior draw $p(z_{\rm true}\mid z_{\rm obs}) \propto \mathcal N \cdot p_{\rm pop}$. Sampling the tempting $z_{\rm true} \sim \mathcal N(z_g,\sigma)$ omits the population prior and commits the exact Eddington error the volume deconvolution was built to remove. Doing (B) properly is ill-posed at $\sigma_z/z \approx 0.5$.

Realized noise ≡ kernel width, exactly RATIFIED realistic_host_observation_model.md §2 (RATIFY-R2) The realization must draw one total Gaussian from the same stored column the inference kernel reads: $$ \delta z_g \sim \mathcal N\bigl(0,\ \texttt{z\_error}_g\bigr), \qquad z_{{\rm obs},g} = z_g + \delta z_g, $$ not a re-derived component-wise sum of peculiar-velocity and measurement terms. Then $\sigma_{\rm realized} \equiv \sigma_{\rm kernel}$ holds per row by construction, and cannot be broken by a constants drift or a catalogue regeneration. This is Chapter 7’s counted-once rule, now on the generative side. Verified on the production catalogue (22,634,764 valid rows): the #40b fold is present in every row (0 violations); only 0.41% of rows are provably in a known PV class, which is why the total is realized rather than its components; and a $10^6$-draw Monte Carlo matches z_error to better than 0.12%.

Two consequences make the rest of the pipeline cheap and honest. Because (A) leaves every injected coordinate untouched, the event’s $d_L$, waveform, SNR and Cramér–Rao covariance are unchanged — the GPU products and the entire injection pool stay valid, and only the catalogue file handed to the evaluation changes realistic_host_observation_model.md §5 (RATIFY-R5). And the selection legs split cleanly along Mandel, Farr & Gair’s rule: $p_{\rm det}$ legs integrate over true quantities, catalogue-composition legs describe what the analyst’s catalogue contains and therefore use observed rows.

The fourth decision is the guard. A ratified derivation can be enforced, and this one is: the evaluation refuses to run a point-resolving host-redshift kernel — and refuses generator_marginal altogether — whenever the loaded catalogue is a scattered realization, because that mode’s selection leg was derived for the unscattered generator. The guard is one-directional: running the unscattered baseline with the width kernel is merely conservative, and stays legal §3.4, §9 (RATIFY-R3/R9) bayesian_statistics.py:112, :186, :273. That is what it looks like when a derivation is executable rather than decorative.

The honest price, stated by the packet itself RATIFIED realistic_host_observation_model.md §1.2 Under (A) the declared truth redshift distribution equals GLADE+’s observed one — photo-$z$-smeared and Malmquist-shaped — rather than the deconvolved sky. That is a second-order, population-level statement; it does not touch per-event consistency; it is standard in dark-siren mock studies that draw hosts from a real catalogue (Gray et al. 2020, arXiv:1908.06050 §IV). It is marked ASSUMED, to be stated in the paper. Remember this sentence when §6 asks why the catalogue’s hosts are systematically heavier than the population’s.

Two measured facts about the realized venue close this section. First, the hosts are photometric — all of them: at $z \lt 0.15$ the pruned catalogue is 97.4% photometric by rate weight, and the campaign realized 164 in-catalogue hosts, zero spectroscopic, which falsifies the derivation’s own expectation of about 3.4 spectroscopic hosts per seed §4 (RATIFY-R4), P6. A ratified packet made a quantitative prediction about its own venue and the venue said no; the packet is not thereby wrong, but the deferral that rested on that prediction is.

Second, a documented approximation: realized redshifts are clipped at $z_{\rm floor} = 10^{-5}$ rather than redrawn, because redrawing would break $\sigma_{\rm realized} = \sigma_{\rm kernel}$. The clip fires 108,395 times out of 22,641,048 rows in r1 — 0.5%, all of them photometric rows at $z \lesssim 2\sigma$ that carry essentially no information precisely because their kernel is that wide sources map §7 item 8. It is small. It is also a generative clamp, and the project’s own history contains a $+0.030$ harness bias that was re-attributed to exactly such a clamp ledger #69 — which is why every citation of that exoneration has to carry this caveat rather than inherit it.

3. What am I normalizing to?

Now the deep problem of this chapter. Chapter 3’s catalogue likelihood is a discrete sum over catalogue rows. Chapters 4 and 5’s selection integrals — $D(h)$, $\beta_G$, $\beta_{\bar G}$ — are continuous population integrals. The master equation multiplies them together. Something has to make a count of galaxies commensurable with a volume.

The Option-A bridge RATIFIED G2c §3 Treat the catalogue as a Monte-Carlo realization of an in-catalogue number density $n_G(z,\Omega) = \bar n_{\rm gal} f(z,\Omega)$ with $\bar n_{\rm gal}$ constant in comoving coordinates. Then for any smooth per-galaxy statistic $Q$, $$ \sum_{g \in {\rm cat}} w_g\, Q(z_g,\Omega_g) \;\approx\; \bar n_{\rm gal} \int\! dz\, d\Omega\; f(z,\Omega)\, \bar w(z)\, Q\, \frac{dV_c}{dz\,d\Omega}, $$ and applying it to Gray’s (A10) denominator gives $$ \Sigma_{\rm global}(h) \;\approx\; C\,\beta_G(h), \qquad C = \bar n_{\rm gal}\langle R_{\rm eff}\rangle \ \text{independent of } h $$ up to second order. Everything unknowable — $\bar n_{\rm gal}$, the rate normalization — then cancels.

That approximate identity is the hinge of the whole normalization story, and the pipeline offers three ways to lean on it G2c §4.1–4.3:

  • global — $L^{\rm cat} = \sum_{\rm ball} w_g N_g / \Sigma_{\rm global}(h)$. The faithful discrete transcription of Gray’s (A10). Deprecated and warned: on photometric catalogues it is empirically mis-calibrated, ~0% coverage, railing to the grid edge.
  • local_ratio — $L^{\rm cat} = \sum_{\rm ball} w_g N_g / \sum_{\rm ball} w_g D_g$, self-normalized over the same galaxy set, so all per-galaxy constants cancel row-by-row and no density calibration is needed at all.
  • volume_deconv — the same local ratio with Chapter 7’s volume-deconvolved host-redshift kernel inside both $N_g$ and $D_g$. The library default.
A citation to not propagate FINDING G2c §6 C1 The code comment at bayesian_statistics.py:1578-1582 calls local_ratio “the Gray A.9/A.10 literal local self-normalized ratio-of-sums”. It is not literal: (A10)’s selection sum runs over the whole catalogue, not over the event’s candidate ball. The nearest published analogue is the catalogue-patch case, Gray Eq. (A20), with the patch identified event-by-event with a three-dimensional localization ball and without (A20)’s explicit $\Omega_{\rm rest}$ term. The mathematics implemented is as documented; only the attribution is wrong. The book says “deliberate deviation, empirically justified by P–P calibration”, which is what it is.

4. Two estimands

So: local_ratio and volume_deconv never invoke the Option-A identity, because they never build $\Sigma_{\rm global}$. The two absolute modes do — and they do it differently.

The two absolute normalizations RATIFIED DERIVATION_GENERATOR_CONSISTENT_NORM.md §2.3, §3.1 Both assemble the same per-event likelihood shape, $$ p_i(h) \;=\; \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{g\in B_i} w_g N_g(h) \big/ n_w(h) \;+\; B^{\rm num}_i(h)}{D_\bullet(h)} , $$ and differ in what $n_w$ and $D_\bullet$ are:
absolute_marginal (campaign #53, the realistic runs): $\bar n_w = \Sigma_{\rm glob}/\beta_G$ and $D = \beta_G + \beta_{\bar G}$ — i.e. it uses the Option-A identity. ($\Sigma_{\rm glob}$ is the normalization packet’s spelling of G2c’s $\Sigma_{\rm global}$; same object, the catalogue-channel detection expectation.)
generator_marginal (campaign #51, the idealized baseline): $\hat n_w = W_{\rm cat}/V_f(h)$ and $D_{\rm gen} = \Sigma_{\rm glob}/\hat n_w + \beta_{\bar G}$, with $W_{\rm cat} = \sum_{z_g \lt 1.5} w_g$ an $h$-independent scalar and $V_f(h) = \int_0^{1.5} \bar f(z,h)\,(dV_c/dz)/(1+z)\,dz$. Here $\bar n_w$ does not survive: no model integral is ever compared against a discrete catalogue sum anywhere in the estimator. The Option-A identity is not approximately enforced — it is never invoked.

The second mode’s mixing fraction is derived, not posited: the injection is a per-event $\mathrm{Bernoulli}(F)$ channel split, so $F(h) = V_f(h)/V_{\rm tot}(h)$, measured at $F = 0.0175370\ldots$ and exactly $h$-independent, reproducing the generator’s own F_incat_population to machine precision. It was pre-registered to not de-rail the deep venue; the prediction was falsified, favorably — 1D and 2D MAP both landed at 0.73 ledger #81.

Here is the trap. Both modes report a field called w_G, “the probability the host is catalogued”. On the same run they report 0.1215 and 0.0497. Neither is corrupt. They are different estimands — different conditionals — each internally consistent inside its own mode. The project spent real effort chasing a phantom “45% discrepancy” between two numbers that were never the same quantity, and declared a diagnostics column corrupt when it was simply the other mode’s value CLAIM_2D_BIAS_20260730.md, “Loose thread” → C9.

5. The identity that fails

absolute_marginal is the mode campaign #53 ran, so the Option-A identity is not an academic point for this book — it is load-bearing for every realistic number in it. The commission asked for it to be checked by summing the real GLADE catalogue directly. It was.

Most of it, and the leftover is the finding. $(0.86/0.60)^3 = 2.94$ against a measured 2.48: the $h^3$ density scaling is the dominant piece, it is common to the discrete numerator and denominator of $L^{\rm cat}$, and it cancels in the likelihood ratio. 6. C9 — the generator and the estimator describe different universes

Which brings the chapter back to its cold open, now with the machinery to read it. The estimator’s mixture weight $w_G = \beta_G/D$ is a statement about the generator: of the events this universe produces and this detector sees, this fraction should have a catalogued host. It is checkable by counting, and the count is 164 of 3135.

Trap — “If the generator and the estimator disagree, the estimator is wrong.” Sometimes the generator is. What C9 establishes is that somebody is wrong, at 11.9σ, inside one run.

7. What this chapter cannot tell you

The idealized campaign recovers truth at $-0.24\sigma$ and $-0.36\sigma$ on two independent seeds. It is worth being exact about what that buys. Those runs use each host’s true redshift and mass, point-to-point: recovery is guaranteed by construction, and the measured $\sigma_h = 0.00030$ matches “$z$ exact, GW distance error only” to about 5%. It is a consistency test of the generator–estimator pair — the readout says so in its own caveat section — and it therefore cannot test redshift kernels, catalogue scatter, mass proxies, or the completion branch’s calibration.

Self-check

Q9.1   The pipeline uses $\Omega_m = 0.2726$, not the Planck value. Bug or design choice, and how would you tell?

Show answer
Design choice, and you tell by asking what the number is for: it matches the Barausse (2012) M1 cosmology that the EMRI population itself is drawn from, so the mock universe is self-consistent. Using Planck’s $\Omega_m$ with an M1 population would put the generator and its own rate model in disagreement — a worse error than a known offset. The Planck mismatch is tracked as a quoted systematic ($+1.5$–$3\%$; $+0.16\%$ at $z = 0.05$ rising to $+3.31\%$ at $z = 1.5$ G7 row 6), not silently absorbed. The general rule this chapter teaches: a constant is a bug only relative to a stated purpose.

Q9.2   Two normalization modes give $w_G(0.73) = 0.1215$ and $0.0497$ for the same run. Is one of them wrong?

Show answer
No — they are different estimands, and both are internally consistent within their own mode. absolute_marginal and generator_marginal normalize the catalogue likelihood differently, so “the probability the host is catalogued” means a different conditional in each. The danger is not the ambiguity but the comparison across modes: the project spent real effort chasing a phantom “45% discrepancy” that turned out to be two incompatible estimands placed side by side, and one diagnostics column was declared corrupt when it was merely the other mode’s value C9 / loose thread.

Q9.3   The estimator’s $w_G(0.73) = 0.1215$; the realized detected in-catalogue rate is $164/3135 = 0.0523$. Why is this a defect independent of whether it causes the bias?

Show answer
Because it is an internal inconsistency: the same code, in the same run, models a quantity at 2.3–2.5× what the same code’s own generator realizes, at binomial $z = -11.86$. Whatever it does to the MAP, the estimator’s mixture weight is not the quantity the simulation produced, and no downstream number can be defended while that holds. The project localizes the whole gap to the catalogue’s relative detection efficiency — $\beta_G$ weights by a pool-marginal, population-mass $p_{\rm det}$, while Malmquist-selected catalogue hosts carry heavier black holes — with two independent suppression measures agreeing to 0.2σ (0.39248 vs $0.3991 \pm 0.0312$) CLAIM C9.

Q9.4   The idealized campaign uses each host’s true $z$ and $M$ point-to-point. Name what that guarantees and what it therefore cannot test.

Show answer
It guarantees recovery-on-truth by construction — with the redshift exactly right, each golden event pins $h$ at its GW distance precision, which is why the measured $\sigma_h = 0.00030$ matches “$z$ exact + GW $d_L$ error only” to ~5%. It therefore cannot test anything about redshift kernels, catalogue scatter, mass proxies, or the completion branch’s calibration. It is a consistency test of the generator–estimator pair, and the readout says so in its own caveat section. The book quotes that caveat rather than celebrating the number IDEALIZATION_LEDGER.md I1–I4.

Q9.5 (transfer)   Your estimator recovers truth at $-0.24\sigma$ on two independent seeds. State the strongest claim you are entitled to make, and the claim you are not.

Show answer
Entitled: “on this venue, with these idealizations, the generator and the estimator agree, and no gross error is present in the path that connects them.” Not entitled: “the estimator is unbiased” or “the estimator is calibrated” — the first needs many realizations, the second needs coverage. And there is a sharper trap: the project measured that 133 golden events carry ~100% of the curvature, so the closure test validated host association, not the selection machinery it was assumed to validate.
