1. The inner product, and the noise that defines it

Start with the only question a detector can answer: how much of this waveform is there, given how noisy I am at each frequency? LISA is not equally deaf everywhere. It has a sweet spot around a few millihertz, laser shot noise rising at high frequency, acceleration noise rising at low frequency, and — because tens of millions of galactic white-dwarf binaries chirp in the same band — an irreducible confusion foreground that is itself a source of noise. All of that is packed into one function of frequency, the one-sided power spectral density $S_n(f)$, with units of strain² per hertz.

Given $S_n$, there is exactly one natural way to compare two waveforms: weight each frequency by how well you hear it.

The noise-weighted inner product RATIFIED G8 §1.1 parameter_estimation.py:335 Finn 1992, PRD 46 5236 Eq. 2.3 Cutler & Flanagan 1994, arXiv:gr-qc/9402014 Eq. 2.4 For two time-domain signals with continuous Fourier transforms $\tilde h_1,\tilde h_2$, summed over the TDI channels $\alpha \in \{A,E\}$, $$ \langle h_1 \mid h_2\rangle \;=\; 4\,\mathrm{Re} \sum_{\alpha} \int_{f_{\min}}^{f_{\max}} \frac{\tilde h_1^{\alpha}(f)\,\tilde h_2^{\alpha *}(f)}{S_n^{\alpha}(f)}\, df , \qquad \tilde h(f) = \int dt\; h(t)\, e^{-2\pi i f t}. $$ Everything in this chapter is built from that one bracket. The signal-to-noise ratio is $\rho = \sqrt{\langle h\mid h\rangle}$ (parameter_estimation.py:488), the detection threshold is $\rho \ge 20$ (constants.py SNR_THRESHOLD), and the Fisher matrix of §3 is the same bracket taken between derivatives.

Two things about that bracket deserve a sentence each, because both of them have cost this project real numbers.

The PSD is a physics input, not a convenience. The galactic confusion foreground was missing from $S_n$ until Phase 9 — a landed fix, recorded with the honest verdict fixed-and-landed, insufficient alone ledger #1, and it was not the only thing wrong in that part of the code provenance panel.

It is dimensionless, and that is checkable. $[\tilde h^2/S_n]\cdot[df] = (\text{strain}^2\,\mathrm{s}^2)\,/\,(\text{strain}^2/\mathrm{Hz})\cdot\mathrm{Hz} = 1$. An SNR is a pure number. Hold on to that, because §2 is the story of what happened when the implemented expression was not dimensionless and nobody noticed for four months.

2. $dt^2$: a Riemann sum, and a different universe

Equation (1) is an integral over a continuous Fourier transform. A computer has samples. The bridge between them is one line of undergraduate numerical analysis: numpy.fft.rfft returns the un-normalized discrete transform $X_k = \sum_m h(t_m) e^{-2\pi i k m/n}$, and approximating $\tilde h(f_k) = \int h(t)e^{-2\pi i f_k t}dt$ by its Riemann sum on the sampling grid $t_m = m\,\Delta t$ gives

The DFT ↔ continuous-FT correspondence RATIFIED G8 §1.2 parameter_estimation.py:396 $$ \tilde h(f_k) \;\simeq\; \Delta t \cdot X_k \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad |\tilde h|^2 = \Delta t^2\,|X|^2 . $$ The implemented inner product used $X_k$ where the formula wants $\Delta t\,X_k$, in both slots. It therefore returned exactly $\langle h_1\mid h_2\rangle/\Delta t^{2}$ — and at this pipeline’s $\Delta t = 10\ \mathrm{s}$ that is a factor $10^{-2}$, every time.

Dimensional check (the tell). Without $\Delta t^2$ the expression carries units of $\mathrm{Hz}^2$, i.e. $1/\mathrm{s}^2$ — it is not a squared signal-to-noise ratio at all. Only the $\Delta t^2$-corrected form is dimensionless.

One line, one factor, no physics. Now follow it through. The bracket is quadratic in the signal, so:

  • every SNR came out $\sqrt{10^{-2}} = 1/10$ of its physical value;
  • every Fisher matrix, being a bracket of derivatives, came out $100\times$ too small;
  • every Cramér–Rao $\sigma$, going as the inverse square root of the Fisher, came out $10\times$ too large.

And here is why that is not a rescaling. The pipeline is internally self-consistent — threshold, $p_{\rm det}$, and the stored covariances all shared the same mis-scaled bracket, so nothing downstream broke. But the threshold is a cut on an absolute number. “$\rho \ge 20$” applied to a quantity that is ten times too small selects physically $\rho \ge 200$ sources: the near ones. The mock universe the analysis could see collapsed to the neighbourhood.

The principle this chapter takes from it FINDING ledger #51 G7 row 1 commit fcc49c4 A normalization that multiplies a threshold changes which data exists. Constants that only rescale an answer are bookkeeping. Constants that sit next to an inequality are physics, because they decide the sample. This one was found by the G8 soundness gate on 2026-07-02 and fixed before the Phase-2 campaign; the CRB tables this whole book uses are post-fix.

3. Fisher and Cramér–Rao: where the ellipsoid comes from

An EMRI waveform is described by 14 parameters — two masses, the spin, the orbital elements $(p_0, e_0, x_0)$, the distance, four angles for sky position and spin orientation, and three initial phases. The question is: having observed one, how well is each of those pinned down, and by how much do the answers trade off against each other?

Near a high signal-to-noise maximum the log-likelihood is locally quadratic, and a quadratic log-likelihood is a Gaussian. The curvature of that quadratic — the matrix of second derivatives — is the Fisher information matrix, and because the noise is Gaussian it can be written entirely with the bracket of §1:

Fisher matrix and the Cramér–Rao bound RATIFIED parameter_estimation.py:399 parameter_estimation.py:430 Vallisneri 2008, arXiv:gr-qc/0703086 $$ \Gamma_{ab} \;=\; \bigl\langle \partial_a h \,\big|\, \partial_b h \bigr\rangle , \qquad \Sigma \;=\; \Gamma^{-1} , $$ with $\Sigma$ the covariance of the best achievable unbiased estimator — the Cramér–Rao bound. For 14 parameters the symmetric upper triangle is 105 bracket evaluations per event, each one an FFT and a PSD-weighted integral; this is the computational hot path of the whole simulation pipeline.

Note what $\Gamma$ is made of: derivatives of the waveform. There is no analytic form for an EMRI waveform’s parameter derivatives, so they are taken numerically — and the accuracy of that numerical step is a physics decision, not an implementation detail.

If you have met Fisher matrices before, this is the section where the GW stratum earns its keep: the folds from here on carry the 14-parameter version, the stencil, and the conditioning. Switch persona at the top of the page, or just open them.

Trap — “The Cramér–Rao bound is the posterior.” It is the leading-order Gaussian approximation to the likelihood around its peak, and this pipeline’s $\rho \ge 20$ threshold exists partly to keep that approximation honest. What degrades first as $\rho$ falls is not the width but the shape: non-Gaussian tails and secondary sky modes. That matters more here than in most applications, because — as the cold open showed — the candidate-host sum is an integral over exactly those tails. A 5% error in $\sigma$ is harmless. A missing secondary sky mode is not.

4. Fraction coordinates: the three numbers the likelihood actually sees

The inference does not use all fourteen parameters. It needs the sky position, because that is what selects candidate galaxies, and the distance, because that is what carries $h$. So the 14×14 covariance is projected onto three coordinates — and the choice of which three is where the pipeline’s design shows.

The GW likelihood in fraction coordinates RATIFIED dark_siren_likelihood.md §2.1 G2a §1 bayesian_statistics.py:1856 _mvn_pdf With $\phi = \phi_S$ (ecliptic azimuth), $\theta = q_S$ (ecliptic polar angle) and the fractional distance $u = d_L/\hat d_L$, the per-event GW likelihood is $$ p(x_{\rm GW}\mid \phi,\theta,d_L) \;\simeq\; \mathcal N_3\bigl((\phi,\theta,u);\ \mu,\ \Sigma\bigr), \qquad \mu = (\hat\phi,\ \hat\theta,\ 1), $$ $$ \Sigma \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} \sigma_\phi^2 & C_{\theta\phi} & C_{d_L\phi}/\hat d_L \\ C_{\theta\phi} & \sigma_\theta^2 & C_{d_L\theta}/\hat d_L \\ C_{d_L\phi}/\hat d_L & C_{d_L\theta}/\hat d_L & \sigma_{d_L}^2/\hat d_L^{\,2} \end{pmatrix} . $$ The mean’s third component is 1 by construction: at the maximum-likelihood point the fractional distance is one. code, current tree: bayesian_statistics.py:2389-2409 (assembly), :2459 (mean)

Why fractional? Because the gravitational-wave amplitude determines $d_L$ multiplicatively — the strain goes as $1/d_L$, so what the detector constrains is a ratio, not a length. The claim is that this makes $\sigma_u$ approximately SNR-set and approximately distance-independent, which would make the stored covariance reusable and the Gaussian approximation better behaved.

The fraction coordinate is not a convention; it is the coordinate in which this measurement is almost one-dimensional.

And the sky is not. The same product for $\sigma_\phi$ and $\sigma_\theta$ spreads by factors of 5.4 and 9.2. Sky localization depends on how the LISA antenna pattern sweeps past that particular source over the mission — geometry that $\rho$ knows nothing about. Both halves matter: the first licenses the distance-fraction coordinate; the second is the honest bound on the SNR rescaling the next widget uses.

Two consequences follow immediately, and they are the reason this section exists.

First: the GW leg is $h$-independent. $\Sigma$ and $\mu$ are properties of the detector and the source. Nothing in them knows about $h$. All of the cosmology enters through one place — the argument at which the Gaussian is evaluated, $u = d_L(z;h)/\hat d_L$. Turning $h$ up shrinks $d_L(z;h)$ for every candidate galaxy at once, sliding the whole candidate set along the $u$ axis. That is the entire mechanism by which a galaxy catalogue measures the Hubble constant, and it is why a per-event $\ln p_i(h)$ curve is interpretable at all dark_siren_likelihood.md §2.4.

Second: no Jacobian. The integration variable is $z$ (dimensionless), the Gaussian is parameterized in $u$ (dimensionless), and the change of variables from $d_L$ to $u$ is already absorbed into $\Sigma$ — so no $dd_L/dz$ appears in the numerator. In these coordinates the GW factor is a likelihood evaluated at $d_L(z;h)$, not a density in $z$: it carries no measure of its own. Two entries in the project’s ledger are that one sentence, learned the hard way; they are in the fold below ledger #5 · H0R:1010.

4.1 The fourth coordinate, and why it is nearly degenerate

The mass channel adds a fourth coordinate: $M_{z,\rm frac} = M_z/\hat M_z$, the fractional redshifted mass, with mean 1 for the same reason. What matters here is that the 4×4 Gaussian then splits exactly, by the standard multivariate-normal conditioning identity: the 3D marginal of the 4D Gaussian is the 3D likelihood, and everything the mass adds is one conditional Gaussian sitting beside it. The two channels share a numerator factor exactly, not approximately — the algebra is in the fold below.

For event 889 the numbers are: $\sigma_{M_z}/M_z = 1.36\times10^{-9}$ before conditioning and $\sigma_{\rm cond} = 1.19\times10^{-9}$ after — knowing the sky position and distance buys 13% on the mass. And there is the source of that $10^{11}$ condition number in the 4×4: it mixes a coordinate of width $\sim10^{-3}$ with one of width $\sim10^{-9}$, and the condition number of a matrix is roughly the square of that dynamic range.

The redshifted mass is measured absurdly well — and “absurdly” is a word this chapter should price rather than admire. The population median $(\sigma_{M_z}/M_z)\,\rho = 2.57\times10^{-6}$ implies $1.3\times10^{-7}$ at the detection threshold $\rho = 20$. Published EMRI Fisher forecasts — Babak et al. (2017), the same paper this book takes its population model and its SNR-20 horizon from — quote fractional redshifted-mass precisions of order $10^{-5}$–$10^{-6}$ at comparable SNR. This run is therefore roughly 8 to 80 times better than the published range. That is a gap worth naming out loud: it could be the AK-vs-numerical-derivative difference, the mission baseline, or the familiar optimism of a high-SNR Fisher approximation. It is not tested here, and nothing in this book leans on it — Chapter 8’s result turns on what the mass channel does to the sum, not on how narrow it is, and its answer is not the obvious one ⏭ Ch 8. seed61000 CRB, 1590 rows Babak et al. 2017, arXiv:1703.09722

Provenance — what is real, what is rescaled, and which catalogue

What is real and what is rescaled. The covariance, the sky position, the search radius, the redshift window and every galaxy plotted are measured — the first four from the run’s own Cramér–Rao row, the last from the GLADE+ reduced catalogue, put through the production pipeline (stellar→BH mass relation, equatorial→ecliptic rotation, mass/redshift prune). Trap 6.A — “Sky localization and distance are separate measurements.”

They are correlated through the antenna pattern, and the correlation sits in the off-diagonals of the stored 3×3 covariance. Factorizing them mis-weights the candidate galaxies — measured just above on event 361: 35% of the candidate weight moves, and the top-weighted candidate changes.

▸ For the GW reader — search radius, redshift window, and the measure question

The ball. Candidates come from a BallTree over unit vectors, so the query metric is the Euclidean chord, and the radius is $n_\sigma\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(J\Sigma_{\rm sky}J^{\mathsf T})}$ with $J = \mathrm{diag}(|\sin\theta|, 1)$ rescaling the azimuthal standard deviation to a great-circle length ($ds^2 = d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2$). Production passes $n_\sigma = 1.5$, explicitly, at the one and only ball-search call site handler.py:575-578 bayesian_statistics.py:2838. This chapter reproduces that exactly. Note the trap, because it caught this book once: the signature default of get_possible_hosts_from_ball_tree is $n_\sigma = 2$ (handler.py:568) and production never uses it, while a different multiplier of 2.0 sits fifteen lines above at :2823 — parameterizing the redshift window, not the ball. Chapter 3’s census was measured at the signature default until 2026-07-31 and has been regenerated ch03_FLAGS.md F-ch03-2. The 1D channel then applies the redshift window only; the 2D channel adds a mass window on top handler.py:584-592 vs :594-603 — a structural asymmetry that is load-bearing later ⏭ Ch 8.

The redshift window. Bounds come from get_redshift_outer_bounds, capped at the analysis redshift limit. Note a small piece of dead code found while reproducing it: the function takes a sigma_multiplier argument that its body never uses — both bounds hardcode $3\sigma$ physical_relations.py:546-567 — so production’s sigma_multiplier=2.0 (bayesian_statistics.py:2823) has no effect and the window is always $\pm3\sigma_{d_L}$. This is conservative, not wrong: the window bounds which galaxies enter the sum, and the weighting inside it is done by the Gaussian, not by the window. This chapter calls the production function and therefore inherits the real behaviour ch06_FLAGS.md F-ch06-4.

The measure. The Fisher matrix is computed with respect to the bare coordinates $(\phi_S, q_S)$, so $\mathcal N_3$ is a density with respect to $d\phi\,d\theta\,du$ — not with respect to the solid angle $d\Omega = \sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi$ G2a §1. The sky panel above is drawn in the great-circle tangent plane (the $J$-scaled coordinates, which is what the ball search uses), so the plotted ellipse and the plotted circle are geometrically honest; the weights come from the bare-coordinate Gaussian. That distinction is not pedantry — the missing $\sin\theta_{\rm det}$ Jacobian in the completion term was a real, landed fix worth a median $1.15\times$ per-event over-weighting ledger #46 commit 4a259b7 ⏭ Ch 5.

The two Jacobian entries §4 defers here. A spurious $/(1+z)$ factor in the with-BH-mass numerator was found and removed in Phase 15 ledger #5. Separately, a proposed Jacobian — the “missing $dd_L/dz$” in the catalogue term — was REFUTED on sign grounds H0R:1010. One was a real factor that should not have been there; the other was an imagined factor that should not have been added. Both are the same lesson about what the GW factor is a density in.

5. The frame story: the same matrix, in the wrong sky

One number in $\Sigma$ has not been discussed: none of them, individually. The question of §5 is about all of them at once — in what coordinate system are they written?

LISA waveform conventions are barycentric ecliptic. GLADE+ stores positions in equatorial ICRS J2000. The two frames differ by the obliquity of the ecliptic, 23.4°. And the median BallTree search radius in that era was 1.76°.

The primary mover of its era FINDING ledger #12 H0R §3.2 H0R:134 (Audit A5) Catalogue ingestion stored equatorial coordinates while the EMRI sky angles were ecliptic. A 23.4° systematic offset against a 1.76° ball does not add noise; it puts the ball somewhere else. About 15 of 60 events landed on spurious near-host matches and most of the rest found no possible hosts at all. Measured after the repair: host recovery 31 → 38 of 60, “no possible hosts” 10 → 1, a 5.4σ sky anisotropy in the Q3 quartile → 0σ, and the MAP 0.860 → 0.730.

That is the single largest movement any covariance-side repair has produced in this project’s history, and it came from a rotation, not from a formula. It is also the reason Chapter 5’s question — can I trust either branch? — is a question about the covariance and not only about the mixture: a mis-framed $\Sigma$ systematically picks up the wrong galaxies, and since galaxies at different sky positions sit at different redshifts, the wrong galaxies prefer a wrong $h$ coherently across events. Coherent error is bias. Incoherent error is noise. A frame is coherent.

And then the part that makes this a two-sided story.

The same hypothesis, later, on a different venue EXONERATED ledger #27 H0R:1010 In June a residual bias on the seed400 venue prompted the obvious suspicion: frame mismatch again. It was checked by tracing the full construction chain and survived two adversarial refutation attempts. Verdict: non-cause. That covariance was already natively ecliptic, the migration script had deliberately not been run on it, and rotating it would have double-rotated — introducing the very defect the fix was meant to remove. Neither verdict transfers to the other’s venue, and the project’s response was not a better rotation but a stamp: the lesson is written into a guard’s error message as stamp, do not rotate.

6. What the open box cannot fix

Take stock. EMRI-889’s distance is known to nine parts in ten thousand. Its sky position is pinned to about a square arcminute. Its detector-frame mass is known to nine significant figures — a claim §4.1 prices against the published forecasts and does not entirely believe. Two galaxies are inside its ball. By any standard of gravitational-wave astronomy this is an extraordinarily well-measured event, and the instrument that measured it is now fully specified: an inner product, a Fisher matrix, an inverse, three fraction coordinates, and a frame stamp.

Now put a number next to it. The catalogue redshift of a host galaxy — the other half of every term in the sum — is known, for the events that carry this run’s information, to a median fractional precision of about 49% IDEALIZED_BASELINE_READOUT.md:50-52.

The failure the next chapter answers The gravitational-wave side of this measurement is, for these events, effectively exact — the redshift side is effectively unmeasured, by roughly three orders of magnitude. Every remaining question about this pipeline is therefore a question about handling a wide, badly-known redshift correctly. And the naive handling — put the catalogue’s central value in, widen the error bar a bit — does not merely widen the answer. It moves it.

Self-check

Q6.1   The Cramér–Rao bound is a bound. Under what conditions is using it as the posterior width defensible, and what would you expect to break first as those conditions fail?

Show answer
It is the leading-order Gaussian approximation to the likelihood around its peak, valid at high SNR where the log-likelihood is locally quadratic. This pipeline’s $\rho \ge 20$ threshold is chosen with that in mind constants.py SNR_THRESHOLD, and the loudest events — up to $\rho = 1424.7$ in this run — are extremely safe. What breaks first as SNR falls is not the width but the shape: non-Gaussian tails and multimodality in sky position. That matters enormously here because the candidate-host sum is an integral over exactly those tails — at $\rho = 28$, event 555’s ball holds 12,056 galaxies, so the sum is dominated by the far wings of the Gaussian, not by its core. A 5% error in $\sigma$ is harmless; a missing secondary sky mode is not.

Q6.2   The distance is parameterized fractionally, $u = d_L/\hat d_L$. Why is that the natural coordinate, and what would go wrong with absolute $d_L$?

Show answer
Because the GW amplitude determines $d_L$ multiplicatively — the strain goes as $1/d_L$ — so the fractional error is roughly SNR-set and roughly $d_L$-independent, whereas $\sigma$ in absolute distance scales with distance itself. This run measures the claim directly: $\sigma_u \times \rho$ has median 1.040 with a p95/p5 spread of only 1.29 across 1590 events spanning $\rho = 20$–1425 and $d_L = 0.068$–7.3 Gpc, while the absolute $\sigma_{d_L}$ over the same events runs from 0.08 Mpc to hundreds of Mpc seed61000 CRB. In fractional coordinates the covariance is approximately stationary across the population, which makes the stored CRB reusable and the Gaussian approximation better behaved. It also means the mean of the fractional coordinate is exactly 1 by construction, which is why the code’s mean vector is $(\hat\phi,\hat\theta,1)$ bayesian_statistics.py:2459. Note the honest limit: the sky widths do not collapse this way (spread 5.4 and 9.2), so “fraction coordinates make everything SNR-set” would be the wrong generalization.

Q6.3   A missing $dt^2$ made every SNR 10× too small. Without looking it up: what does a factor 10 in SNR do to (a) the Fisher matrix, (b) the CRB $\sigma$, (c) the detectable population depth?

Show answer
(a) The Fisher is quadratic in the signal, so 100× too small; (b) the CRB $\sigma$ goes as the inverse square root of the Fisher, so 10× too large; (c) since $\rho \propto 1/d_L$ at fixed source, a 10× SNR deficit shrinks the horizon about 10× — and because the number of detections goes roughly as the enclosed volume, the population is not rescaled, it is deleted. The project measured its mock population capped at $z \le 0.11$ instead of $\lesssim 1.5$ G8 §2 L5; this chapter’s counterfactual on the production injection pool gives deepest $z$ = 0.124 instead of 1.108 and 45 detections instead of 7548. The lesson the book draws: a constant factor in an inner product is never “just a normalization”, because normalizations that multiply a threshold change which data exists.

Q6.4   Why does a covariance stamped in the wrong sky frame bias H₀ rather than merely add noise?

Show answer
Because the candidate-host sum weights each galaxy by the Gaussian evaluated at that galaxy’s coordinates, so a frame error systematically mis-places the ellipsoid relative to the real large-scale structure and the sum picks up the wrong galaxies. Since galaxies at different sky positions sit at different redshifts, the wrong galaxies prefer a wrong $h$ — and they do so coherently across every event, because a frame offset is the same rotation for all of them. Coherent error is bias; incoherent error is noise. The project measured host recovery 31 → 38 of 60 and the MAP moving 0.860 → 0.730 from this alone ledger #12. The scale is what makes it decisive: a 23.4° obliquity against a 1.76° median search radius is not a perturbation of the ball, it is a different ball. The matching negative result is worth as much: when the same hypothesis was raised again on a later venue it was EXONERATED — that covariance was already ecliptic, and “fixing” it would have double-rotated ledger #27.
