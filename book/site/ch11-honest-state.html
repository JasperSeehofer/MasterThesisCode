2. C5, fairly: a rail that is real, and smaller than it sounds

Start with the claim that most invites over-reading. Of the 76 events whose host galaxy is in the catalogue — the ones that are supposed to carry the measurement — 44, or 57.9%, have their per-event 1D posterior peaking at $h = 0.86$, the top of the prior grid. Median per-event argmax: 0.860. In the idealized baseline the same 76 events give median 0.730 and 4/76 = 5.3% at the edge.

The obvious objection is the right one to make first, and the project made it against itself: any bounded grid piles probability at its edge, so a concentration at 0.86 could just be a broad, uninformative profile getting clipped. That is a refutation route, and it was executed.

3. The two runaways, unlocked

In Chapter 5 you split the event set in two and found the two halves disagreeing: the 76 in-catalogue events peaking at 0.86, the 1512 dark events at 0.64, and the combined answer sitting between them at 0.700–0.742. You were asked to hold that thought for six chapters ⏮ Ch 5. Here is what it was for.

Two readings were on the table. Reading A: both branches are fine, the classes genuinely carry different statistical power, and their crossing is a legitimate combination — that is what combining evidence is. Reading B: at least one branch is tilted, and the crossing is a coincidence of two opposing errors, in which case the “measurement” is not centred on truth, it is balanced there.

Those two readings make different predictions about something you can measure: leverage. Under reading A, small perturbations to the class composition should barely move the answer, because each class is separately informative about $h$ and they agree. Under reading B, the answer sits where two steep opposing slopes happen to cross, so a small change in the relative weight of the two classes slides the crossing point a long way.

The leverage of a crossing attack_c5_leverage.py:160-200 attack_c5_class_weight.py Write the combined log-posterior as the sum of two class profiles, $S(h) = S_{\rm in}(h) + S_{\rm dk}(h)$, and reweight the in-catalogue class by $\lambda = 1+\varepsilon$: $$ S_\lambda(h) \;=\; \lambda\,S_{\rm in}(h) \;+\; S_{\rm dk}(h), \qquad h^*(\lambda) \;=\; \arg\max_h S_\lambda(h). $$ Differentiating the stationarity condition $S'_\lambda(h^*) = 0$ gives the leverage $$ \frac{dh^*}{d\varepsilon} \;=\; -\,\frac{S'_{\rm in}(h^*)}{S''_{\rm tot}(h^*)} . $$ A well-conditioned combination has a small class slope over a large total curvature. A crossing has the opposite: each class is steeply sloped at $h^*$ (they must be, to cross) while their sum is nearly flat there. The natural size of $\varepsilon$ is the Poisson fluctuation of the class count, $\pm 1/\sqrt{N_{\rm class}}$ — a statistical nothing, not a modelling choice.

Predict, then reveal — how much can a class-count fluctuation be worth?

You are about to reweight the in-catalogue class by $\pm 1/\sqrt{76} = \pm 11.5\%$ — the size of a Poisson fluctuation in how many of the 1588 events happened to have a catalogued host. Nothing about the physics changes; nothing about the data changes. How far does the combined MAP move in the realistic venue? Commit before you drag anything.

4. Three measured inconsistencies, all on one side

Three things about this estimator are now measured to be internally inconsistent — not “biased”, which requires knowing the truth, but inconsistent with the project’s own model, code or generator. That is a stronger and cheaper kind of finding: you can establish it without ever asking whether $h = 0.73$.

C7 — the host-redshift kernel integrates against the wrong prior

Status FINDING promoted to FINDING (measured), with corrected law; scope narrowed — live. Chapter 7 argued it in full ⏮ Ch 7; the board above carries the verbatim status. What this section adds is the part neither of them can settle.

The numerator’s host-redshift kernel weights candidate redshifts by the cosmic volume prior $w_{\rm pop} = (dV_c/dz)/(1+z)$ with no $p_{\rm det}$ and no catalogue selection — i.e. it deconvolves a wide photometric redshift against a monotonically rising prior that never turns over.

They are answers to different questions — and it is worth being exact about which, because the difference is not a scale artefact of $\sigma_z$. They are priors over two different random variables ⏮ Ch 7 Trap 7.B:

  • G2b’s $w_{\rm pop} = (dV_c/dz)/(1+z)$ is the prior over where an EMRI host is, given that an EMRI happened. On that question G2b’s proof is fine: unique, consistent with the rate model, exactly $h$-independent.
  • The object the numerator actually deconvolves is a row in a flux-limited catalogue. Its prior is the catalogue’s own selected number density, $\phi_{\rm cat}(z) \propto f(z,\Omega)\,(dV_c/dz)/(1+z)$, and for the numerator of a detected event it should further carry $p_{\rm det}$. That prior turns over. The bare one does not — and the difference between a prior that turns over and one that does not is the whole of C7’s $+3(\sigma_z/z)^2$.

Naming the axis is not adjudicating on it, and this book does not adjudicate: whether the numerator’s prior should be $\phi_{\rm cat}\,p_{\rm det}$ rather than $w_{\rm pop}$ is a derivation nobody has written, and writing it is the fix that must supersede G2b. G2b establishes which weight is uniquely consistent with the rate model and the selection integrals, and proves the limiting behaviour. C7 measures what that weight does at finite width when the numerator’s integration window is proportional to $z$. Both are correct about what they measured; the premise of one and the finite-$\sigma_z$ behaviour of the other are in tension. The project’s binding rule is therefore not “the kernel is wrong” but: any C7 fix must explicitly supersede G2b, and must not silently contradict it. This book does not pick a side, and it may not say “the volume kernel is calibrated, full stop” nor “the kernel is wrong, settled”.

Three further constraints on anyone reaching for the obvious fix. First, the measured historical failure mode of this deconvolution at large $\sigma_z/z$ was over-correction — the opposite sign to where a C7 fix pushes ledger #62 / #68. Second, C7 is channel-common: prior_num multiplies both numerators identically, so C7 is not a candidate for the 2D-versus-1D difference at all; and it acts against the dark class’s rail, because dark events sit at $\sigma_z/z \approx 0.10$ where $K = 1.03$. C8 — well-posedness defect; the stated cause was REFUTED and relocated FINDING CLAIM_2D_BIAS_20260730.md C8 gate_b_20260730/README_C8.md It is not “a 4D numerator against a 3D $D(h)$”: $D$, $\beta_G$, $\beta_{\bar G}$ and $\Sigma_{\rm glob}$ are all mass-dimensionless, so mass-marginalising $D$ could not restore invariance. 7. What would you do first?

You now know this estimator’s three live inconsistencies, its attribution — confounded until the night of 2026-07-31, when the control finally ran — and, behind all of it, a ledger of 98 hypotheses about this bias, each with what it cost. The temptation at this point is overwhelming and it is wrong: propose a fix.

Here is why it is wrong, in this specific inference. The combined likelihood surface is near-flat at its peak, and you measured its leverage with your own hands in §3. In the corrected units the project itself had to fix, one nat of window-integrated log-likelihood over a 0.08-wide window is worth $\Delta h = \Delta\text{nats}\cdot\sigma_h^2/\Delta h_{\rm window} \approx 4.9\times10^{-3}$ BOOK_SOURCES_MAP.md §7.20. So a ±10-nat intervention — and almost any plausible change to a kernel, a prior or a normalization is worth ±10 nats — moves the MAP by ~0.05. A fix that moves the answer toward truth is therefore almost no evidence that the fix is right. The ledger is 98 rows of what that produces: fixes right in isolation and wrong in the pipeline, right in sign and 4× wrong in magnitude, and one that merely relocated the tilt to a different branch (+94…+455 nats, 12/12 fail).

The discipline this book has been teaching is the answer, and it is unglamorous: run the control; isolate one variable; check whether it has been tried before. Section 5 is what the first two look like when they work — one job, one variable, a prediction written down first, and an attribution that had resisted a month of argument settled in a night. The third is not rhetorical — the project maintains a binding do-not-re-try union (the claim file’s Exonerated list plus the ledger’s §2 items 1–17), and the entries absent from the first list are precisely the live re-litigation risk.

The state of the art, in one paragraph A dark-siren $H_0$ estimator built from a ratified selection integral, a ratified two-branch mixture, a ratified volume kernel and a CANDIDATE 2D mass pairing, run on 3135 detections from two mock universes, delivers a 1D channel whose centredness is a contingent crossing and a 2D channel biased +4σ in 10/10 runs. Three internal inconsistencies are measured, all on the completion / prefactor / kernel side; none convicts the catalogue leg; the attribution between estimator and catalogue was confounded by three simultaneous changes — and the one experiment that separates them was pre-registered, submitted, and landed: the estimator configuration owns 72% of the 2D displacement, and the realism layer is largely exonerated. That is a result. What to do about it is still a position, and it is an honest one.

Q11.1   58% of in-catalogue hosts peak at the top of the prior. Give the strongest argument that this is an artifact of the prior’s upper bound, then say how the project killed it.

Show answer
The argument: any bounded grid piles up probability at its edge, so a concentration at 0.86 could simply be truncation of a broad, uninformative profile. Q11.2   The same claim carries a binding fair-framing amendment: per event the rail is “cosmetic” (0.30–0.47 σ_event) but the class-summed displacement is +3.4 to +6.1 σ_class. Explain how both can be true, and why quoting either alone is a misrepresentation.

Show answer
Both are true because they are different summary statistics of the same data: individually each event’s peak sits well inside its own (very wide) uncertainty, so no single event is anomalous; but the displacements are coherently same-signed, so they add linearly in the class sum while the class uncertainty grows only as $\sqrt N$. Quoting only the per-event number (“it’s within 0.5σ”) hides a >3σ systematic; quoting only “58% rail” invites the reader to picture 58% of events being individually broken, which they are not. The project made carrying both mandatory for any write-up, and the book obeys. CLAIM_2D_BIAS_20260730.md C5 fair-framing amendment

Q11.3   C7 says the host-z kernel omits selection. The ratified derivation G2b confirmed that exact weight, without $p_{\rm det}$, as uniquely consistent — and protects it with a regression gate. How can both be right?

Show answer
They are answers to different questions. G2b establishes that $(dV_c/dz)/(1+z)$ is the unique weight consistent with this project’s rate model and its selection integrals, and that it is exactly $h$-independent and reduces correctly as $\sigma_z \to 0$. C7 measures what that same weight does at finite $\sigma_z/z \gtrsim 0.256$ when the numerator’s window is $z$-proportional: it inflates. The honest statement is that G2b’s premise and the finite-$\sigma_z$ numerator kernel are in tension, so any C7 fix must explicitly supersede G2b rather than quietly contradict it — and must reckon with the fact that the measured historical failure mode of deconvolution at large $\sigma_z/z$ was over-correction, the opposite sign to where the proposed fix pushes.
