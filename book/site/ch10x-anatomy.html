Chapter 10½ — The Anatomy of the Bias

The −0.1083 was never one thing. It decomposes, with mechanism, into three contributions — and the decomposition was only trustworthy because every verdict along the way was adversarially amended.

Interlude, not a numbered chapter This page reports a two-day August 2026 campaign that runs entirely inside the catalogue/completion mixture Chapters 5 and 7 already built. It changes no tool and adds no new machinery — it takes the single number Chapter 8 left on the bias rail and asks where, exactly, it comes from.

Predict, then reveal — where would you look?

A −0.108 bias decomposes into three parts. Two of them you have already met: the catalogue leg’s wrong-host candidates ⏮ Ch 5 and the host-redshift kernel’s own inflation law ⏮ Ch 7. The third was hiding somewhere neither chapter looked. Before reading on: what kind of bug survives every validation harness this project has built?

“A numerator that lost a factor its own normalizer kept.” The completion leg’s numerator $B^{\rm num}$ did not carry the survival factor S̄φ that its own normalizer $\beta_{\bar G\varphi}$ carries — a mismatch inside one leg, invisible to every harness that generates and evaluates from the same model. Section 3 below is the whole story.

1. One equation, six rungs

Every per-event likelihood the estimator computes, no-BH-mass analysis, is one equation:

$$ \mathrm{combined}(e,h) \;=\; \frac{\beta_{G\varphi}(h)\,\mathcal L_{\rm cat}(e,h) \;+\; B^{\rm num}(e,h)}{\tilde D_\varphi(h)} $$

“The event’s host is either one of the catalogue’s candidates (first term) or a galaxy the catalogue doesn’t contain (second term), normalized by the total selected population.” Everything this chapter says is a name for a piece of that equation, a way of reading it out, a switchable variant of it, or a synthetic universe it was tested in docs/PRIMER_BIAS_CHANNELS_20260822.md. Six vocabulary rungs stack on top of it, and this book uses them exactly as the primer defines them:

  1. Legs — the two numerator terms: catalogue leg = $\beta_{G\varphi}\mathcal L_{\rm cat}$, completion leg = $B^{\rm num}$. S̄φ, the φ-averaged detection survival at $z$, is the recurring object: which terms carry it, and does each numerator’s S̄φ match its own normalizer’s?
  2. Channels — three ways to read the posterior out: full (production, as-is), matched (the dark-conditional $B^{\rm num}/\beta_{\bar G\varphi}$ — “if we knew the host is not catalogued, what does the completion leg alone say?”), pure (full minus the catalogue leg, exact subtraction).
  3. Contributions — the three-way split of the headline bias itself (§2 below). “Contribution” for this split; “channel” only for rung 2’s readouts — the two words collided in early reports and the primer retires the collision for good.
  4. Cells — switchable instrumentation flags that change one numerator convention, always byte-identical to production at their default: selection_in_completion_numerator (“off” = the legacy defect; “fused” = the fix) and catalogue_numerator_survival (“the twin,” §4).
  5. Arms and venues — which synthetic universe an experiment ran in. B-SEL: real candidates, but the true host is never one of them — powerful for mechanisms, unable to certify catalogue-leg correctness. C-SG-F: self-generated, so any deviation from zero is the estimator’s own inconsistency. b0: genuinely catalogued hosts — the only venue that can adjudicate (§5).
  6. Governance words — the A-numbers (A17…A22) are process amendments earned by specific failures; bands are pre-frozen verdict regions; gates VOID a run rather than shade it. This chapter is as much about rung 6 as about the physics — the campaign that found the physics is the demonstration of why rung 6 exists.

3. The cell nobody varied

Here is the mechanism, in one sentence: the completion leg’s numerator $B^{\rm num}$ does not carry the survival factor S̄φ that its own normalizer $\beta_{\bar G\varphi}$ carries. Every run-of-record before this campaign — campaign #51, campaign #53, every earlier C-SG pass — stood on this off cell without anyone noticing, because it is invisible to a self-consistent harness: if the harness generates events from the same model it analyses, the mismatched factor cancels between generation and analysis and the effect is identically zero. It took a harness built specifically to break that symmetry — C-SG’s own registered falsifier — to expose it ledger #155.

4. Leverage is not correctness

The catalogue leg has the same structural shape as the completion leg: its numerator $\mathcal L_{\rm cat}$ is evaluated without S̄φ, while $\beta_{G\varphi}$ — its own normalizer — integrates it. Inserting it (the catalogue-leg analog of the fused fix, called the twin) moves the venue headline by a small, real, positive amount on B-SEL: +0.0155 ± 0.0037 (12/12 positive, 4.2σ) ledger #162. That is where the story could have stopped as a footnote. It did not, because the very next question — is that the right fix, or just an effective one? — produced a genuine refutation.

A proposed “completed pairing” (dividing the twin’s level back out by a rescale $R = \beta_G/\beta_{G\varphi}$) was checked against its own derivation and failed: $\beta_{G\varphi}$ over the global selection sum is a measure conversion whose S̄ cancels algebraically, and re-installing $R$ reinstates exactly the un-derived-multiplier shape of the retired B_scale defect ledger #168. The twin, taken as measured and nothing more, is the derivation-coherent candidate — but only on the fused basis, because it shares the completion leg’s own S̄φ pairing question. The author ratified that reading (“ratify B, run fused re-measure + b0 test”) ledger #169, and on that basis the twin grew:

TWIN-FUSED-MATERIAL, as banked (amendment-20 quotation) On its coherent (fused) basis, the twin moves the venue headline +0.0291 ± 0.0051 (12/12, 5.7σ) — on the registered, censored grid. The un-truncated read, on the full h-grid with no boundary censoring, is +0.0634 ± 0.0089 — 2.18× the registered figure, because censoring compresses the effect, which makes the registered number the conservative one to quote. ledger #173

Read that pair of numbers as a warning label, not a result: a real, reproducible, derivation-coherent effect of this size is exactly the kind of thing a leverage artifact also looks like. B-SEL, by construction, can never certify the twin’s correctness — every candidate in it is an impostor, so any change that suppresses the catalogue leg’s pull on an all-impostor venue “helps” mechanically, whether or not it is physically right docs/PRIMER_BIAS_CHANNELS_20260822.md §5. The thread had measured a real, sizeable, and derivation-clean effect — and had run entirely out of venues that could tell it apart from a mechanically favorable coincidence. That is exactly the question §5 was registered to answer.

5. The adjudicator

The b0 venue is the one place in this project where the true host really is a catalogue row — an aligned generator, not an all-impostor mirror. That licenses an exact identity no other venue can state: for events genuinely drawn from the catalogued class, the class-conditional expectation of the responsibility odds must equal 1,

$$ \mathbb E_{d\sim p(d\mid G)}\!\left[\frac{1-w(d)}{w(d)}\right]\cdot\frac{P_G}{P_{\bar G}} \;=\; 1 ,\qquad w(d)=\frac{P_G\,p(d\mid G)}{p(d)} , $$

an odds-form identity that holds for a correctly arranged mixture and is violated exactly when a leg’s numerator/normalizer pairing is broken PREREGISTRATION_B0_IDENTITY_20260823.md §1. Three arms ran it on the fused basis: the coded (off-cell-corrected) configuration, the twin, and — as the registered control — the refuted Appendix-A rescale, run expecting it to fail the identity. A control engineered to fail is the sharpest instrument this project has: if the wrong arrangement passes anyway, the test has nothing to say.

That is what happened. Through two full rounds of clean-context adversarial review — eight A20-class reviews across the two days that produced this chapter, zero FATAL findings, and every single verdict materially amended along the way ledger #173 — the test executed exactly as registered, all gates passed, and it came back UNDISCRIMINATING. The registration’s own control clause caught the reason: one ordinary event — not anomalous, just carrying an extremely small responsibility weight — is enough on its own to inflate the identity statistic’s standard error so far that the refuted arrangement passes the identical bands the correct one does. The bands could not tell the two apart, so the test could not adjudicate either ledger #177.

No — and expecting it to be one is the trap. A near-match to four figures would be more suspicious than reassuring — it would suggest the three measurements were not actually independent. ledger #150

Q10½.2   Why was the off-cell S̄φ omission invisible to every earlier validation harness, including Chapter 10’s own P–P coverage instrument?

Show answer
Because a self-consistent harness generates the data it then analyses from the same model, so a numerator/normalizer mismatch inside one leg cancels between generation and analysis and produces an effect of exactly zero — the same blindness class Chapter 9’s idealized closure has to a population mismatch, and the same reason Chapter 4’s starvation exhibit needed an injection scan rather than a coverage run. It took C-SG’s registered falsifier, engineered specifically to compare a leg against its own conditional rather than against the generator that shares its assumptions, to expose it. ledger #155
