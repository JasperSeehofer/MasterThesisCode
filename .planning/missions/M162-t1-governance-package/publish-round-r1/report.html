Q1 [RULE]: the tie rule and the σ treatment (R27)

Tie rule (E-2). The cell is decided in a fixed order, and the first step that fires decides:

  1. A red gate ⇒ NO-READ.
  2. The rail clause wins: an edge-node mass above 1e-3, or a MAP on an edge node, ⇒ AT-RAIL-LOW or AT-RAIL-HIGH, banked as a one-sided bound.
  3. Exactly one band cell (TUNED, TRANSFER or UNBIASED) holds, and the 1D replicate agrees ⇒ that cell.
  4. Two or more band cells hold, or none, or 1D disagrees ⇒ INTERMEDIATE. This is the same "none or several" wording you applied to m1 (R25).

What "rail wins" costs, stated plainly. A posterior that sits at the TUNED value 0.666 but has a fat lower tail (m1 had 4.4 % of its mass on the 0.600 node) reads AT-RAIL-LOW, not TUNED. The card's consequence row still reports its distance to TUNED, and the consequence comes back to you as a fresh ruling.

σ treatment (E-3). The bands use the measured σ_h, as registered. The check:

  • Compute ρ = σ_h / σ_exp, where σ_exp is the anchor σ scaled by sqrt(1588/N).
  • If ρ is outside [0.8, 1.25] in either channel, the cell is assigned again at σ_exp.
  • Same cell both times ⇒ it banks.
  • A different cell ⇒ INTERMEDIATE (σ-sensitive), and both results are reported.
  • On m1, ρ = 0.70, so the check would have fired. The band cell would not have changed.

Overlap disclosure. At σ ≳ 0.010–0.015 the TUNED and TRANSFER windows overlap for the lower part of the prior, so INTERMEDIATE (several) is a live outcome.

Cell definitions, from draft §6 with the anchors made channel-specific:

  • TUNED_c: |mean_h,c − TUNED-mean_c| ≤ 3σ_band,c AND |MAP_c − 0.665| ≤ 3σ_band,c. The draft's precondition |h_inj − 0.73| ≥ 0.06 holds for every draw from P-A.
  • TRANSFER_c: |(mean_h,c − h_inj) − δ_c| ≤ 3σ_band,c AND |(MAP_c − h_inj) − δ_c| ≤ 3σ_band,c.
  • UNBIASED_c: |mean_h,c − h_inj| ≤ 2σ_band,c.
  • Rail_c (the rail clause): MAP_c on an edge node (0.600 or 1.000), OR m_lo,c > 1e-3, OR m_hi,c > 1e-3. Side: LOW if the 0.600 condition holds, HIGH if the 1.000 condition holds, BOTH if both hold.

Assignment, applied in this order. The first step that assigns a cell ends the procedure.

  1. Gates. Any red gate in E-6 ⇒ NO-READ. Nothing is banked and the seal is spent (revision cap 1).
  2. Rail clause wins (R27). If Rail_2D fires, the read is AT-RAIL-<side of 2D>, banked and read as a one-sided bound, not NO-READ. If side = BOTH, the read is INTERMEDIATE instead. TUNED, TRANSFER and UNBIASED are not assigned, whatever their bands say. The band distances are still computed and reported, labelled "reported, not cell-bearing". The replicate must agree: if Rail_1D does not fire, or fires on the other side, the read is INTERMEDIATE (channel disagreement). If Rail_1D fires and Rail_2D does not, the read is also INTERMEDIATE (channel disagreement).
  3. Band cells. Evaluate TUNED_c, TRANSFER_c and UNBIASED_c on both channels.
    • Exactly one band cell holds in 2D, and the same single cell holds in 1D ⇒ that cell.
    • Two or more band cells hold in either channel ⇒ INTERMEDIATE (several). This is the parent wording "none or several of the above", the same text R25 applied to m1.
    • No band cell holds in 2D ⇒ INTERMEDIATE (none).
    • 2D and 1D hold different single cells ⇒ INTERMEDIATE (channel disagreement).
  4. σ robustness (E-3). E-3 may turn a cell from steps 2–3 into INTERMEDIATE (σ-sensitive). It never turns one band cell into another.

  • AT-RAIL-LOW banks the statement "mean_h,2D ≤ X at the 0.600 floor", with X the measured mean. The posterior is then censored by the grid floor and lies below both the TRANSFER window and the UNBIASED window for every draw from P-A: under P-A the TRANSFER mean is 0.726–0.756 and the UNBIASED mean is 0.79–0.82.
  • AT-RAIL-HIGH banks "mean_h,2D ≥ X at the 1.000 ceiling".

Why the rail is banked as a bound and is not NO-READ. This settles open defect D-2 (CONVENTIONS.md). The draft makes the rail NO-READ (§8 g-censoring); the operative m1 dossier made it a banked BOUND (§2). R27's "rail clause wins" is a statement about which cell is assigned, so a winning rail clause has to book a cell. That cell is the dossier's BOUND reading, which is the m1 precedent the ruling was made on. NO-READ stays reserved for gate failures (pins, freeze, commitment, blindness, population, resolved flags, the P2 invariance gate). This reading is put to the author as its own line on the ratification card (Q1b).

Disclosure on the rail threshold (CONVENTIONS C-22). The 1e-3 edge-node threshold is kept exactly as registered. It is tight: on m1 it fired at a declining tail with an interior MAP. The 55-node grid moves the upper edge to 1.000, which is ≥ 9σ above any P-A truth, so HIGH is practically reachable only through a defect. The 0.600 floor sits 0.066 below the TUNED centre. Under TUNED with σ ≈ 0.014–0.020 that is 3.3–4.7σ, so a TUNED-shaped posterior can still trip the LOW rail through its tail, as m1 did. That outcome is booked AT-RAIL-LOW by this rule, not TUNED. This is the main cost of "rail wins", and the author should see it before ratifying: a TUNED-like posterior with a fat lower tail reads AT-RAIL-LOW, a bound that excludes TRANSFER and UNBIASED. It cannot read TUNED. The consequence row for AT-RAIL-LOW (E-8) therefore carries the TUNED-band distance as a reported number, and returns to the author as a fresh RULE before any paper consequence attaches.

Where two band cells can both hold under P-A (ORCHESTRATOR-DERIVED; disclosed so that INTERMEDIATE (several) is not a surprise):

  • TUNED and TRANSFER windows are separated by h_inj − 0.73 = 0.06–0.09. Each window has half-width 3σ, so they overlap when σ > (h_inj − 0.73)/6, i.e. σ > 0.010 (h_inj = 0.79) up to σ > 0.015 (h_inj = 0.82). At the m1 width σ ≈ 0.014, the band cells cannot tell TUNED from TRANSFER for the lower part of the prior.
  • TRANSFER and UNBIASED windows are separated by |δ| ≈ 0.064 and their half-widths sum to 5σ, so they overlap when σ > 0.0128.
  • TUNED and UNBIASED are separated by ≥ 0.124 and never overlap at σ ≤ 0.024.
  • Reading of the overlaps: if the posterior falls in an overlap, the registration answers INTERMEDIATE (several) rather than choosing. This is the R25 precedent applied forward.

E-3. R27(ii): treatment of σ_measured < σ_anchor (and the symmetric case)

m1 came in with σ_h = 0.0140 (2D) against the anchor's 0.0185 at larger N, 24 % narrower. The registration expected about 9 % wider (CONVENTIONS D-3). A width discrepancy moves every 3σ band, so it changes which cell fires. E-3 keeps the registered statistic as the primary rule and adds a mechanical robustness check.

rule block E3-sigma-treatment begins

Definitions, per channel c:

  • N_used = the number of distinct event_idx in the scored likelihood table.
  • σ_anchor,2D = 0.018475 and σ_anchor,1D = 0.017526, both at N_anchor = 1588 (row #302).
  • σ_exp,c = σ_anchor,c · sqrt(N_anchor / N_used). This assumes Fisher information adds over events, so σ ∝ N^(−1/2). It is the same scaling the draft's §5 SNR-loss estimate used.
  • ρ_c = σ_h,c / σ_exp,c.

Rule:

  1. Primary. σ_band,c = σ_h,c, the measured width, exactly as registered. Assign the cell by E-2.
  2. Width check. If 0.8 ≤ ρ_c ≤ 1.25 in both channels, the primary cell banks as is.
  3. Width-discrepant (ρ_c < 0.8 or ρ_c > 1.25 in either channel). Re-run E-2 steps 2–3 with σ_band,c = σ_exp,c in both channels. Then:
    • Same cell as the primary ⇒ the primary cell banks, annotated "σ-discrepant, cell-robust (ρ_2D = …, ρ_1D = …)".
    • Different cell ⇒ the read is INTERMEDIATE (σ-sensitive). Both assignments and both ρ values are reported, and the read returns to the author as a fresh RULE.
    • The rail clause does not depend on σ, so an AT-RAIL read is never changed by this step.
  4. Reported, not cell-bearing. Both ρ values; all band distances under σ_h and under σ_exp; the T-4 per-event pull calibration (KS, mean, std of the pulls; draft §6 secondary) on events with curvature > 0. A pull std well below 1 is the expected companion of ρ < 0.8 and is reported as such.
rule block E3-sigma-treatment ends

Where 0.8 and 1.25 come from (ORCHESTRATOR-DERIVED; flagged for ratification):

  • The band is symmetric in log width. 1/1.25 = 0.8.
  • It is wider than the change between N = 1588 and the draft's expected P-A N = 1230–1340: that scaling is a factor 1.09–1.14 and is already absorbed into σ_exp.
  • It is narrower than m1's observed ρ = 0.0140/(0.018475·sqrt(1588/1343)) = 0.0140/0.02009 = 0.70. So the m1 case would have triggered the check.
  • Applied to m1 for illustration only (m1 stays banked INTERMEDIATE by R25): at σ_exp the 2D TUNED distance is 0.0382/0.0201 = 1.90σ, so the band cell is unchanged. The rail would still fire. Under this erratum, m1 would have read AT-RAIL-LOW with no σ-sensitivity.
  • The check is symmetric because R27 named the narrow case, but the same logic applies to an unexplained wide posterior. A one-sided check would itself be a forking path.

(m2) p_det pool option: P2 first, P1 fallback

Date: 2026-10-19. Mission M162. Author of record: Jasper Seehofer. Status: DRAFT FOR RATIFICATION, together with ERRATUM_m2_R27_R28.md. The binding text is the erratum's rule block E-5. This memo gives the reasoning, the costs and the cluster reads E-5 depends on. Nothing was run.

1. What the pool is for and why reuse is defensible

SimulationDetectionProbability builds p_det from injection horizons d_hor,k = SNR_k · d_L,k / SNR_thr. Its docstring claims this is h-invariant by construction: the survival grid is built once and reused for every trial h (darksiren_emri/bayesian_inference/simulation_detection_probability.py:12-24). The reasoning is that at fixed z, d_L ∝ 1/h and SNR ∝ 1/d_L, so SNR · d_L does not depend on h.

  • If the claim is exact, a pool generated at h_inj adds samples and no information. Reusing the 0.73 pool is then legitimate.
  • If the claim is not exact, a pool at another h is the only way to see the difference.

T-1's literal text asks for "a freshly generated injection pool at that cosmology" (PHYSICS_METHODOLOGY_REVIEW.md:401-415). P2 does not meet that literally; it tests the property that would make it unnecessary. The ruling accepts this, and the gap is disclosed in the prereg.

Pool of record (erratum E-1, R28):

  • Content-manifest list-md5 75f4030d5d3b0405fd948049bef5767e.

2. P2: design, and the defect in the draft gate it fixes

**Draft text (REGISTRATION_DRAFT.md §3):** "a SMALL unsealed pool at a public h (e.g. 0.67, 20 tasks ≈ 10 GPU-h) compared to the rescaled 0.73 pool's survival grid (max abs Δp_det on the grid ≤ 1e-3 registered)".

Defect (ORCHESTRATOR-DERIVED). As written, the comparison is between two independent sets of draws:

  • 20 tasks give about 20 × 283 ≈ 5,700 rows (200,100 rows / 707 tasks ≈ 283 per task).
  • The production grid is 60 × 40 = 2,400 cells, so ≈ 2.4 rows per cell on average.
  • The binomial standard error of a survival fraction at that occupancy is O(0.1–0.3). The local_linear smoother and the z-resolution reduce it, but not by two orders of magnitude.

So a 1e-3 gate between unpaired pools would fail on Monte Carlo noise alone. It would make P1 mandatory whatever the physics, which is not what the ruling intends.

Fix, registered in E-5: a paired comparison.

  • The small pool reuses the seeds of tasks 0–19 of the pool of record, BASE_SEED + task, with the same population and mixture configuration and h_P2 = 0.67.
  • The injection loop draws z (and the host, mass and extrinsic parameters) from seeded generators and only then computes luminosity_distance = dist(event_z, h=h_value) (main.py:1220). The mixture uses spawned generators that are independent of h (main.py:1022-1042). Paired rows should therefore be the same draws with a different d_L.
  • Pairing check P2-0 verifies this: ≥ 95 % of small-pool rows must find an exact-parameter match. Timeouts drop rows on either side, so row-for-row equality is not required.
  • The gate compares the survival grid from the matched small-pool rows against the grid from the same matched rows of the 0.73 pool. MC noise cancels, and what is left is the true h-dependence plus numerical noise. Only then is 1e-3 a meaningful threshold.

Unresolved by reading, found at run time. Whether the z or host draw depends on h somewhere (a z_max from an h-dependent d_L cap, for example) cannot be settled without running. P2-0 is the run-time check. If it fails, the ruling's fallback (P1) applies; there is no silent switch to an unpaired comparison with a looser threshold.

Cost: 20 tasks × ≤ 0.5 GPU-h wall cap (cluster/inject.sbatch:19) ≤ 10 GPU-h. The gate itself is a CPU script, minutes.

Ordering: P2 is unsealed (h = 0.67 is public), so it runs and is gated before the draw. Its number goes into the launch row. A P2 FAIL after a draw would leave a spent seal with no legitimate pool; doing it first avoids that.

3. P1: the fallback, and why it needs no code change

  • Fresh pool at h_inj, cap 250 GPU-h (ruling). The draft sized P1 as 500 tasks × 0.5 h. A like-for-like replacement of the 707-task pool of record would cost ≤ 353.5 GPU-h. Within the 250 GPU-h cap, a P1 pool has ≤ 500 tasks, so fewer rows than the pool of record: about 71 %, disclosed. If the author wants 707-task parity, that is a cap change and a fresh [DO].
  • No naming shim (corrects draft §3). The draft said P1 needed "a naming shim (new code, no physics)" because file names carry h (injection_h_(\d+p\d+), simulation_detection_probability.py:316) and the h_inj column is loaded (:350, used at :416 and :651-656). A shim would have to sit inside darksiren_emri/, which R24 forbids. A rename outside the package would be worse: the estimator reads h_inj per row to recompute d_L, so a mislabelled pool would corrupt p_det.
    • Instead, P1's pool is generated inside the sealed RUN_DIR, with honest file names, and is covered by the procedural blindness rule (erratum E-6). The run directory already leaks h in four places (draft §4 item 3), so one more inside it adds nothing.
    • What P1 must never do is sit in a shared $WS/injection_pool_* directory, where an agent listing pools would see h.
  • P1 seeds: a fresh BASE_SEED recorded in the launch row, not reused from any 0.73 task, so the two pools are independent.
